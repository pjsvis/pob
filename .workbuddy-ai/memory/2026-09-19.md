## Second pass (same day) — the phase-change and remodelling claims

Appended **Part 6** to the same digest (no second file — same investigation). Claims assessed: the floor incentivises positional exploration; pressure and warmth phase-change the thixotropic elements to liquid; this provides a remodelling opportunity.

**Findings added:**

- **The remodelling claim's hard constraint is quantitative.** Collagen half-life: **117 y** in cartilage (Verzijl et al., *JBC* 2000); **197.5 ± 18.2 y** in equine tendon, 95–215 y in disc (Sivan et al.). Hyaluronan half-life in tissue: **<1 day to several days** (Laurent & Fraser 1992). Roughly six orders of magnitude apart. So a floor session offers a **matrix** remodelling opportunity, not a collagen one. This should bound every future statement of the claim.
- **Three corrections to the phase-change mechanism.** (i) Thixotropy is not a phase change; native HA has no sol/gel transition at body temperature — the sol/gel HA literature requires chemically grafted NIPAM. (ii) The trigger is **shear, not pressure**; normal pressure does not shear, so lying still cannot do it. (iii) The warmth runs the **wrong way** — felt temperature is thermal effusivity, so a hard floor *conducts heat away* and favours the mattress, not the floor.
- **Reformulation worth keeping:** the floor does not shear anything — it supplies the **anchorage** that makes shear possible (a reaction surface). This unifies the shear argument with the calibration argument under one mechanism.
- **Internal contradiction in the original claim:** if the matrix genuinely liquefied it could not transmit load, and load is the remodelling signal. Gliding is the goal, not liquidity.
- **The strongest surviving version:** remodelling follows habitual load, not transient load, so the session does not remodel directly — it **changes the configuration from which the following day's loading proceeds**. This matches the diurnal-budget frame in `maintenance-burden-equation.md` and supplies a reason for daily rather than occasional practice.
- **Practice-vocabulary problem flagged:** chapter 15 calls it "the Rug Sandbox". A rug is compliant (smears the pressure readout — the mechanism that works) and insulating (retains warmth — the mechanism that does not). The rug is optimised for the wrong half. Unresolved decision, recorded in the digest at §6d.
- **The incentive claim** is plausible and structurally motivated (a plane constrains one side, so the unconstrained surface is always the salient next DOF — the brief's own queue logic extended from segments to positions). Competing accounts named: discomfort and novelty. Separable by self-report, making it the cheapest claim to test.

The observed protocol is exactly that ordering, arrived at behaviourally. Plus the stool supplies the readout step the derivation didn't specify. Protocol reads: load the datum → unload → shear by moving → sample from a third rig. Recorded as a coincidence of the right kind, not as proof.

**Substantive additions:**

- **The stool is a third datum and the right kind** — rigid (preserves the pressure readout), unloads the feet without loading the pelvis, and it is *not the floor*, which is what makes the check a real measurement rather than a re-read of the same rig.
- **Open-ended duration is defensible for a specific reason:** the ache → shift loop is the pressure-relief reflex, so the loop that makes the protocol work is the same thing that makes the duration safe. - **Nutrition: sound biochemistry, weaker appetite science.** Vitamin C (ascorbate cofactor for prolyl/lysyl hydroxylase), protein/glycine (protein leverage hypothesis), carbs (synthesis energy cost, already in `maintenance-burden-equation.md:72`), salt (HA is a polyanion; hydration is a Donnan/osmotic effect mediated by Na⁺; sodium appetite is one of the few well-established human nutrient-specific appetites). **Copper is missing from the list and belongs on it — lysyl oxidase is copper-dependent.** But the inferential step is weaker than the biochemical one: specific hungers for vitamins/minerals are mostly *not* established in humans; cravings do not generally signal deficiency. Salt strongest, protein moderate, the rest unestablished.

## Fifth pass (same day) — the session sequence (ache → crack → new range → careful motion)

Appended as **§9–§11 of `assets/2026-09-19-the-unattended-protocol.md`** — same protocol, so same file, not a fifth asset.

**The load-bearing finding: the ache reduction and the range gain are on different clocks, and the model has been conflating them.**

- **Ache reduction is neural and fast** — gate control (Melzack & Wall), exercise-induced hypoalgesia, and extinction of the threat response. Seconds to minutes.
- **Range gain is mechanical and slower** — thixotropic breakdown of the SREC, viscoelastic creep, reduced reflex guarding. Hours to days.
- So "the map re-zeroing" is at least **two layers**: a neural layer changing in seconds and a mechanical layer changing over hours-to-days. Same two-timescale structure as the remodelling argument, one level up. This explains why the ache can vanish while the range has not yet changed, and the reverse.

**Other additions:**

- **The crack is a marker, not a mechanism.** Kawchuk et al. 2015 (PLOS ONE) imaged joint cavitation in real time: the crack is cavity *formation* (tribonucleation), not bubble collapse; the ~20-min refractory period follows from gas re-dissolution. And the manipulation literature establishes that an audible pop is **not required** for the effect. So the crack and the range change are both consequences of one mechanical event, not cause and effect. **Actionable rule: do not use the sound as the evidence — measure the range.**
- **Three distinct sounds separated**, because the distinction is free to record and diagnostic later: cavitation (single crack, refractory period, intra-articular) / snapping (tendon or fascia over a bony prominence, *repeats with the same movement* — snapping hip, scapula, triceps; the "elastic band" quality points here) / crepitus (grinding; can indicate chondral change).
- **Refinement to the model's map account:** "sensitive" is the signature of newly-mapped territory, and it is the **opposite sign** of the arm routine's "subdued" hand. Subdued = the region *lost* its data (pendular, unanchored). Sensitive = the region just *gained* data, and novel afferent input is flagged as news. Two signs of one condition — uncalibrated — differing by the direction of the signal change. Belongs in the felt vocabulary.
- **"Careful motion" is not timidity — it is the writing phase**, the same role pandiculation plays in the brief's anchoring routine (step 4). The carefulness *is* the calibration procedure.
- **The serious caution, recorded prominently:** the ache → crack → new-range → "release" sequence is exactly the phenomenology the manual-therapy literature over-reads, and chasing it is a known harm pattern. If the range gain comes with reduced protective tone, pursuing more range is the wrong direction; acquired hypermobility/instability from repeated manipulation aimed at "releasing" restrictions is a real clinical pattern. **Rule: the range that has opened is to be *mapped*, not extended.** Chasing the crack converts a calibration procedure into a stretch habit, and what gets calibrated away is protective tone.

## Correction to the fifth pass (same day) — the sound is an interface phenomenon, not cavitation

The operator corrected the record: **not cavitation** — "tension release in a snapping motion with boundary effect noise." Appended as **§9f** of `the-unattended-protocol.md`, with a supersede note at §9b.

**The correction lands on better physics than either cavitation or stick-slip, and it is recent and directly on point.** Djellouli et al., "Squeaking at soft–rigid frictional interfaces," *Nature* (2026), Harvard SEAS / Nottingham / CNRS / Hebrew University. High-speed imaging (10⁶ fps) shows the sound comes from **opening slip pulses** — wrinkle-like detachment fronts propagating along the contact interface at near-supersonic speeds, detaching and reattaching it. Pitch is set by the pulses' repetition rate. The authors state explicitly that stick-slip "does not fully capture the physics of soft-on-rigid interfaces."

**Three findings recorded:**

1. **The sound is generated at the interface** — a boundary phenomenon literally. "Boundary effect noise" is the correct description, not a metaphor.
2. **Geometry sets the character of the sound.** Flat/irregular interfaces → irregular pulses → **broadband** noise. Confined ridged geometries → periodic pulses → **focused tonal pitch**.
3. **The pulses significantly affect frictional resistance** — the sound is coupled to the mechanics, not incidental.

**Two consequences for the model:**

- **§9b's epistemic conclusion is revised.** For cavitation, "marker, not mechanism" holds — the pop carries no information about the therapeutic mechanism. For the interfacial sound it does **not** hold: the sound is a **readout of the friction regime**, because the pulses are coupled to frictional resistance. Different epistemic status; the two sounds must not be lumped.
- **A new diagnostic, and it resolves a limit stated in §9b.** That section said tendon snapping and interfacial slip could not be told apart from the inside. The geometry result gives a physical basis for telling them apart from the sound: **tonal → a structured, confined pathway** (tendon in a pulley or retinaculum); **broadband → a flat or irregular interface** (fascial sheet over a plane of bone). Makes the sound worth recording in more detail: tone vs broadband, single vs repeating, stable pitch vs wandering.

**On "tension release":** what the slip-pulse frame has releasing is **adhesion at the interface** — the contact opening — not stored elastic energy in a band. The interface reading is the one the physics supports; the "band discharging elastic energy" reading would be a material failure and is unsupported. Marked: the *Nature* result is rubber on glass, so it establishes the mechanism class, not that a given bodily snap is this mechanism. The body does contain the same class of system — tendon through a retinaculum or pulley, fascia over bone — with measured friction coefficients and gliding resistance, and trigger finger is described as a disruption of the gliding system.

**Coherence worth keeping:** the thread's thesis is gliding-is-the-goal-liquidity-is-not. The slip-pulse result says a stuck soft–rigid interface releases in **discrete events**, not smoothly. So the snapping is the acoustic signature of a densified/high-friction interface returning to a gliding state — exactly the transition the model wants to produce.


## Sixth pass (same day) — cascades, and the crackling-noise frame

The operator's further observation: the cracking sounds come in **cascades**, ending either abruptly ("that was the last one") or by decaying in amplitude until gone. Appended as **§9g** of `the-unattended-protocol.md`.

**The frame is crackling noise** — Sethna, Dahmen & Myers, *Nature* 410:242 (2001). "Crackling noise arises when a system responds to changing external conditions through discrete, impulsive events." Requires: many metastable states, a threshold, **slow drive**, and disorder → avalanches of many sizes, power-law distributed. Friction interfaces are in the class (slip avalanches and their acoustic emission; extreme-value statistics of stick-slip).

**The load-bearing inference: a cascade is evidence of coupling.** Independent sites give isolated events; coupled sites give cascades, because one slip redistributes elastic stress and can push a neighbour past its own threshold. This is a fragment of support for the "long fascial chain" idea (digest §6a) — the part previously flagged as map, not measured structure. It does *not* show the named chains exist; it says the tissue is not behaving as a set of independent points.

**Both termination modes mean the same thing** — local stress below threshold everywhere. Abrupt = one large final event consumed the unstable sites. Decaying = progressive exhaustion approaching the relaxed state. So the **end of a cascade is a state marker**, which gives the practice an *observable* termination criterion rather than a felt one. By the §6 closed-loop / open-story split, that makes it a safe directive: *follow the cascade to its end.*

**Four cautions, first one primary:**
1. **Crackling noise is a universality class** — magnets, earthquakes, torn paper, martensites. Finding cascades establishes "slowly-driven threshold system with disorder," true of nearly everything. Consistent with the model, **not diagnostic** of it.
2. **The felt "last one" is not reliable** — expectation and auditory adaptation. Free test: **move again.** Silent = genuinely relaxed; more come = the "last one" was premature.
3. **Decay and growth mean opposite things** — decaying cascades converge; growing cascades mean the interface is getting *more* stuck.
4. **A large cascade is a large mechanical event** — "a snap is something small letting go" is wrong on the size axis.

**The experiment:** avalanche dynamics predicts a **heavy-tailed** size distribution (many small, few large), not a clustered one. Testable by ear: tally each event barely audible / clear / loud, within and across sessions. A heavy tail would be a real if modest result, and a self-report practice is unusually well placed to make it.

**Methodological sharpness:** decay alone does **not** discriminate. Avalanche dynamics → heavy-tailed distribution with a decay envelope; progressive lubrication → monotonic decay without a scale-free distribution. Both fade. **Only the distribution shape separates them** — so the tally is worth keeping and the decay alone is not informative.

Marked: prediction, not finding. No crack-size distribution for a floor practice has been published.

## Seventh pass (same day) — the guard-release reading

The operator's further reading: the cracking feels like **muscle guards being fully released so that a recovered range of motion is available again.** Appended as **§9h** of `the-unattended-protocol.md`.

**Split verdict: second half supported, first half breaks on physics.**

- **"Guard released → range available" is standard.** Guarding is a direct ROM limiter; reduce the nociceptive drive and range returns on the fast neural clock (§9a). Not controversial.
- **But a guard is a policy, not a material.** A maintained pattern of motor-neuron output is an electrochemical set point; it does not emit a broadband impulsive crack. Materials emit acoustic emissions when they slip or fail. Policies do not.
- **What muscle actually sounds like is the opposite of a crack.** Acoustic myography records a **low-frequency rumble** in the sound/infrasound band from the lateral dimension change of fibres during contraction (Oster & Jaffe, *Biophys J* 30(1):119, 1980) — continuous, low-frequency, present *during* contraction. Muscle working is a hum; this practice's sound is a snap. Different signal classes.

**The steelman worth keeping:** the guard changes the **load** on the interface. Held contraction alters stress across the soft–rigid contact; release changes the boundary condition; the interface then slips past threshold. Guard release as *trigger*, crack as the interface's *response* — preserves the intuition, moves the arrow. Consistent with §9f and §9g.

**But the cascade structure argues against the direct reading.** Motor-unit de-recruitment would track motor events one-to-one with motor-unit-sized events. Avalanche dynamics is a material process, not a recruitment process. So §9g is a datum that *separates* the two readings, not just a decoration on them.

**Better framing: co-effects of a common cause.** The nociceptive drive holding the guard is generated by the tissue state; change the tissue state and both fall together. Felt simultaneity is real; the causal chain is not. **Same error as §9b (cavitation) but in the opposite direction** — §9b mis-assigned the sound, §9h mis-assigns the cause of the sound.

**Supporting pattern from manipulation literature:** joint space increases **whether or not** an audible pop occurs; no consistent correlation between sound and outcomes (pain, mobility, disability). Stated explicitly: clinicians "should refrain from using the presence of an audible sound as an indicator of successful joint manipulation" — the pop is "a biomechanical byproduct rather than a marker of clinical efficacy" (Sillevis et al., reported 2025). Synovial joints, not fascial interfaces — establishes the pattern, not the specific case.

**Terminological caution:** "muscle guard" is loosely operationalised. Quintner, Bove & Cohen (*Rheumatology* 54(3):392, 2015) — the palpable taut band has not been shown to be a contractile entity. So "the guard released" is a *story* (§6), "the range holds when I move again" is a *loop*.

**The discriminator — opposite predictions from the same observation:**
- **Guard-limited:** new range is **reversible within minutes** by re-imposing guarding (voluntary co-contraction, nociceptive re-drive).
- **Interface-limited:** range is **persistent across the session**, unaffected by voluntary guarding, tracking cascade events rather than relaxation.
- **Directly instrumentable: surface EMG.** Guard release → EMG drop coincident with the cascade. Interface slip → EMG unchanged. The multimodal hypertonia rig (synchronised sEMG + force + kinematics, *PLOS ONE* 2026) is the instrument class and is portable.

**Also flagged:** "recovered" presupposes the range was there before and was guarded *from*. If never available in the adult lifetime, no guard was holding it, and §9c's newly-mapped reading applies instead. The practitioner is the only one who can say which history he is in.

**Added to §11:** a named pattern — the sequence has **three clocks** (ache/neural, range/mechanical, crack/interface) and the readings keep collapsing them into one chain. Three candidate mechanisms for the crack have now been tried and set aside (cavitation §9b, stick-slip §9f, guard release §9h), each failing the same way: **a real temporal correlation read as a causal arrow.** The sequence is a sequence, not a chain.
