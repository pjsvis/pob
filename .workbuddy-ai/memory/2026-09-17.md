## Session: "The Second Sleep: A Hypothesis" — assessment (opinion, no repo artefact written)

User proposed: the historical second sleep (mid-night waking, then return to sleep) is a
biological event. First sleep = lying flat, ground as reference plane, subcortical movement
systems untangle the day's accumulated excessive strains. On completion the subcortical system
wakes the sleeper for vertical orientation calibration, then sleep resumes for dreams.

Assessed against the existing repo material: this is an extension of `assets/traditional-practices-sleeping-postures.md`
§7 "the vertical-datum cycle" (recorded there as first-person data), plus the Buddhist
"middle watch of night" cell in that asset's §8 synthesis table — which currently has no account
of *why* the middle watch.

### Verified literature (read, not summarised from a model — per the repo's citation rule)

FOR the phenomenological reading:
- **Wehr, T. A. (1992), "In short photoperiods, human sleep is biphasic," *J. Sleep Res.* 1(2):103–107.**
  10-h photoperiod (14 h dark). Sleep expanded and usually divided into two symmetrical bouts
  "several hours in duration, with a 1–3 h waking interval between them." Nocturnal melatonin
  secretion duration also expanded. **Wehr's own proposed mechanism: dual oscillators entrained
  separately to dawn and dusk, or a two-process model with a lowered sleep-onset threshold during
  the scotoperiod** — i.e. an environmental-timing mechanism, NOT a homeostatic-completion signal.
- **Blumberg, M. S., Myroth, A., Horton, J. et al. (2026), "REM-sleep twitching in adults and the
  maintenance of specialized sensorimotor systems," *Current Biology*, doi:10.1016/j.cub.2026.02.048.**
  Twitching persists into adulthood; proposal is that it serves **maintenance and updating of vital
  sensorimotor functions** — "development, calibration, maintenance, and repair of neuroplastic
  systems." Anchors the *class* of mechanism the user proposes (sleep runs motor programmes for
  calibration/maintenance). Limit: the evidence is about specialised *active-sensing* appendages
  (trunk, whiskers, snout, hands) and neural magnification — not whole-body postural strain.
- **Brooks, P. L. & Peever, J. H. (2016), "A Temporally Controlled Inhibitory Drive Coordinates
  Twitch Movements during REM Sleep," *Curr. Biol.* 26(7).** REM twitches are not random byproducts:
  they follow a defined temporal trajectory (absent at REM initiation, peaking toward termination),
  controlled by a progressive weakening of GABA/glycine drive onto motoneurons.
- **Lee, H. et al. (2015), "The Effect of Body Posture on Brain Glymphatic Transport," *J. Neurosci.*
  35(31):11034–11044.** Lateral position optimises clearance; the authors propose the lateral sleep
  posture **evolved to optimise waste removal during sleep and that posture must matter.** Direct
  published claim that sleeping *posture* has a physiological function — and it is in the lateral
  direction, converging with the lion posture and the ape side-lying data (~65%) already in the repo.
- SWS/GH architecture: SWS dominates the first third of the night; ~70–80% of nightly GH secretion
  in the first SWS episode; REM dominates the last third. The user's *block assignment*
  (first = body, second = mind) matches the gradient.

AGAINST the historical reading:
- **Boyce, N. (2023), "Have we lost sleep? A reconsideration of segmented sleep in early modern
  England," *Medical History* 67(2):91–108, doi:10.1017/mdh.2023.14.** Substantial critique of Ekirch.
  - "First sleep" most commonly meant the **deep** sleep, not a first *segment* — Joseph Caryl (1656):
    "the first sleepe is the deepe sleepe." Depth, not segmentation.
  - EEBO: only **15** hits for "second sleep"; only **5** contain both first and second; 2 are
    metaphorical (the "Second Sleep" of death).
  - The waking was often framed as **troublesome** — prayers "if you happen to awake," "when you
    awake, or cannot sleep" filed under emergency ejaculations. Not a routine interval.
  - **Ekirch's own qualification: "Most people, upon awakening, probably never left their beds."**
  - Wehr's study: 16 subjects (15 M / 1 F), confined alone in a windowless dark room, activity
    forbidden; one subject became severely depressed/suicidal and was withdrawn. Not a natural night.
  - **Wright et al. (2013)** camping study: 8 subjects, 9 h 20 min dark — circadian shift, but
    **no biphasic sleep reported.** A replication failure under *more* naturalistic conditions.
  - Crichton-Browne (1891): "first sleep" = ~90 min of deep sleep before midnight, not a first block.
  - Modern prevalence: **31.2%** of Europeans wake ≥3 nights/week; 7.7% have difficulty resuming.
    Broken sleep is not a lost pre-industrial trait.
  - Boyce's conclusion: segmented sleep occurred, but was **not the sole or necessarily predominant**
    model; the terms held multiple meanings; ambiguous sources are not made less ambiguous by collation.

### Assessment reached (the opinion given)

1. **The thermal confound is the sharpest objection.** The floor is a conductive heat sink. The
   historical second sleep happened in a bed, in a house, with a fire. A floor sleeper's mid-night
   waking may be a thermal arousal that *resembles* the historical event without being it. This is
   the **same error species as the gas/buoyancy confound already flagged in
   `assets/floor-lying-tensor-analysis.md` §7** — a positional/physical mechanism masquerading as a
   control mechanism. The hypothesis must face it first.
2. **The synchronicity problem.** The historical waking was household-wide and socially coordinated.
   A completion signal should be *individual* — you finish when *your* strain is untangled.
   Synchronicity points to an exogenous driver (photoperiod, temperature, the fire) and shifts the
   burden onto the completion-signal reading.
3. **"Vertical orientation calibration" conflates two calibrations the repo already separates:** the
   *somatic/spatial* one that wants the floor (the floor datum, z = 0) and the *autonomic/baroreflex*
   one that wants the vertical (already stated in the traditions asset §7). The sharper version is
   that the mid-night vertical must be **loaded** (weight-bearing), which the historical record
   supports — people walked to the privy, did chores, visited. Discriminating test: seated vs walking.
4. **The block division is directionally right, categorically too clean.** Both blocks contain both
   stages; it is a gradient (SWS→REM across the night). But the *assignment* — body first, mind
   second — matches the architecture.
5. **Strongest surviving framing:** the user's hypothesis is the **somatic analogue of an established
   glymphatic claim.** Lee et al. (2015) show sleeping *posture* optimises *brain* waste clearance
   with a lateral optimum. The user proposes sleeping *posture* optimises *somatic* strain resolution.
   Same structural claim, different tissue — and the somatic version has no literature at all
   (consistent with the repo's measured finding that sleep-posture biomechanics is an orphan field:
   Cheng 2023, 27 FE studies, all supine; Cary 2019, 4 from 4186).
6. **Best version of the historical move:** not "people used to sleep in two segments" (contested)
   but "a mid-night waking with a distinctive character is a **native capacity** that the modern
   sleep environment suppresses and the diagnostic category mislabels." Boyce's own figure supports
   this — 31.2% of modern Europeans wake ≥3 nights/week and it is called *sleep maintenance
   insomnia*. The capacity is native; the pathology framing is the artefact. That is the book's
   thesis applied to sleep, and it is the same move as mattress-suppresses-the-signal.

### Cheap behavioural falsifiers proposed (repo's named concept)

1. **Load-dependence log** — does time-to-first-waking track the previous day's mechanical load,
   independently of SWS rebound? Completion signal says yes; dual oscillator says no. No kit.
2. **Seated vs walking mid-night** — autonomic datum → seated suffices; loaded spatial datum → must
   be weight-bearing.
3. **Thermal control** — hold skin temperature constant (heated mat / bag rated to the floor).
   If the waking vanishes, the completion-signal reading is dead.
4. **Surface control** — floor vs mattress, same protocol. If both the waking and the flat-low-**a**
   signature vanish on a mattress, the floor is doing the work; if they persist, the floor claim
   loses its grip.
5. **Dream log across both blocks** — is the second block dream-rich in this person? Tests the
   block assignment at n = 1.
6. **Corpus re-read** — does the documentary record describe *loaded* mid-night activity or
   *sedentary*? Does it describe the waking as satiated or as afflicted? Boyce says afflicted.
   A reading task; cheapest of all, and the one that would let the book make the historical claim
   honestly.

## Follow-up: "remove the beds from the equation" — does it change the conclusions?

User's move: reappraise sleep systems, strip out Big-Bed's 250-year confounding, note that beds are
recent and not wholly good, and ask whether that changes the assessment.

### Corrections to the user's framing (verified)

- **"Beds are recent" is wrong; "compliant mattresses are recent" is right.** Beds are ancient
  (Egyptian, Roman, Chinese). What is recent: (a) the *compliant* mattress — innerspring coil
  patented **1865**, mass production by the early 20th c., foam/memory-foam mid-to-late 20th c.;
  (b) the bed as a *universal* household item (~200 years, industrialisation); (c) the private
  per-person bedroom. So the "250 years" figure is roughly right for the *industrial bedding trade*
  and ~160 for the innerspring, but not for beds.
- **The pre-industrial bed was not a compliant mattress.** The foundational surface is the **straw
  tick** (*paillasse* / *palliasse*) on a rope or board frame — firm, nonuniform, re-stuffed
  seasonally; the **feather tick** is the compliant end and became common in the 19th century.
- **Consequence: the correct variable is the repo's own compliance axis** (rigid Signorini contact
  vs compliant Winkler foundation), *not* the bed/no-bed axis. A straw tick on boards is
  mechanically closer to the floor than to a pocket-spring. Ekirch's corpus is therefore
  **heterogeneous on the axis that matters** — which converts Boyce's "the corpus is ambiguous"
  into a *structured prediction*.

### The load-bearing correction: Boyce's Wright 2013 counter is overstated

Read the paper directly (Wright et al. 2013, *Curr. Biol.* 23(16):1554–1558; text extracted from
the PDF, not summarised):
- Campers were on a **14 h 40 min light : 9 h 20 min dark** cycle — vs **Wehr's 10 h light : 14 h
  dark**. A **4 h 40 min** difference in the scotoperiod, and the scotoperiod is the driver in
  Wehr's own dual-oscillator / two-process model.
- Measured sleep: **6.8 ± 0.7 h** natural light vs **6.7 ± 0.9 h** electrical (p = 0.268);
  efficiency **87.0 ± 4.6%** vs **87.6 ± 5.6%** (p = 0.49). A short, *consolidated* night — the
  subjects were not sleep-satiated.
- Sleep *structure* (fragmentation, mid-night waking) was **not analysed**; duration and efficiency
  were the reported outcomes. So Boyce's use of Wright is an **unreported outcome, not a tested
  null** — and the paper is a circadian-entrainment study, not a sleep-architecture one.
- Note: camping included **camp fires** — artificial light was present, the early-modern condition.

### The integration that results

**The photoperiod sets the window; the strain sets the use.** Wehr's 14 h dark created the
opportunity and the 1–3 h waking used it; Wright's 9 h 20 min dark left no spare dark time, so the
pattern could not express. This makes the user's claim **conditional** rather than universal — a
native capacity that expresses when the conditions allow — which is the same shape as the floor
claim, and it survives *both* studies. It also relocates the falsifier: not "does the waking
happen" but "**given a long scotoperiod, do the waking's timing and character track the day's
mechanical load?**"

### Which objections moved, and which did not

- **Dissolved as stated, replaced by a sharper one:** the thermal confound. Early modern houses
  were cold, the fire died, people woke into the cold and returned to the warm bed — so the
  historical condition was *also* thermally disturbed, and thermal disturbance cannot distinguish
  bed from floor. But this cuts *against* the historical grounding: if the historical second sleep
  was thermally driven, Ekirch's corpus is evidence for a thermal mechanism and gives the
  completion signal nothing. **Wehr survives as the clean datum** (controlled temperature, 14 h
  dark, pattern still appeared).
- **The real problem with removing beds:** the bed is a **sampling frame**. Ekirch's corpus is
  entirely drawn from a bedded, housed, literate population. Remove beds and you do not get bedless
  evidence — you get *no* descriptive evidence, because the bed generated the documents. The
  bedless record is a *different register* (prescriptive, not descriptive) and the repo already
  holds it; the datum that speaks to the claim is the **Buddhist middle watch**, prescribed
  individually rather than household-wide.
- **Weakened in the user's favour — synchronicity.** The shared bed and shared room would *produce*
  household-wide coordination (shared surface, shared thermal environment, shared schedule).
  Remove the bed and the waking should become individual — and the bedless prescriptive record *is*
  individual. So the synchronicity objection may be a bed artefact.
- **Weakened in the user's favour — corpus ambiguity.** If the compliant substrate *accommodates*
  asymmetry rather than exposing it (the §1 formalism), a bedded population should have a *less
  clean* completion event: vaguer, more variable, sometimes absent. **Boyce's ambiguity is what the
  bed hypothesis predicts.** Testable via the surface heterogeneity of early modern beds — re-read
  Ekirch with the sleeping *surface* as the variable; second-sleep reports should cluster on the
  firm end (straw tick, boards) and thin out on feather ticks.

### "Not wholly good" — the four harms, from the repo's own formalism

1. **Signal suppression** — compliant substrate accommodates asymmetry instead of exposing it → no
   reference-config remodelling (§1, §6).
2. **Supine sprawl** — the configuration no ape adopts (~65% side-lying, Samson 2013), which the
   traditions discourage, and the one absolute exception in the pregnancy section. A mattress makes
   supine sprawl comfortable; a floor does not.
3. **Thermal trapping** — a bed *benefit* with a cost: protective, and why the bed won, but it
   decouples sleep from the thermal cycle the floor couples it to.
4. **Industrialisation of a proprioceptive judgement** — firmness rating, thread count,
   "orthopaedic" claims convert "does this surface let my governor re-chart" into a purchase
   decision. Same species as the maintenance fallacy already in
   `briefs/2026-07-10-brief-sleep-tension-curation.md`.

## Follow-up 2: comfy/comfortable, ache as the channel, the arrogance of the cortex

User's move: extend the comfy/comfortable distinction (comfy *specifies* a position, comfortable
merely *permits* sprawl); propose that the subcortical movement systems communicate with the cortex
**via aches**; that on the floor an ache may indicate the ache should be lain on; that *lots of
aches and nowhere to get comfy* implies getting up for a while is required; and that the cortex is
arrogant in believing it controls the results of the autonomic processes.

### 1. The comfy/comfortable extension is the mechanism of map-building, not a nicety

Mechanical version: a compliant substrate **flattens the objective function** — the pressure field
is smeared, the *gradient* is destroyed, so there is nothing for the subcortical system to descend.
A plateau carries no positional information. A rigid substrate **preserves the gradient** —
pressure localises at contact points, and the local minimum of the strain field is *findable*
because the derivative exists. So the floor's comfy point is a **descendable local minimum**, and
the search that finds it is what builds the map. The mattress removes the search, and therefore
removes the map — the sensory-motor amnesia link (Ch17/18), and the mechanical content of "the
governor maps permission."

**Two refinements the user's version needs:**

- **Sign convention.** §7 of the traditions asset states comfy as a *local maximum of comfort*; the
  mechanical argument descends a *minimum of strain*. Same point, but the asset should fix the sign
  so the gradient claim is consistent. Small, real.
- **"Comfortable allows a sprawl" is only true at onset.** At the creep timescale the mattress
  *becomes* prescriptive: you sink into the ditch and are held there (Bowman's point in
  `assets/losing-the-floor-01.md`). So the mattress is permissive→prescriptive and the floor is
  prescriptive→permissive-after-convergence — the timescales invert, which is exactly the Phase
  1/2/3 table already in that asset. The sharper distinction is therefore not sprawl-vs-maintain but
  **who authors the position: the substrate (the ditch) or the body (against a rigid datum).** The
  compliant substrate is an author, not a neutral venue — consistent with the existing "mattress as
  structural cast" line.

### 2. Ache as the channel — and the question the formulation forces

If ache is the subcortical report, the obvious question is: *why report to a party that cannot act
on it?* The cortex cannot do the tensor analysis. The answer that makes the loop coherent: **the
cortex is the only subsystem that can change the boundary condition.** It can shift position; in
the failure case it can get vertical. So **the ache is a request, addressed to the only agent able
to execute a repositioning** — not a symptom. This is the user's own logic carried to its
conclusion, and it is the strongest formulation in the message.

### 3. The escalation account — a genuine improvement on the completion signal

Turn 1's "completion signal" required a completion *sensor*, which does not exist. The user's new
version requires only a **failure to converge** — a property of any search process. No new sensor
needed. **The vertical-datum cycle is the escalation when the horizontal local search is
exhausted.** That is the right repair, and it is a real theoretical advance on the original
hypothesis.

**But it creates a tension that must be faced:** failure-escalation predicts a *restless* pre-waking
state (the bed-insomnia feeling: "I cannot get comfortable"). The reported floor waking is *calm*
— "time has passed but I was not unconscious," no dozy edge, and getting vertical "not to do
anything." So either the calm is the *post*-escalation state, or the account is wrong.
**Cheapest possible test, first-person: is the pre-waking state restless or calm?** The user can
answer this from their own experience without leaving the room.

### 4. The synthesis this produces — the unification

**Boyce's "troublesome" bedded waking and the user's calm floor waking are the two predicted
outcomes of the same loop, split by surface compliance.** A bedded population has a flattened
gradient → diffuse ache field → unsatisfiable request → restless, troublesome waking (which is
exactly what the early modern prayers describe: "if you happen to awake," "when you awake, or
cannot sleep"). A floor sleeper has an intact gradient → localised ache → satisfiable request →
converged comfy point → calm. This unifies last turn's objections with this turn's mechanism, and
it makes the documentary corpus a *test* of the mechanism rather than a problem for it.

### 5. The arrogance of the cortex — the unifying diagnosis, with one repair

Six instances already in the repo, all the same shape (a subsystem claiming authorship of what
another subsystem produced):
1. "Awareness is the gate, not the engine" (traditions asset §7) — the gate believes it is the engine.
2. The maintenance fallacy (`briefs/2026-07-10-brief-sleep-tension-curation.md`) — the practitioner's
   cortex attributes recovery to its routine rather than to the process it merely permitted.
3. The palimpsest — the model's output mistaken for the author's voice.
4. Memory vs documentation — memory claiming an authority that belongs to the documented principles.
5. Big-Bed — the buyer's cortex attributes relief to the purchase rather than to signal suppression.
6. The nesting-regime section — the medical literature "holds only the surface and loses the woman"
   because it addresses the cortex's story, not the autonomic regime.

**Repair needed:** as stated it is unfalsifiable — "the cortex thinks it is in control" is a claim
about a subsystem's self-model and cannot be measured. Cash it as a prediction: **the cortex's
stated reason for an improvement should be wrong in a predictable direction.** The maintenance
fallacy is the named instance. Without that, it is mood, and the repo already flags a "preachy
recovered patient" risk in the tensor asset.

**And the narrow version is stronger than the broad one.** The cortex is genuinely the agent *for
repositioning* — it is the only subsystem that can change the boundary condition (see §2 above). So
the error is not "it thinks it controls everything"; the error is that it **mistakes permission for
production**. It did the repositioning; it did not do the release. That version is defensible and
connects directly to §7's gate/engine formulation.

### 6. Repair to "lie on the ache"

The rule is the **green-yellow frontier in the horizontal frame** — action proposed only at the
frontier. In the red zone (sustained signal) the medical expectation is correct and you must *not*
lie on it. So the rule needs the frontier qualifier, and the same discrimination as the "feels
right, not forced" criterion (§6 of the tensor asset: low-stress remodelling creep vs high-stress
damage creep).

5. **Load-dependence log** (from the first turn) — unchanged, now with the scotoperiod control.

## Follow-up 3: nutrition, fascial creep, and the prestress formulation

User's move: a second subcortical signal is a **requirement for predictable nutrition**; food
becomes **fuel for fascial regeneration**; **side-lying induces creep in the fascia**; and the
bio-tensegrity system spends its floor time, *if allowed to*, **restoring the pre-stress
environment of the overall structure**.

### The prestress formulation is the strongest claim in the whole exchange

"Prestress" is the tensegrity term for the self-equilibrated internal stress state that gives the
structure its shape and stiffness — i.e. it *is* the reference configuration **C**_0. So the claim
**generalises the existing §6 result from the asymmetry to the whole configuration**: §6 currently
tracks ‖Δ**C**_0‖ (the left-minus-right reference-strain difference); the user proposes that what is
being restored is **C**_0 as a whole — its prestress level, not merely its symmetry. That is a bigger
and better claim, and it is the right *level* for the floor-datum argument: a *global* prestress can
only be restored against a *global* datum. A compliant substrate supplies a local,
deformation-history-dependent reference (the ditch), so it cannot restore a global prestress — it can
only accommodate local deviations. The rigid floor is the only substrate that offers a global datum.

It also reframes the ache: not "tension" but a **local deviation from global self-equilibrium** — a
prestress error signal. Consistent with Follow-up 2's ache-as-request.

**Measurement problem this creates.** The existing §10 measure (standing-posture photographs,
single-leg stand, ‖Δ**C**_0‖) is an *asymmetry* measure; a prestress claim needs a *global* measure.
Warn against the obvious one: **diurnal standing height** is confounded — the overnight recovery is
mostly *disc hydration* from spinal unloading, which both bed and floor provide, so it will not
discriminate. Better cheap candidates: **morning pandiculation** (the repo's own §7 says pandiculation
only happens when **a** is already low, so its ease and frequency is a readout of the settled state);
the feel of the first movement on rising; resting postural sway.

### Nutrition: the precision it needs — two timescales, and the gate is retention

The chain has **two distinct timescales** and the user's claim spans both, which is where it needs
care:
- **Creep** (minutes to hours): viscoelastic strain toward an asymptote; rearrangement of existing
  matrix (water, proteoglycan, progressive fibre recruitment). **Needs no new material.** This is the
  Estébanez / Thornton mechanism already in §6.
- **Matrix consolidation** (days to weeks): actual synthesis and turnover. **Substrate-gated** —
  collagen synthesis needs amino acids, vitamin C, iron, copper. This is where "food as fuel for
  fascial regeneration" belongs.

So: **the night's work is not nutrition-gated; the retention of it is.** A floor night on poor
substrate yields the mechanical length change and then loses it. That is the same shape as the
acute-versus-chronic stretch distinction in the tissue literature, and it makes the claim testable.

**"Predictable" is not a preference — it is a phase-locking requirement.** The MMC runs in the
*fasted* state (~90–120 min, motilin-driven; Vantrappen 1979 and Takahashi 2012 already cited in the
tensor asset §7). So predictable nutrition cashes as **predictable fasting intervals**, not calorie
timing. The requirement is on the *gaps*. This is the traditional-practice register's content too
(the Ayurvedic regular-meal regimen; the monastic meal schedule), and the night is the longest fasted
interval of the day.

### Side-lying as the creep-induction posture — right, and it entails alternation

Mechanically well-founded: side-lying presents the largest contact area of the three main postures,
so load is distributed and *long-duration low-load* is achievable without pressure damage — which is
the creep regime. Supine concentrates load on bony prominences (sacrum, occiput, heels) and is
time-limited by tissue tolerance. Prone is universally discouraged (traditions + the ape record).
The block converts it from isotropic to **directed** creep along a specific line (§6).

Two things the claim must carry:
- **Alternation is entailed, not optional.** Creep is directional and side-lying is asymmetric, so a
  single chirality induces one-sided creep. §6's Poynting-by-analogy argument and the traditions'
  rotation (Buddhism right, Ayurveda left, the Yogaratnakara sequence) are the correction. The claim
  *requires* the rotation.
- **It takes a side against received opinion.** The Alexander tradition explicitly cautions that
  side-lying "creates asymmetrical tension patterns" and is emphatically supine. The user's claim is
  against that tradition — fine, but state it as taking a side.

### The novel test this produces

**Is the mid-night waking coupled to the MMC phase?** The night is the fasted window; the MMC cycles
at ~90–120 min; gastric phase III is vagal-dependent (Takahashi 2012, already in the repo); vagal
afferents can trigger arousal. The MMC phase is predictable from the last meal, so if the waking
tracks it, that is a **third mechanism** for the waking and the one that makes "predictable
nutrition" load-bearing for the waking itself. Cheap-ish and genuinely new.

### Honest warning: the waking now has five candidate causes

Cycle boundary, dual oscillator, thermal arousal, completion signal (Follow-up 1), diffuse-ache
escalation (Follow-up 2), and now MMC/gut arousal. **The field needs pruning, not more candidates.**
The tensor asset's own §9 skeptical bottom line applies: elegance is a warning sign, and a
hypothesis that explains the waking five ways explains it zero ways. The perturbation protocol's gate
between "note" and "touch" is the relevant discipline — note the new mechanism, do not add it to the
model until one of the others has been killed.

### Enabling conditions now operate on two timescales

§7's list (safe, warm, comfy) gates the **nightly** process. Nutrition gates the **consolidation** of
it. So the enabling-conditions specification gains a slow-timescale member alongside the fast ones.

**The inference.** If (i) a rigid datum exposes structural deviation, (ii) low-load creep remodels the
reference configuration, and (iii) growth requires continuous remodelling of that configuration, then
the *demand* for remodelling is highest during growth and the behaviours producing it should be most
prevalent in the growing. Common observation confirms it — but it is a prediction the framework makes,
not a description fitted to it.

**The inversion: permitted versus driven.** The conventional account is the *elasticity* account —
children are rubbery so they *can* get into odd positions; the postures are an undirected by-product
of flexibility. The remodelling account makes them a **behaviour with a function** — directed and
sustained. **Discriminating features:** aimed at **rigid edges** (sofa arms, doorframes, railings —
frames, not cushions) and **held** (creep needs duration). The reported observation is the directed,
sustained version. Observation only, no kit.

milestone: epiphyseal fusion 14–18 (F) / 16–20 (M); peak bone mass ~25–30; CNS maturation ~25. But

driver of matrix synthesis — the substrate-gated consolidation step. **So the behaviour may thin
because the consolidation *capacity* falls, not because the growth *demand* stops.** Correlated but
not identical; different predictions (rung 13).

**Suppression versus absence.** Adults do not stop; they do it in private and when licensed —
"stretching," "mobility work," "yoga," the post-work sprawl. So the thinning is better read as

traditions then read as **conservation mechanisms for the developmental default** — which is why they
