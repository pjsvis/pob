# Brief: The Floor as Calibration Datum

**Date:** 2026-07-13
**Status:** Open

## Source

A dialog (2026-07-13) begun from a provocation on SNR and proprioception in a bounded continuum bio-tensegrity system constrained to a Euclidean half-space by an orthogonal 1g field. The dialog produced a model and a first-person floor session that confirmed and extended it. This brief persists both, and proposes the next experiments.

## Objective

Establish that the floor is not merely a support (reducing the inference burden — the contact-polygon brief's point) but a *calibration datum*: contact with the datum re-zeros the proprioceptive map of the contacted segment. Show that this single mechanism does two jobs — compute-cost reduction AND map re-zeroing — and that cost and calibration track together (cheap-to-hold regions stay calibrated; expensive-to-hold regions forget). Propose a family of "anchoring routines" that exploit the mechanism, generalize the arm routine to legs/spine/breath/head, and reconcile the active-pandiculation method with the passive idle-state method of chapters 5 and 18.

## The model (from the dialog)

### Two graviceptors bracket the body

The 1g field is the reference, not the floor. The floor is where the constraint is *enforced by contact*; the field itself is sensed throughout the body. The body carries (at least) two high-bandwidth graviceptors at opposite ends:

- **Plantar** (z ≈ 0): senses the ground reaction — the field's effect transmitted through body mass to the contact. High SNR for "where is my base, what is my support polygon."
- **Vestibular otoliths** (z ≈ 1.5 m, the head): senses the field *directly* as linear acceleration. High SNR for "which way is down." Always on, co-located with the sensorium.

SNR does not decrease monotonically with height. It is a two-ended tent — high at the feet, high at the head, minimum in the upper-limb periphery. "Distance from the reference plane" is the wrong metric; "kinematic distance from the nearest graviceptive anchor" is the right one. The height-monotonic version of the original provocation is the palimpsest to retire; the kinematic-distance version is the load-bearing one.

### The tensegrity continuum cuts against monotonic decay

A tensegrity continuum distributes strain globally; the continuous channel does not decay segment-by-segment the way a jointed chain does. The SNR decrease is a property of the *jointed* chain (each interposed joint adds integration error), not the continuum. The body is a hybrid: a global strain field overlaid on a jointed skeleton. The decrease lives in the joints; the continuum works against it. Confirmed phenomenologically: with the whole arm on the floor and the gravity load removed, the continuum's *pre-stress* becomes audible as "residual tension" — a different signal from position, carrying set-point rather than location.

### The floor converts absolute into relative localization

When a segment lies on the floor, every point on it sits at z ≈ 0 — the segment joins the contact polygon. The absolute problem ("where is my hand in the world frame," which needs vision when the segment is pendular) collapses into a relative one ("what is my forearm's angle relative to my upper arm"), which proprioception solves alone. The floor supplies the absolute frame; the body supplies the relative sense.

### Cost and calibration track together

Contact with the datum both (a) reduces the inference burden (floor takes the load — cheap) and (b) re-zeros the map (segment at the datum gets calibrated). The inverse holds: a pendular segment (far from the datum) is both expensive to maintain AND prone to decalibration. This bridges the contact-polygon piece (cost) and the amnesia chapters (decalibration) — same mechanism, two faces.

## The source observation (the arm routine)

Verbatim from the floor session:

> - Started laying upper arms on the floor and rotating the lower arms.
> - Graduated to laying the whole arms on the floor and sensing residual tension.
> - Began pandiculating the arms by slowly raising and lowering the forearms.
> - Eventually could feel the forearms and the upper arms when raised as being solid and located in space.
> - The sense from the wrists and hands was very subdued, but not worryingly so.
> - It felt more like an arm calibration thing was in process and the wrists and hands were not involved.
> - When raised, the arm was "like a lump of clay"; all that was sensed was angle and presence (body-relative, not world-frame).

### Interpretation

- **Angle + presence = position sense + ownership, purified.** Effort, tension pattern, internal structure, world-frame location, and agency all dropped away. The floor stripped gravity-compensating tension; the pandiculation re-zeroed the joint angle.
- **The purified signal is sparse.** The rich felt shape of the arm in normal life is mostly *effort pattern* (tension habit), not *position data*. The floor filters the effort; what remains is angle + presence. The shape we thought we were feeling was the tension habit, not the geometry.
- **Clay is plastic, not floppy.** The arm held coherent (pre-stress reset) but undifferentiated (habitual tension stripped) — moldable, not empty. The calibrated state is a plastic window, not a void.
- **One DOF calibrated → one clean angle.** Only the elbow (raise/lower) was explored; only that angle came through clean.
- **The hand is the next DOF, not a failure.** Subdued because the wrist wasn't calibrated, not because something is wrong. The queue is proximal-to-distal.

## The bridge to the amnesia chapters

Ch18 models the sensory-motor map as a metric tensor $g_{ij}$ that goes *undefined* in unmapped regions; the floor drops the threat level, the quarantine softens, and the map redraws *passively* — "do not try to feel it more clearly. Do not try to move it... the territory was always there; the chart comes back." The dialog refines this in two ways:

1. **The map is not monolithic.** Ch18 treats "the map" as one thing that goes dark and redraws. The floor session separates it into (at least) two layers: *position* (angle + presence) and *tension pattern* (effort/shape). The floor strips the tension pattern (load removed) and re-zeros the position. The "lump of clay" is the map with tension stripped and position re-zeroed — sparse, not the rich shape of normal sensation. Ch18's "textured, located" verification metric gestures at both layers returning; the dialog shows they are independently affected and separately observable.

2. **Active vs passive — a productive tension.** Ch18's smoke test is explicit: do not move it. The arm routine *pandiculated* — active slow contraction-and-release. Proposed reconciliation: the *passive* idle-state restores **presence/ownership** (the region comes back online — ch18's metric); the *active* pandiculation restores **precise position sense** (the clean joint angle — the dialog's "angle"). The floor enables both (drops threat level + provides datum), but the operation on the map differs: presence returns by attendance; position is re-zeroed by active sweep through loaded↔unloaded. The "lump of clay" = presence (passive) + one precise angle (active), with the tension pattern still stripped. **This needs assessment**: does active efforting interfere with the idle-state redraw, or complement it? The dialog's evidence suggests complement (different layers), but ch18's stated method currently holds the passive line.

Ch17 (the Amnesia of Progress / path-dependence) is the practitioner face: you cannot recover the path $\gamma$ from the terminal state $\mathbf{g}_{\text{terminal}}$. The anchoring routines are relevant here because they are *explicit, repeatable paths* — a partial answer to practitioner amnesia. If the routine can be written down as a sequence (anchor → null-space DOF → pandiculate), the path is not lost to sub-cortical memory. The routines are an attempt to externalize the path that ch17 says disappears.

## The anchoring routine template (generalized from the arm)

1. **Anchor** a segment to the datum (bring it into the contact polygon).
2. **Explore a DOF in the null space** of the contact (motion that doesn't break contact — the cheapest probe).
3. **Remove gravity load** to hear the continuum pre-stress (residual tension).
4. **Pandiculate** — active slow sweep through loaded↔unloaded — to write the new set-point.
5. The **unanchored adjacent segment is the next DOF** (subdued until calibrated).
6. **Work outward** from the anchor along the kinematic chain.

Every routine is a contact-polygon manipulation that converts an absolute-localization problem into a relative one.

## Proposed next experiments

### Leg routines — the mirror

The arm routine anchors *proximal* (upper arm) and works *distal* (toward hand), because the arm's free end is distal and the floor supplies the missing graviceptive anchor proximally. The leg routine should anchor *distal* (foot — already the plantar graviceptor) and work *proximal* (toward hip), because the leg's datum end is the foot. **Prediction: the routines mirror because the graviceptors bracket the body.**

- **Ankle/foot**: supine, heels on floor (or feet standing). Explore ankle DOFs (dorsi/plantarflexion, inversion/eversion) — null-space (heel stays, foot moves). Toes = next DOF. Addresses shod-foot decalibration.
- **Knee**: supine, one foot on floor (knee bent). Heel-slide (extend knee along floor — null-space), then lift the foot (break contact) and feel the leg as clay.
- **Hip**: supine, knees bent, feet on floor. Knees fall in/out (hip rotation, adduction/abduction) — null-space (feet stay, knees splay). Addresses chair-bound hip flexion.

**The leg's twist:** the leg decalibrates at *both* ends (shod feet + chair-bound hips), with the knee relatively maintained (walking). Unlike the arm (one free end), the leg has two decalibrated fronts. **Prediction:** starting from the foot anchor, the hip is the subdued "far end" until calibrated upward.

### Spine routine — the central continuum

Supine, whole spine on floor (maximal contact). Sense where the spine contacts and where it arches away (lumbar, thoracic curves — the gaps are the un-grounded, un-mapped segments). Pandiculate the lumbar: small pelvic tilts (flatten lumbar to floor, release) — loaded↔unloaded sweep. The cervical spine is off the floor in supine (natural curve) = the next DOF, subdued like the hand. Connects to ch08 (sub-occipital origin), ch05 (idle state).

### Breath as internal anchor

The diaphragm is an internal tension structure. Supine, the abdominal breath moves the belly against the floor — the floor references the breath's excursion externally. A different class: not a limb on the datum, but an internal rhythm referenced against the datum.

### Head/eyes — the upper graviceptor

Supine, head on floor: the vestibular graviceptor is now *at* the datum. Eye movements (the vestibulo-ocular reflex) with the head still = null-space motion (head anchored, eyes explore). Calibrates the oculomotor system against the vestibular reference. This is the opposite end from the plantar routines — calibrating the upper graviceptor. Connects to ch08.

### Standing micro-routine (the hard mode)

Feet are the anchor. Slow weight shifts (center of pressure moves within the support polygon) = the standing analog of pandiculation. Noisier: the whole body holds against gravity, so effort masks position. The floor is preferred because it strips effort; standing is the hard mode. Connects to the Frontier Scale (ch14) and floor-state (ch15) material.

## Side-light: the atlas-of-charts parallel (logged 2026-07-13, not formalised)

A digression into GTA 6's engine architecture (Rockstar patents; Jose Crespo's Medium piece, stripped of its "old math vs AI" clickbait frame) surfaced a clean domain parallel. The game engine solves localisation in a large world with an *atlas of local charts* (each zone its own origin, glued by transition maps) because one global float32 frame loses precision far from the origin. The body solves the same problem with two graviceptors (plantar + vestibular) as two local frames glued by the kinematic chain. The floor calibration *is* the local-chart re-anchor: the segment gets a fresh high-precision origin ($z \approx 0$) instead of integrating from the global origin through a long chain that has accumulated drift. The "lump of clay, angle and presence" is the segment's position re-expressed in the clean local chart.

One structural divergence: the game engine localises by *descending a tree* (region → city → district → street → room), discarding whole subtrees — $O(\log n)$. The body's kinematic chain is not a tree; each joint depends on the one above, error compounds, nothing to cull. **Vision exists because the chain is not a tree.** The hands are the float32-jitter-at-the-far-edge, and the body's fix is not a hierarchy (it has none) but a second sensor modality.

Two smaller echoes: (1) the rain patent's *compute flat, map to curved* is the posture-cost story in miniature — supine is the flat problem, standing is the curved one; (2) GTA 6's moving-target ragdoll (recompute the target pose every instant vs GTA 5's fixed animator pose) is ch17's anti-deconstruction argument — the floor's idle-state is the moving-target approach, and the pandiculation trains the controller's moving-target tracking.

The atlas-of-charts is genuinely useful vocabulary for the calibration-datum model — it gives the two-ended graviceptor a clean manifold-theoretic name native to the book's existing register. But this is the first occurrence of the parallel, so by the project's incremental-formalisation discipline it is noted here, not formalised. If the leg/spine experiments confirm the model and the brief moves to a draft, the atlas-of-charts is the right frame to carry it.
