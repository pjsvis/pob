# The Euclidean Baseline

In those first few minutes on the rug, nothing active is happening. You aren’t stretching, you aren't core-bracing, and you aren't doing three sets of ten of anything an influencer recommended. Yet, the physical feedback is immediate.

You feel a slow, horizontal spreading across your upper back. A rib cage that felt compressed suddenly expands. The deep, defensive contraction in your left hip socket—the one you didn’t even realize you were holding—gradually lets go. After ten minutes, you stand up, and the room looks slightly different because your eye-level has shifted a centimeter upward.

Mainstream physical therapy calls this "passive relaxation." That definition is functionally bankrupt. What your central nervous system just executed was a high-level system reset: a total coordinate reset.
