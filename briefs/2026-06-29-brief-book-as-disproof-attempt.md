# Brief: The Book as Disproof Attempt — The Interrogative Stance

**Date:** 2026-06-29
**Status:** Open — the keystone stance. The math-as-illustration brief (`2026-06-29-brief-math-as-illustration.md`) and this one are facets of the same position; this is the spine that unifies them.

## Objective

State the book's operating principle explicitly, in the Preface, in the book's voice: **the book is an attempt to disprove itself.** The question it asks, repeatedly, is *can continuum mechanics handle this?* — and the case it is built around is the most ordinary one: *can continuum mechanics help with my staved finger, or should I trust the debunked old wives' tale about RICE?* The book is not a thesis defended; it is a candidate model subjected to its own disproof attempts, in public, in the reader's body, with the failures reported as honestly as the survivals.

## Context

The external eval (`debriefs/evals/openhuman-repo-eval.md`) read the equations as claims and found them underdeveloped, and read the case files as assertions that outran evidence. Both critiques landed because the book's operating principle was invisible — nothing told the reader that the book was testing, not asserting. The math-as-illustration stance (`2026-06-29-brief-math-as-illustration.md`) clarified *what the equations are* (illustrations of prior art, not proofs). This stance clarifies *how the book holds its claims* — interrogatively, as disproof attempts. Together they make the book's register legible end to end.

The staved finger is the right anchor for the stance because it is the book's opening and its most ordinary claim. The question "can continuum mechanics help with my staved finger, or should I trust RICE?" is not rhetorical — it is the actual question, and RICE is the actual foil. RICE (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation) is the debunked old wives' tale the standard model still reaches for; the book's counter is not "we have a better tale" but "here is a framework, let us see if it handles the staved finger better than the tale does." That is the disproof attempt in miniature: the framework is put on the line against the thing it would replace, on a case anyone can run.

### Why "attempt to disprove itself" is the right formulation

It is not a flourish. It is the operating principle that makes every other stance coherent:

- **Math as illustration** — the equations are not defending a thesis; they are the framework put under load to see if it breaks.
- **Interrogative posture** — "can continuum mechanics handle this?" is the question a disproof attempt asks, repeatedly.
- **Smoke tests** — the moment the framework risks failing, in the reader's body. A book trying to disprove itself *must* offer the test where it could lose.
- **Falsification protocols (Appendix A)** — the formal version: here is how you would show this is wrong.
- **Status-of-evidence notes** (`2026-06-29-brief-status-of-evidence.md`) — the honest report of where the disproof attempt has and hasn't been survived.

A book that asserts cannot offer these honestly; a book trying to disprove itself cannot withhold them. The stances filed this session are facets of this one.

### The Popperian move done right

The common misreading of Popper is "a theory is scientific if it *could* be falsified." The sharper reading — the one this stance enacts — is that a theory is scientific if it is *actively subjected to falsification attempts, including by its own author.* A theory that merely permits falsification but never faces it is a conjecture waiting. The book that offers its own disproof attempts has started doing science rather than describing it.

### The Ptolemy/Kepler parallel

The book's actual move is closer to Ptolemy→Kepler than to Penrose. That shift was not "we discovered new planets" — it was "we re-described the same motions with a cleaner geometry, and the predictions got simpler." The data (the body, the pain, the telemetry) is unchanged; the model is different; the predictions (the smoke tests) are cleaner. That is a modest, strong claim. The Penrose parallel in Ch00 (compression that preserves structural truth) may earn a companion — or cede to — the Ptolemy/Kepler parallel, which fits the book's move more exactly. Open for the Ch00 revisit.

## The load-bearing lines (for the Preface or Section 00)

Two lines carry the stance and should be drafted in the book's voice:

1. **"This is not rocket science — this is science your CNS handles every day without you even thinking about it."** The demystification that makes the deepest point: the body is already the continuum-mechanics computer. The book describes what the CNS already computes; it gives the reader a vocabulary for the computation they already run. This lands the math-as-illustration stance in the reader's body, not just their head.

2. **"The descriptions of physical events link the feeling/telemetry to an explanation — this is always a driver."** The book's actual engine: the felt thing (telemetry) and the formal thing (the framework) are linked by description, and the link is what makes the framework worth carrying. The grad/div/curl commentary is this link made explicit. The driver is not the math; the driver is the linkage.

Around these, the stance statement: the book asks *can continuum mechanics handle this?* — starting with the staved finger, against RICE — and reports honestly. The book is an attempt to disprove itself.

### Telling the reader the question (the Preface as invitation)

The stance is not just stated; it is **handed to the reader** so they carry the question, not just read about it. The difference: a label says "this book tests a framework"; an invitation says "here is the question — can continuum mechanics handle your staved finger, or should you trust RICE? — and the book is going to try to answer it, starting now." The reader who opens with the question already in mind runs every smoke test and case file as *their* attempt to answer, not just the author's. That is the difference between a book describing its method and a book enacting it. The interrogative stance only truly works when the reader inherits the question.

This also resolves the audience ambiguity one final turn: the clinician and the mathematician may not share a math background, but they share the question. The question is legible without the math; the math is the illustration; the question is the hook.

### The tease and the payoff

**Tease the staved finger; pay it off not much later.** The tease plants the question; the payoff answers it while the question is still warm. Too long a gap and the reader forgets they were asking — the question dies and the explanation arrives into an empty room (the applecart principle: a promise unkept is a contract violation). The staved finger is the book's opening image already; the tease is almost free — it is a matter of framing the opening as the *question* rather than the answer.

The shape: open with the staved finger as a felt phenomenon (the reader does it, or recalls it), name RICE as the standard-model reach and its debunking, then pose the question — *can continuum mechanics do better?* — and say the book will try to answer it, starting now. The full explanation lands once the vocabulary (Ch00) and the floor datum (Ch01) are in place — "not much later" — so the reader carries the question through the vocabulary chapter rather than meeting the framework cold.

**The tease must not become a marketing hook.** "Can continuum mechanics cure your pain? Read on..." is the register the book's anti-marketing manifesto explicitly rejects. The discipline: state the question honestly, name the foil (RICE) honestly, say what the book will do (try to answer it) — no more. The reader inherits the question because it is a real question, not because the book manufactured suspense. The smoke tests do the suspense work organically; the prose does not need to.

## Constraints

- **The single-voice gate applies.** The stance is in the book's voice — dry, no "we", no seminar register, no marketing. "This book is an attempt to disprove itself" fits the voice (it is the kind of thing the book already says); humble-bragging about falsifiability does not.
- **The interrogative must not become coy.** "Well, can it? Who knows!" is the seminar register wearing humility as a costume. The discipline: ask the question, then show the framework attempting to answer it, and report honestly whether it succeeds. The smoke tests are what save the interrogative from evasion — each is the framework on the line, in the reader's body.
- **The applecart principle applies.** The stance must read as a clarification of what the book is, not a retreat from what it claimed. The book is not less bold for saying it is testing; it is more honest, and honesty is the book's register. If the stance reads as "sorry, we might be wrong," it is wrong; if it reads as "here is what we are doing, and here is where it could fail," it is right.
- **The tease is not a marketing hook.** The question is stated honestly, the foil (RICE) named honestly, the book's intent (try to answer it) said plainly — no manufactured suspense. The smoke tests do the suspense work organically; the prose does not. The anti-marketing manifesto holds.
- **The payoff comes while the question is warm.** Tease the staved finger in the opening; pay it off once Ch00 (vocabulary) and Ch01 (floor datum) are in place — not much later. A gap too long is a promise unkept.
- **The perturbation process applies.** Stance statement drafted and reviewed first; the Preface touch second, gated on approval.
