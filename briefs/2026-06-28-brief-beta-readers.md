# Brief: Beta Readers — Emma, Barbara, Craig

**Date:** 2026-06-28
**Status:** Open

## Objective

Identify and document the beta reading cohort for the book, with specific reading assignments matched to each reader's expertise.

## The Readers

### Laura — the humanities test

- Studying history at the University of Ottawa
- Tests whether the book's narrative and argumentation hold up for a reader with no science or engineering background — someone who reads for the story, the structure, and the quality of the prose
- A history student will notice if the argument is circular, if the transitions are weak, or if the voice is inconsistent. These are the same skills a good editor brings.
- **Assign:** the staved finger essay, the onboarding chapter, Chapter 16 (The Amnesia Paradox — the fallacy of deconstruction is an argument about historiography as much as biomechanics), Chapter 18 (The Clavicular Fuse — a detective story, which should read as a narrative)

### Emma — the accessibility test

- Niece; studies biology at the University of Ottawa
- Known as "tardigrade girl" — deep interest in extremophile biology
- Represents the book's primary audience: an intelligent, curious reader who is *not* a mathematician or engineer
- She will test whether the Familiar Hooks land, whether the deks make sense, whether the grad/div/curl explanations are legible to someone who hasn't taken vector calculus
- **Assign:** the onboarding chapter, the staved finger essay, Chapter 03 (The Crystalline Grid — impact dynamics, relevant to biology), Chapter 12 (The Plantar Interface)

### Barbara and Craig — the rigour test

- Aircraft certification engineers at Transport Canada
- Work with continuum mechanics professionally — modern flexible wings are analysed using stress tensors, anisotropic materials, and finite-element methods
- They will test whether the math is correct, whether the tensor formulations are sound, and whether the book's claims would survive scrutiny from someone who uses these tools daily
- **Assign:** the onboarding chapter, Chapter 02 (The Torsion Engine — the Poynting Effect is directly relevant to wing torsion), Chapter 10 (The Laminated Core — laminated shell mechanics, directly parallel to composite wing structures), Chapter 18 (The Clavicular Fuse — brittle failure, stress invariants)

## Why This Cohort Works

The book has three simultaneous goals: *taken seriously*, *inviting for the regular reader*, and *well-written as prose*. A single beta reader cannot test all three. Laura tests the writing and the argument; Emma tests the invitation; Barbara and Craig test the seriousness. If a chapter passes Emma but fails Barbara, it is accessible but wrong. If it passes Barbara but fails Emma, it is correct but opaque. If it passes both but fails Laura, it is correct and accessible but poorly written. The book needs to pass all three.

The tardigrade connection is a bonus: Emma's interest in extremophile biology may surface cross-species comparisons that the book's bio-tensegrity framework can speak to (tardigrades as a case study in extreme continuum mechanics — survival through tun state, a phase change in the tissue matrix).

Laura's history training is also a bonus: the book's argument structure — assertion, evidence, falsification — is closer to historiography than to experimental science. A historian's eye will catch arguments that assert without demonstrating, or that demonstrate without asserting.

## Deliverables

- This brief serves as the record of the beta reading plan
- When chapters are ready for beta reading, package them as standalone PDFs (`just pdf`) and distribute
- Collect feedback in a debrief after each reader responds
- Do not send chapters until the voice pass is complete — sending raw Gemini drafts would test Gemini's voice, not the book's

## Constraints

- Beta readers see PDFs, not the website or the markdown source
- Each reader gets a tailored package, not the whole book
- Feedback is collected individually, not in a group — avoid consensus bias
