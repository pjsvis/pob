## The core claim

In classical biomechanics, posture is treated as a free lunch — or at least a flat rate. The body stands up, the muscles hold it there, and the cost is assumed to be negligible or constant. In a biological bounded continuum, posture is neither free nor flat. It is a highly dynamic, daily operational expenditure. The body spends energy to maintain its shape against gravity, and the amount it spends depends on the state of its fascial eigenstructure.

The thermodynamic trade-off is the one-line distillation: **energy cannot be cheated.** If the fascia cannot carry the prestress passively, the muscles must carry it actively. There is no third option. A person who sits at a desk all day and feels as exhausted as if they had run a marathon has spent the day burning metabolic and computational fuel just to stop their head from falling through the floor. The exhaustion is not mysterious; it is the maintenance burden, paid in full.

## The Maintenance Burden Equation

The Maintenance Burden ($M_B$) is the total energetic cost of maintaining structural integrity over a 24-hour cycle:

$$M_B = E_{\text{metabolic}} + E_{\text{computational}}$$

Where:

- $E_{\text{metabolic}}$ is the physical energy (ATP) consumed by active muscle guarding to hold the structure against the $1g$ vector field. This is the cost of maintaining eigenvectors that the fascia cannot carry — the muscles are doing the fascia's job.
- $E_{\text{computational}}$ is the CNS tax paid to continuously calculate, process, and filter noisy spatial telemetry. This is the cost of the covariant-to-contravariant transform running on a degenerate or noisy metric tensor — the cerebellum is balancing an unmapped, wobbly structure, and the computation is expensive because the data is bad.

Both terms are spectral quantities. $E_{\text{metabolic}}$ is the cost of clamping eigenvectors that the tissue should carry but can't. $E_{\text{computational}}$ is the cost of reading a noisy eigenstructure that the tissue should have computed but didn't. The maintenance burden is the price of a degraded eigenstructure, paid in two currencies: ATP and CPU cycles.

## The three profiles as eigenstructure states

The three structural types are not a moral hierarchy (good/better/best). They are regions of a continuous spectrum — the maintenance burden is a continuous quantity, and the types are where the spectrum clusters.

### Type A: The Degraded Eigenstructure (High $M_B$)

The standard modern, mattress-sleeping chassis with unresolved restrictions and accumulated structural debt.

- **Eigenstructure state:** Degraded. The fascial eigenstructure is misaligned, matted, or unmapped. The eigenvectors (fiber directions) don't match the principal stress directions of habitual loading. The eigenvalues (resting tensions) are maintained by active muscle, not by passive fascial prestress.
- **$E_{\text{metabolic}}$: High.** The CNS runs constant, heavy muscle guarding to freeze unstable joint spaces. The psoas and spinal stabilizers fire 24/7, not because the body is moving, but because the fascia can't hold the eigenvectors and the muscles must. This is the metabolic cost of a spectral structure the tissue can't maintain.
- **$E_{\text{computational}}$: High.** The proprioceptive map is noisy — low SNR, blind spots, structural distortions. The cerebellum runs heavy error-correction continuously, balancing an unmapped tower on bad data. The covariant-to-contravariant transform is expensive because the metric tensor is degenerate. This is the computational cost of reading a noisy eigenstructure.
- **The diurnal loop:** The nightly re-computation is bypassed (mattress sleeping — the compliant surface absorbs the perturbation before the eigenstructure can be read). The system starts each day with accumulated debt and pays it in guarding and computation until exhaustion.
- **$M_B$: Bankrupting.** The system consumes so much energy maintaining static verticality that it has little left for recovery, creative processing, or high-performance movement. The exhaustion after a sedentary day is not laziness; it is the maintenance burden, paid in full.

### Type B: The Re-Computed Eigenstructure (Efficient $M_B$)

A system that runs the nightly re-computation — the floor-to-suspension/bar calibration pipeline.

- **Eigenstructure state:** Re-aligned. The nightly floor practice re-computes the eigenstructure: the eigenvectors are re-aligned to the floor's known boundary, the eigenvalues are redistributed, the fascia carries the prestress passively. The map is clean because the re-computation ran.
- **$E_{\text{metabolic}}$: Low.** The structural prestress is carried by the passive, elastic properties of the fascial sheets. Muscles fire only when active movement is required, not to maintain static posture. The fascia is doing its job; the muscles are free.
- **$E_{\text{computational}}$: Low.** The spatial coordinate map is clean (high SNR, the floor re-indexed it). The cerebellum reads the eigenstructure cheaply — the covariant-to-contravariant transform runs on a clean metric tensor. No background warning loops, no heavy error-correction.
- **The diurnal loop:** The day's accumulated tension is systematically liquidated every evening on the floor. The system starts each day with a clean map and pays only the maintenance cost of the day's loading, not the accumulated debt.
- **$M_B$: Efficient.** The maintenance overhead is minimal. The system is quiet, resilient, and responsive. The energy saved on guarding and computation is available for recovery, creative processing, and movement.

### Type C: The Settled Eigenstructure (Minimal $M_B$)

The asymptotic state of sustained floor practice. The eigenstructure has converged to a stable, wabi-sabi configuration that the tissue maintains with no active support.

- **Eigenstructure state:** Settled. The eigenvectors have converged to the lowest-maintenance configuration for this particular body — unique, asymmetric, weathered, wabi-sabi. Not a Platonic ideal; the body's own optimal eigenstructure, which is not anyone else's. The eigenvalues are carried entirely by passive fascial prestress.
- **$E_{\text{metabolic}}$: Nominal.** The system distributes the gravity field across its balanced tensegrity rigging. The fascia carries the prestress; the muscles are silent. The gravity vector is routed through the eigenstructure, not fought against it.
- **$E_{\text{computational}}$: Near zero.** The alerts are torn down. The system operates on a silent log level — the eigenstructure is stable, the map is clean, the cerebellum has nothing to correct. The computational cost approaches the passive minimum.
- **The diurnal loop:** The daily tension is processed almost automatically; background movements (desk rotations, walking, breathing) keep the thixotropic fascial fluid sheared and hydrated with zero conscious effort. The nightly re-computation still runs, but it is a light maintenance pass, not a deep re-solve.
- **$M_B$: The absolute minimum.** The system requires virtually no active energy to maintain its structure. The body is not fighting gravity; it is resting in its own eigenstructure.

## The thermodynamic trade-off

The core rule: **if the fascia cannot carry the prestress, the muscles must.** There is no third option.

The eigenstructure is either maintained by the tissue (cheap, passive, low $M_B$) or by the muscles (expensive, active, high $M_B$). The mattress-sleeper who bypasses the nightly re-computation pays the cost in muscle guarding and computational load during the day. The floor-sleeper who runs the re-computation pays a small cost at night and saves the cost during the day.

Floor practice is not a wellness intervention. It is a maintenance cost reduction. You pay a small amount of metabolic and computational energy at night (lying on the floor, breathing, re-computing) to avoid paying a large amount during the day (muscle guarding, error-correction, exhaustion). The diurnal budget is the frame; the floor is the cost-saving measure.

## The felt marker: "activating"

The maintenance burden has a felt signature. The "activating" word from the felt vocabulary (see `the-felt-vocabulary.md`) is the felt report of the nightly re-computation running — the metabolic cost of the eigenstructure being re-aligned.

- **The carb craving** is the metabolic demand of collagen synthesis. The fascia is rebuilding its fiber directions (eigenvectors) and resting tensions (eigenvalues), and collagen synthesis is metabolically expensive — it requires amino acids and energy. The body signals for fuel.
- **The thirst** is the fluid demand. The fascial ground substance is a hydrophilic gel that needs water to reorganise. The eigenstructure can't be re-computed in dehydrated tissue.
- **The weird, slightly uncomfortable, not-bad feeling** is the felt report of the eigenvectors shifting. The nervous system is reading the rearrangement and flagging it as news — not threat (it's not Orange or Red), but change. The "activating" feeling is developmental telemetry: the body saying "under construction," not "danger."

The presence or absence of "activating" is diagnostic:

- **Type A** never feels "activating" because the re-computation is bypassed. The cost is paid in daytime exhaustion instead.
- **Type B** feels "activating" after floor sessions. The re-computation is running; the cost is being paid at night, in tissue, where it belongs.
- **Type C** rarely feels "activating" because the eigenstructure has settled and needs little re-computation.

The absence of "activating" in a person who does not feel Type C's settled quiet is the signal: you are Type A, you are bypassing the re-computation, and you are paying the maintenance burden in daytime exhaustion. The felt vocabulary gives the reader a way to diagnose their own type, from the inside, without any external measurement. This is a Class 2 measurement (see `the-inspection-boundary.md`) — the felt report is internal telemetry, not externally inspectable.

## The falsifiability

**Assertion: the maintenance burden is a real energetic cost, and it is higher for a degraded eigenstructure than for a re-computed one.**

Falsifier: if resting metabolic rate (measurable by indirect calorimetry) does not correlate with structural state — i.e., if a person with chronic muscle guarding (Type A) does not have a higher resting metabolic expenditure than the same person after sustained floor practice (Type B) — then the $E_{\text{metabolic}}$ claim weakens. The prediction is that resting metabolic rate drops as the eigenstructure settles, because the muscles stop doing the fascia's job. Testable, though the effect size may be small relative to total metabolic rate.

**Assertion: the computational drain is a real cognitive cost.**

Falsifier: if cognitive performance on attention/fatigue tasks does not correlate with structural state — i.e., if a Type A person's cognitive performance is the same before and after floor practice that reduces their guarding — then the $E_{\text{computational}}$ claim weakens. The prediction is that reducing the maintenance burden frees computational resources, measurable as improved attention or reduced subjective fatigue. Testable with standard cognitive batteries.

**Assertion: "activating" is the felt marker of the re-computation cost.**

Falsifier: if the carb craving and thirst that accompany the "activating" feeling do not correlate with floor practice (i.e., if they occur at the same rate regardless of whether the re-computation ran), then the connection between "activating" and the metabolic cost of fascial realignment weakens. The prediction is that "activating" + carb craving + thirst cluster after floor sessions and do not cluster at other times. Testable by self-report, by the reader, on their own body.

## The economic frame

The maintenance burden is the economic layer of the spectral frame. The spectral hinge names the structure (eigenvalues, eigenvectors). The inspection boundary names the epistemology (Class 2, internal telemetry only). The felt vocabulary names the experiential report (good/ache/twinge/pain, activating, unmasked). The maintenance burden names the cost. Four layers, one frame:

- **Structure:** the eigenstructure stored in fascia.
- **Cost:** the maintenance burden of holding it, paid in ATP and CPU.
- **Report:** the felt vocabulary for what the cost feels like.
- **Access:** the inspection boundary that says you can only measure it from inside.

The same thing, said four times, not dogmatically. Where the equation is self-evident, one rendering is enough. Where the connection between the cost and the felt report is non-obvious, the layers earn their place by naming it.

## Epistemic register

Same as the spectral hinge: model, not measurement. The maintenance burden equation is a theoretical frame for why posture is exhausting when the eigenstructure is degraded. It is falsifiable (resting metabolic rate, cognitive performance, "activating" clustering). The book presents the frame, states the falsifier, and connects it to the felt vocabulary so the reader can diagnose their own type from the inside. The Jaynes floor applies: if the maintenance burden turns out not to be a real energetic cost (the guarding muscles don't consume more ATP, the noisy map doesn't consume more CPU), it was a charming wrong hypothesis that named a useful question — why does sitting still make you tired? — and the answer turned out to be different than predicted. That's still worth having written.
