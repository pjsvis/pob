In a continuous biotensegrity model, the legs are not independent, vertically stacked compression pillars; they are discontinuous compression struts suspended within a single, pre-stressed, continuous fascial network (Levin, 2007).

When managing contradictory vectors (e.g., simultaneous braking, propulsion, lateral shear, and upstream spinal torsion), equalisation between the limbs is governed by four coupled mechanisms:

**The Pelvic Differential (Sacroiliac Force Closure)**
The pelvis acts mechanically like a limited-slip differential. Through reciprocal nutation and counternutation at the sacroiliac joints (SIJs), unilateral ground reaction forces are converted into structural stiffening on the stance side while tensioning the contralateral myofascial matrix for swing-phase release (Vleeming et al., 1995; Snijders et al., 1993). This self-bracing mechanism ("force closure") prevents shear failure by dynamically shifting structural loads across the midline rather than isolating them to one hip.

**Contralateral Cross-Body Tensioning**
Force equalization is achieved through global diagonal tensegrity lines rather than local limb control:

* **Posterior Oblique Sling:** Gluteus maximus couples with the contralateral latissimus dorsi across the thoracolumbar fascia, translating push-off power on one side into rotational deceleration of the opposite shoulder and lumbar spine (Vleeming & Stoeckart, 2007).
* **Helical & Spiral Lines:** The spiral fascial tracts (Myers, 2001) loop around the torso and cross under the arches of the feet. A lateral imbalance in one ankle or knee immediately alters the tensional baseline across the entire torso, prompting the contralateral leg to adjust its compliance and landing trajectory.

**The Upstream "Vestibular Tax"**
The primary top-level boundary condition of human locomotion is maintaining the craniocervical junction and visual-vestibular horizontal axis (Gracovetsky, 1988). If an upstream rotation exists (e.g., scoliosis, rib cage shear, or cranial tilt), the biotensegrity continuum forces the lower extremities to act as adaptive shock absorbers. The legs will deliberately sacrifice bilateral symmetry to maintain cephalic equilibrium.

**Dynamic Equilibrium Over Static Symmetry**
Under an operational heuristic, the system does not seek identical 50/50 bilateral load bearing. Instead, it seeks **energetic hysteresis and phase equilibrium**—using the elastic recoil of the aponeuroses and tendons to dissipate asymmetric shock waves through the whole continuum rather than allowing localized tissue overload.
