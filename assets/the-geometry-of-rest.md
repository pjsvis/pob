**3. Telemetry, Pandiculation, and the Art of Slow Movement**

Slow movement increases the bit-depth of our sensory telemetry. It allows the CNS to detect "discontinuities"—localized areas where the physical tissue does not match the CNS's internal predictive map. These discontinuities manifest as "Yellow" signals: ambiguous aches that say, *test this area, slowly.* By approaching and retreating from these areas with slow, deliberate movement, the CNS gathers the high-resolution data required to update the body schema and dissolve the localized tension.
