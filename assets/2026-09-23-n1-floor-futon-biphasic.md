# n=1 field report: the floor–futon biphasic observation

*Recorded 2026-09-23, after ~3 weeks of floor work. Relevant to `td-fdcbc2` (floor-lying tensor analysis) and the futon-specs series. An n=1 — read as one observer's trajectory, not a claim.*

## The observation

Three data points, in order:

1. Basic futon tried; floor felt better for doing-nothing and pandiculation. Futon shelved.
2. Retried a few nights later — same verdict.
3. Today: floor felt "too much"; futon hauled back out. In the mid-morning session the futon's larger contact surface proved *more* useful for pandiculation than the floor had.

## The interpretation on record

The floor's rigid, small-patch contact treats the high-amplitude peaks of strain — after weeks of floor work the peaks are gone, and the residual strain field is diffuse and low-amplitude ("rolling foothills"). Point contact now delivers discomfort without information; distributed contact sweeps the diffuse field.

## Why the corpus should care

The futon specs framed the futon as *transitional scaffolding* — an off-ramp on the way from bed to floor, to be left behind. This data suggests the opposite shape: not a ladder but a **toolkit**. Floor = acute/targeted instrument for high-amplitude localized strain. Futon = maintenance instrument for low-amplitude diffuse strain. Instrument choice follows the field being treated — the contact-pressure distribution should track the residual strain distribution, not decline monotonically toward the hard floor.

## The confound, held open

"Peaks removed" is the flattering reading. The alternative: the peaks were the *treatment*, and the futon is comfort-seeking that parks the remaining adaptation on the easy instrument. Falsifiable check: a weekly floor session as control. If the foothills are genuinely flattening, the floor's "too much" should progressively soften (broad contact tolerance rising). If "too much" intensifies or the diffuse aches persist indefinitely on the futon, the adaptation has stalled. **The floor stays the benchmark; the futon is a working surface, not a destination.**
