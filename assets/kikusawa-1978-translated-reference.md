## English abstract (verbatim, from J-STAGE)

Japanese official statistics show that the physique of children in Japan has improved remarkably since the end of the second World War. It has been pointed out that this is due to changes in diet, especially the increase in animal protein. However, it can not be ignored that the environmental factors such as living conditions in the home have also a great influence on the improvement of physique from the standpoint of human ecology.

This investigation reports the influence of the Western life style such as the use of chairs and western type beds on the physique.

Using the data obtained by questionnaires distributed to 793 subjects who were students in women's junior colleges in Hiroshima and Osaka cities, 108 subjects having used a western style bed for at least twelve years were designated as the bed group, whereas 108 subjects having used only Japanese bed mat were designated as the futon group.

Among the members of each group, then, 12 subjects who used chairs while eating meals, studying or spending time in the living room for at least eight years were designated as the chair using group, whereas 12 subjects with no experience using chairs were designated as the floor sitting group.

Photographs of front and side view of the subjects in the above groups were taken with a camera called "silhouetter." Using these photographs, the lengths and angles of the body were measured and analysed.

The results were as follows:
1. Statistically significant differences were not found between the bed group and the futon group regarding such values as average height, breadth, depth, girth, depth/breadth index, etc.
2. E-angle and H-angle of the futon group were larger than the ones of the bed group and differed significantly.
3. M-angle, upper body reclined angle and B-angle of the bed group were larger than the ones of the futon group and differed significantly.
4. The height of pillows had no relation with the neck-reclined angle.
5. There was no significant difference between the chair using group and the floor sitting group concerning height, girth, depth/breadth index and various angles of the side body view.
6. Curvatures of the back of the upper part of the body of the bed group and the chair sitting group were larger than the ones of the futon group and the floor sitting group, respectively.

## Method (translated and condensed from the Japanese body)

A questionnaire on bed and chair use-history was distributed in 1976 (Jul–Sep) to 793 women's junior-college students in Osaka (340) and Hiroshima (453); 773 were returned (97.5%). Two cohorts were carved out:

- **Bed group (ベッド生活群):** 108 students who had used a Western-style bed (spring mattress or ≥10 cm urethane-foam mattress, with or without a bed frame) for **at least the past 12 years** (i.e. since before age ~8).
- **Futon group (フトン生活群):** 108 students with **no bed-use history at all**, drawn from the 289 purely futon-using respondents, matched.

Within each cohort, a chair-vs-floor-sitting sub-comparison was made:

- **Chair-using group (イス座生活群):** 12 students who used chairs for eating, studying, and living-room time for **at least the past 8 years** (drawn from both the bed and futon cohorts — 24 total).
- **Floor-sitting group (ユカ座生活群):** 12 students with no chair-use experience. (Because purely chair-free studying was rare (~3%), the criterion was relaxed to chair-free eating and living-room time, not studying.)

**Measurement.** Each subject was photographed in front and side view with a "silhouetter" camera (1/10-scale silhouette photograph), wearing a slip and a thin tricot leotard, with adhesive markers on the tragion, cervicale, jugular notch, acromion, and a needle rod on the head; standing in the ear-eye horizontal, heels together, feet 60° apart, arms down. Lengths, breadths, depths, girths, and 18 lateral-view body angles were measured from the silhouettes.

**Angle definitions (from the Japanese body).** The side-view angles denoted J, M, A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I in Fig. 3 of the paper, plus derived neck-, upper-body-, and lower-body-recline angles. The ones that come up in the significant findings are:

- **M-angle (あご元角, "chin-base angle"):** at the chin/jaw base.
- **B-angle (肩胛骨下角, "inferior scapular angle"):** at the inferior angle of the scapula.
- **E-angle (乳頭点下角, "below-nipple angle"):** at the nipple, lower component.
- **H-angle (b点角):** at the intersection of the abdomen-front-point–thigh-front-point line with the horizontal through the buttock-back-point and the trunk-front-point.
- **Upper-body reclined angle (上体傾斜角 / 上体傾斜角):** the backward lean of the upper body from vertical.

## Results — the load-bearing findings (translated)

The body's results section gives the means and χ² tests for the 18 angles, separated by sleep-surface (bed vs futon) and by sitting-style (chair vs floor). The significant discriminations (with the direction):

- **E-angle and H-angle:** futon > bed (significant; E at p<0.01, H at p<0.05). The futon group's lower-body and abdominal configuration is more forward/open.
- **M-angle, upper-body reclined angle, and B-angle:** bed > futon (significant; M and upper-body at p<0.01, B at p<0.05). **The bed group's upper body is more reclined (leans further back), and the inferior-scapular / chin-base angles are larger.**
- **Pillow height:** no relation to neck-recline angle (finding 4 of the abstract) — i.e. the postural deviation is not a pillow artifact.
- **Chair vs floor-sitting:** no significant difference on heights, girths, depth/breadth index, or the lateral-view angles (finding 5). **Only finding 6 — back curvature of the upper body — separates chair from floor sitting**, and it does so in the same direction as the bed-vs-futon split (chair/bed more curved than floor/futon).

## Discussion — the Staffel-classification passage (translated)

The discussion interprets the bed-group's angle pattern in terms of **Staffel's 19th-century posture classification** (Staffel's 彎曲 / round-back type). Paraphrased translation of the key passage:

> Dividing the body into three blocks — neck, upper body, lower body — and comparing the bed and futon groups: in the neck, although the neck-recline angle (about the tragion) does not differ, the M-, J- and Q-angles are larger in the bed group, so the neck is neither tilted forward nor back but the **whole head is translated forward, the chin jutting out** — this is the bed group's characteristic cervical posture. In the upper body, the bed group's G- and I-angles are larger, so the **upper-body reclined angle is also larger, the upper body tilts backward**; and because the B-angle is larger while the A- and E-angles are smaller, **the back of the trunk shows greater posterior protrusion of the scapular region relative to the trunk-back-point, and the front shows less anterior protrusion of the nipple relative to the trunk-front-point — the so-called Staffel round-back type (円背型)**. In the lower body, the bed group's F-angle is larger and H-angle is shifted toward the negative — the upper body reclines and the abdomen protrudes, so to balance in standing the lower limbs tilt backward.

> The curvature S of the back (shoulder-blade point to buttock point) is **25.5 in the bed group vs 20.8 in the futon group** — the bed group's back is more curved. Overall, the bed group's posture is: head thrust forward, upper body reclined, abdomen forward, buttocks back, more pronounced than the futon group. **This corroborates Ohara's ergonomic finding that the sleeping posture on a soft bed takes the W-shape.**

(Ohara is cited in the Japanese body as the source of the W-shape / soft-bed sleeping-posture observation; this is the corroboration we pair Kikusawa with.)

The conclusion paragraph states: the sleeping-style difference shows up mainly in the upper-body reclined angle; the bed group tends toward the **enpai (round-back / kyphotic) type**; the chair-vs-floor-sitting comparison shows the same directional tendency (chair-sitters more curved than floor-sitters), though only on the curvature measure.

## Why this paper matters to the floor-lying thesis

This is a **longitudinal cohort that isolates the sleeping-surface variable** (12+ years of bed vs futon, matched) and finds the postural deviation the tensor formalism predicts the compliant surface produces — kyphotic/round-back tendency, W-shape, greater upper-body backward lean — corroborated by the Ohara bedding-ergonomics line. It is almost certainly not in the English biomechanics citation graph (a 1978 Japanese hygiene journal), and it is the **cleanest single surface-isolated signal** for the cultural-shift claim. The honest framing for the book: the surface predicts *posture* cleanly (this paper); *pain* is downstream of posture and forty other things, and the CUPID Japan data cut against the simple story until the confounds are named.

## Translation honesty notes

- This is a project translation, not a professional one. The angle definitions and the Staffel-classification passage are the load-bearing parts and have been checked against the figure captions and the results table; the surrounding interpretive prose is paraphrased.
- The "silhouetter" (シルエッター) is a commercial 1/10-scale silhouette camera used in Japanese apparel-pattern-drafting research of the period; the method is described in the body but not in the abstract.
- Finding 5 (chair vs floor-sitting null on angles) is weaker than the abstract implies — only finding 6 (curvature) separates them. The study is cleaner on the sleeping-surface axis than the sitting axis.
- The paper does not report pain, only posture. The posture→pain inference is the book's, not Kikusawa's.
