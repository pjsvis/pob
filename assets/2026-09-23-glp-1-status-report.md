# GLP-1: Status Report No. 1

*A snapshot of the GLP-1 situation as of 2026-09-23, intended for update as it develops. Primary source: NYT/Dani Blum on the Orandi et al. study in* Obesity *(`2026-09-23-glp-1-nyt.md`, incl. 141 reader letters). Not a publication — a share-out with opinion attached. The premise on record: Peter suspects this becomes a rolling disaster. This report tests that suspicion rather than adopting it.*

---

## The situation in one paragraph

A records analysis of 92M US adults found 1.1M GLP-1 users with no coded qualifying diagnosis, growing 15-fold between 2021 and 2025 — an undercount, since telehealth and compounded supply are invisible to it. More than one in three of the unqualified cohort has a normal BMI, and the cohort carries elevated rates of documented eating disorders. The headline said "no medical reason"; what the study measured was *absence of an eligibility code*. Those are different things, and the gap between them is where the whole situation lives.

## The three readings of "off-label"

1. **Coding artefact.** Two-thirds of the off-label cohort was overweight, not normal-weight — and "overweight" is a medical reason under most clinical judgment, just not under the FDA label. Maintenance dosing after successful weight loss looks like "no medical need" to a database and is the medically indicated use to a clinician (the Jackie Greaney case). Expect this to be the majority of the 1.1M.
2. **Threshold-induced.** Insurance gates push patients to acquire the qualifying disease before affordable treatment arrives (denied as prediabetic, fully covered as diabetic). The off-label population is partly manufactured by the eligibility rules themselves.
3. **Genuinely unindicated.** Normal BMI, no comorbidity, seeking the drug for weight or for the buzzy frontier benefits (alcohol, food noise, inflammation). This is the fraction the headline implies is the whole — and it contains the real alarm: the eating-disorder overlap.

The policy conversation collapses these three into one number and one moral. Every reader letter that begins "I'm one of those people" is evidence against the collapse.

## What the letters know that the study doesn't

The comment section is a folk census of a natural experiment, and two clusters are hypothesis-generating in the formal sense:

- **Alcohol suppression** — reported independently by at least a dozen letter-writers, including two describing third parties (a problem drinker with a prior DUI, an 82-year-old with CHF). The trials are in early stages; the population is running ahead of them.
- **"Food noise" relief** — reported as the primary benefit by several, with weight loss incidental to it. This reframes the drug's mechanism of subjective value for a population nobody designed it for.

Both clusters are anecdotes, worth nothing individually. The clustering is signal. Nobody is collecting it.

Against that: AM's letter — a 25-year-old with a decade-long eating disorder, normal BMI, prescription obtained in a single visit, "nothing compares to being skinny." That letter is the study's worst finding made flesh, and it required no statistics to write.

## Opinion: is this a rolling disaster?

**Half agree. The disaster is real, but it isn't pharmacological — it's institutional.** Three claims, in ascending confidence:

1. **The drug is probably better than the discourse.** Twenty years of data exist for the diabetic population; the letters' benefit clusters keep replicating across independent reporters. A pharmacological calamity of opioid scale would need a mechanism nobody has proposed. Hold this weakly — the long-term data in *non-obese* populations genuinely doesn't exist, and Krüger's point stands: malnutrition and muscle-mass loss at low starting BMI are real, unresearched risks.
2. **The experiment is uncontrolled in both directions.** Millions are generating benefit signals no trial will confirm for a decade, while a vulnerable minority self-doses toward malnutrition with no adverse-event collection attached. Signal and harm are both evaporating as comment-section anecdotes. A rolling epistemic disaster: the data needed to govern this is being produced and discarded continuously.
3. **The allocation system is the disaster already here.** Coverage withdrawn upon success (Jennifer: kicked off Mounjaro the moment her A1c improved). The genuinely obese priced out ($700/month on Medicare) while the merely curious buy compounded tirzepatide for $149. The actuarial logic is rational at every node — insurers expect you on a competitor's plan by the time prevention pays off — and the aggregate is a machine that punishes exactly the behaviour it claims to want. Systems over villains: no actor in this story is doing anything but responding sensibly to the incentive structure in front of them. That's what makes it durable.

So: not a chip pan fire, but not a pizza shop either. A well-run uncontrolled trial with a broken billing department.

## Expanded: the insurance question — system failure or misapplied instrument?

The churn argument, formalised: prevention is a **capital investment** with a decade payback. The US health insurance contract is an **annual lease** with an expected tenancy of two to four years (job change, plan change, state change). No pool rationally finances an asset whose returns accrue to a competitor. The actuary declining your Zepbound is not the villain; the incentive is structural and the actuary is obeying it correctly. Systems over villains, and this system is working exactly as built.

The verdict: **misapplication, not system failure — with the boundary drawn precisely.**

- What insurance *is*: pooling exogenous risk across a population at a point in time. What GLP-1 maintenance dosing *is*: a certain, recurring cost — a subscription. A pool that finances a certainty isn't insurance any more; it's a savings plan, and pools correctly refuse to become savings plans. This refusal is a feature of the instrument, not a defect.
- What the US structure lacks is any mechanism to pool **across time**. Three demonstrated fixes exist: (1) **risk equalisation** — transfer payments between insurers so the pool that prevented is compensated when the patient lands elsewhere (Netherlands, Germany, Australia run versions of this); (2) a **lifelong residual claimant** — a single payer owns your decade, so prevention pays (the NHS logic); (3) the **integrated payer-provider** — when the insurer is the provider, prevention pays (the Kaiser structure). The US has none at scale, because its structure was never designed: employer-linked insurance is a WWII wage-control artifact that accreted into the default.
- So the correct diagnosis is category error, not broken machinery. Annual-priced, churn-normalised insurance is a one-year instrument; GLP-1 prevention is a thirty-year problem. Asking the bet pool to be the pension fund fails exactly the way the letters show: coverage denied on entry (Steven, acquiring the qualifying disease), withdrawn on success (Jennifer, ejected the moment her A1c improved).

**The second-order game.** Denial feeds the grey market; the grey market removes its users from every measurement system — no records, no codes, no adverse-event capture. Insurance blindness and pharmacovigilance blindness are not two failures; they are one mechanism, upstream and downstream. Which leads to the next section.

## The unattributed-harm hypothesis: down-regulated bio-tensegrity

The formal claim: musculoskeletal tissue is load-maintained. Bone, tendon, and muscle are a prestressed structure whose maintenance budget is set by demand — Wolff's law, and everything the book is about. Pharmacological weight loss removes both the mass being carried and (through appetite suppression) much of the loading behaviour that drove maintenance. Documented lean-mass loss runs to a quarter or more of total weight lost in the trials. In normal-BMI users the reserve is thin to begin with: the structure down-regulates to match reduced demand. The tensegrity network thins. That is not a side effect; that is the expected adaptation of a load-following structure to a reduced load — the system doing precisely what it evolved to do.

**Why it will be unattributed.** Pharmacovigilance is event-based. Bowel obstruction gets coded and counted (Floyd Hall's letter, and his 13 recommends). Sarcopenia at seventy does not. The harm is a *trajectory divergence*, not an event: it presents as "aging," arrives years after the prescription, is confounded by everything, and lands on a population that is invisible to records — compounded product, telehealth prescribers, off-registry. Jeff's letter again: "my doctor didn't ask how I lost the weight." Nobody is holding the other end of that causal chain, and by design of the system, nobody will be.

**The demographic stacking.** The letters' largest single cluster is post-menopausal women — the population already running a structural maintenance deficit, since estrogen withdrawal unwinds the bone-maintenance budget exactly when lean-mass reserves are lowest. Stacking pharmacological weight loss on top of that is spending the principal. The population most represented in the anecdote stream is the population with the least structural reserve to spend.

**The known-but-undelivered antidote.** Resistance loading plus protein intake alongside the drug is the standard mitigation for lean-mass loss. The intervention exists; the delivery does not. Nobody tells the 65-year-old microdoser to lift heavy things — the telehealth questionnaire does not ask, and the compounding pharmacy does not coach. If the disaster rolls, it is a disaster of omission: a known countermeasure, undelivered, to a predictable adaptation, unmeasured.

**The synthesis.** The same horizon mismatch, three times over. Insurance prices annually. Pharmacovigilance codes events. Eligibility rules measure BMI points. GLP-1's real costs and benefits are decadal and trajectory-shaped. A rolling disaster of this shape never arrives as an event — it arrives as a statistic: a frailty curve bending upward in a population nobody follows, discoverable only in retrospect, by which time it will have been renamed "aging."

## Watchlist for Report No. 2

The falsifiable indicators, so the next update measures instead of narrates:

- **Eating-disorder cohort size.** Does the documented overlap grow, stay flat, or get absorbed into better screening at the telehealth gates?
- **FDA label expansion.** Alcohol use disorder and other frontier indications — each approval converts an off-label population into a labelled one and shrinks the "no medical reason" number without changing a single patient.
- **Compounding loophole.** Post-approval, does compounded supply close (as it did with semaglutide precedent) or persist as the grey channel?
- **Insurance reaction.** Do payers respond to the 15-fold growth with broader coverage (converting the coding artefact into covered care) or harder gates (enlarging the grey market)?
- **The uncontrolled-trial gap.** Does anyone actually start collecting adverse-event and benefit data from the off-label population — a registry, anything? If Report No. 2 is written and this is still "no," the epistemic disaster claim is confirmed and the pharmacological one remains unfalsifiable.
- **Long-term outcome data in normal-BMI users.** The one number that could flip claim 1.
- **The frailty curve.** Any longitudinal lean-mass / BMD measurement in non-obese GLP-1 users, anywhere? If Report No. 2 finds none, the unattributed-harm hypothesis stands untested — not refuted.
- **Payer structure.** Movement toward risk-equalisation transfer payments, Medicare price negotiation (the ga letter's ask), or integrated delivery — any structural repair to the horizon mismatch.
