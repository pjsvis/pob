## 1. Origin

A sustained line of enquiry developed across a single session, beginning from a proposition about
the historical "second sleep" and ending in a global formulation of what floor time does. The
material has four distinct parts, which should not be conflated:

1. **The historical proposition** — that the documented pre-industrial mid-night waking is a
   biological event rather than a cultural artefact of pre-industrial lighting.
2. **The mechanism** — floor lying removes postural demand; the activation vector falls; creep runs;
   the reference configuration is restored against a global datum.
3. **The channel** — the subcortical system reports to the cortical system as *ache*, and the ache
   is a request for a boundary-condition change.
4. **The practice** — the enabling conditions (warm, safe, comfy) are the intervention; the
   cortical contribution is to refrain.

Parts 1–3 are hypotheses with falsifiers (§10). Part 4 is an operational claim whose status is
different in kind and is treated separately in §13.

## 2. The hypothesis as proposed

Stated in the form given, before assessment:

- The historical second sleep describes people waking in the middle of the night and reading,
  talking, or otherwise occupying themselves before returning to sleep.
- **First sleep** = the bio-tensegrity system lying flat on the ground, using the ground as a
  reference plane, while the sub-cortical movement systems untangle the excessive strains
  introduced during the day.
- **On completion**, the sub-cortical system wakes the sleeper for a vertical orientation
  calibration — movement around, upright, for a period.
- **Second sleep** = the sleeper returns to sleep and dreams.
- Later extensions: a second sub-cortical signal is a requirement for **predictable nutrition**;
  food becomes fuel for fascial regeneration; **side-lying induces creep in the fascia**; the system
  spends its floor time, *if allowed to*, **restoring the pre-stress environment of the overall
  structure**.
- Floor time is available **during the day** as well as at night.

## 3. What the literature actually says

### 3.1 The historical register — and its 2023 critique

**The popular version.** Ekirch (2001, *American Historical Review*, "Sleep We Have Lost"; 2005,
*At Day's Close: Night in Times Past*) argues that pre-industrial Europeans slept in two segments —
a "first" and "second" sleep — separated by up to an hour or more of quiet wakefulness during which
people rose to urinate, smoke, visit neighbours, make love, pray, and reflect on dreams. He further
claims consolidated sleep is "unnatural" and that modern middle-of-the-night insomnia is a throwback.

**The critique.** Boyce (2023, *Medical History* 67(2):91–108, doi:10.1017/mdh.2023.14) re-reads the
sources and substantially weakens the strong version:

- **"First sleep" most often meant the *deep* sleep, not a first *segment*.** Joseph Caryl (1656):
  "the first sleepe is the deepe sleepe." Depth, not segmentation. Crichton-Browne (1891) uses
  "first sleep" for ~90 minutes of deep sleep before midnight. This is the most damaging re-reading.
- **EEBO yields only 15 hits for "second sleep"**, of which only 5 contain both terms, and 2 are
  metaphorical (the "Second Sleep" of death, a "spirituall sleep").
- **The waking was frequently framed as troublesome**, not routine — prayers headed "if you happen to
  awake" and "when you awake, or cannot sleep," filed under emergency ejaculations.
- **Ekirch's own qualification:** "Most people, upon awakening, probably never left their beds."
- **Wehr's protocol was not a natural night** (see §3.2): 16 subjects (15 male, 1 female), confined
  alone in a windowless dark room, activity forbidden; one subject became severely depressed and
  suicidal and was withdrawn.
- **Crichton-Browne's incomplete quotation** and **Gombauld's *Endimion*** (interesting dreams occur
  in the *second* sleep, contradicting Ekirch's first-sleep-dreams claim).
- **Modern prevalence undercuts the "we lost it" framing:** a 2010 European survey found 31.2% of
  the general population wake at least three nights per week and 7.7% have difficulty resuming.
  Broken sleep is highly prevalent *now*.
- **Boyce's conclusion:** segmented sleep occurred, but was not the sole or necessarily predominant
  model; the terms held multiple meanings; ambiguous sources are not made less ambiguous by
  collation.

**Consequence for the hypothesis.** The historical register cannot carry the claim that people
"used to sleep in two segments." What it *can* support is narrower and still useful: a mid-night
waking with a distinctive character is **widely and independently reported** across a large,
pre-theoretical corpus. That is a behavioural regularity, not a sleep-architecture claim.

**The sleeping surface is a live variable, and it is heterogeneous.** Pre-industrial beds were not
compliant mattresses. The foundational surface is the **straw tick** (*paillasse* / *palliasse*) on a
rope or board frame — firm, nonuniform, re-stuffed seasonally. The **feather tick** is the compliant
end and became common in the 19th century. Ekirch's corpus therefore spans the compliance axis that
matters (§8), which converts Boyce's "the corpus is ambiguous" into a *structured prediction* — see
§10, rung 11.

### 3.2 The photoperiod experiments: Wehr and Wright

**Wehr (1992), "In short photoperiods, human sleep is biphasic," *J. Sleep Res.* 1(2):103–107.**
Normal individuals transferred from a conventional 16-h photoperiod to an experimental **10-h
photoperiod** (i.e. **14 h of darkness**). Sleep episodes expanded and "usually divided into two
symmetrical bouts, several hours in duration, with a **1–3 h waking interval** between them." The
durations of nocturnal melatonin secretion and of the nocturnal phase of rising sleepiness also
expanded.

*Wehr's own proposed mechanisms:* dual oscillators entrained separately to dawn and dusk, or a
two-process model with a **lowered threshold for sleep-onset during the scotoperiod**. Both are
**environmental-timing** mechanisms, not homeostatic-completion mechanisms. This matters: the
strongest experimental support for a biphasic pattern also supplies the null hypothesis against the
"completion signal" reading.

**Wright et al. (2013), "Entrainment of the Human Circadian Clock to the Natural Light-Dark Cycle,"
*Curr. Biol.* 23(16):1554–1558.** Eight participants (two female), aged 30.3 ± 8.5, camping in tents
for approximately one week with only natural light (sunlight and camp fires; no flashlights, no
personal electronic devices). Light-dark cycle: **14 h 40 min light : 9 h 20 min dark**.

Measured sleep outcomes:

| Measure | Electrical light | Natural light | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly sleep duration | 6.7 ± 0.9 h | 6.8 ± 0.7 h | 0.268 |
| Sleep efficiency | 87.6 ± 5.6% | 87.0 ± 4.6% | 0.49 |

**This is not a replication failure of Wehr, and Boyce's use of it overstates the counter.** Three
reasons: (i) the scotoperiod differed by **4 h 40 min** (9 h 20 min vs 14 h), and the scotoperiod is
the driver in Wehr's own model; (ii) the subjects slept 6.8 h — a short, *consolidated* night, not a
sleep-satiated one; (iii) sleep *structure* (fragmentation, mid-night waking) was **not analysed** —
duration and efficiency were the reported outcomes, so this is an **unreported outcome, not a tested
null**. The paper is a circadian-entrainment study; sleep was a secondary measure.

**The integration this permits.** The photoperiod sets the **window**; the strain sets the **use**.
Wehr's 14 h dark created the opportunity and the 1–3 h waking used it; Wright's 9 h 20 min dark left
no spare dark time, so the pattern could not express. This makes the claim **conditional** rather
than universal — a native capacity that expresses when the conditions allow — which is the same shape
as the floor claim, and it survives both studies.

### 3.3 Sleep architecture: the two blocks are a gradient

SWS (N3) dominates the first third of the night; approximately **70–80% of nightly growth hormone
secretion occurs in the first SWS episode**; REM dominates the last third. The mid-night waking falls
near the block boundary.

**Read carefully:** this supports the *assignment* (first block = the body's structural block;
second = the REM/consolidation block) but **not a clean dichotomy** — both blocks contain both
stages, with a gradient across the night. Over-clean dichotomies are the failure mode the tensor
asset's §9 warns against ("compulsive narrative syndrome"; elegance is a warning sign).

### 3.4 Sensorimotor maintenance during sleep

**Blumberg, Myroth, Horton et al. (2026), "REM-sleep twitching in adults and the maintenance of
specialized sensorimotor systems," *Current Biology*, doi:10.1016/j.cub.2026.02.048.** Twitching is
an abundant infant behaviour and a defining feature of REM sleep; it supports development of the
sensorimotor system. It **persists into adulthood** for unknown reasons. Whereas infantile twitches
are distributed across all body parts, adult twitching occurs more selectively in appendages used for
**active sensing** — elephant trunks, rat whiskers, pig snouts, raccoon hands — which also have
disproportionately large sensorimotor cortical representations (**cortical magnification**). The
authors propose that active sensing and neural magnification explain the persistence, as a
subcategory of "a broader need to maintain and update vital sensorimotor functions," and frame it as
supporting "the development, **calibration, maintenance, and repair** of neuroplastic systems across
the animal kingdom."

*This is the direct literature support for the mechanism class the hypothesis proposes* — the
sleeping nervous system runs motor programmes for calibration and maintenance. **The limit is real:**
the evidence concerns specialised active-sensing appendages, not whole-body postural strain.

**Brooks & Peever (2016), "A Temporally Controlled Inhibitory Drive Coordinates Twitch Movements
during REM Sleep," *Curr. Biol.* 26(7).** REM twitches are **not** random byproducts of paralysis
lapse. In adult rats they follow a defined temporal trajectory — largely absent during REM initiation,
increasing steadily, **peaking toward REM termination** — controlled by a GABA and glycine drive onto
motoneurons that weakens progressively. Twitches are coherently generated events.

### 3.5 Posture has a physiological function: the glymphatic result

**Lee et al. (2015), "The Effect of Body Posture on Brain Glymphatic Transport," *J. Neurosci.*
35(31):11034–11044.** Lateral position showed significantly higher clearance efficiency than supine
or prone. The authors propose that "the most popular sleep posture (lateral) has evolved to optimize
waste removal during sleep and that **posture must matter**."

**Why this is load-bearing for the present material.** It is a *published* claim, in a strong
journal, that sleeping **posture** has a physiological function — and it points in the **lateral**
direction, converging with the Buddhist lion posture (right side), the Ayurvedic left-side
recommendation, and the ape posture data (~65% side-lying; Samson & Shumaker 2013: dorsal 20.5%,
ventral 8.6%, right 31.5%, left 33.2%, vertical 6.2%).

**The hypothesis in §8 is the somatic analogue of this claim.** Lee et al.: sleeping posture
optimises *brain* waste clearance, with a lateral optimum. The proposal here: sleeping posture
optimises *somatic* strain resolution. Same structural claim, different tissue — and the somatic
version has no literature at all.

### 3.6 The measured thinness of the sleep-*posture* biomechanics register

The gap is measured, not asserted:

- **Cheng et al. (2023), "Computational Biomechanics of Sleep: A Systematic Mapping Review,"
  *Bioengineering* 10:917.** Maps 27 finite-element sleep studies: 19 on obstructive sleep apnea,
  3 on sleep bruxism, 5 on sleep ergonomics (pillow + mattress). **Every ergonomics study modelled
  supine only.** The review states outright: "there was no research exploring side-lying positions in
  this review, and all evaluated supine posture."
- **Cary et al. (2019), *BMJ Open*, scoping review on sleep posture and spinal symptoms.** Screened
  4186 articles, identified **four**; all used self-reported posture (inaccuracy up to 33%); the
  authors conclude "there were not enough high-quality studies to adequately answer our research
  question."
- **Saini et al. (2025),** systematic review of posture versus low back pain: six observational
  studies, crude findings.

**The absence is produced by funding topology** (sleep medicine funds brain and breathing; the
bedding industry funds mattress comfort and pressure relief; nobody funds "does floor sleeping
remodel your hip capsule"), **disciplinary boundary** (no department owns resting-posture
biomechanics), and **methodological difficulty** (longitudinal in-vivo tissue remodelling under
sustained load).

**The traditional-practice register is not thin — only the biomechanics register is.** The gap is a
*translation failure between registers*, not a recording failure. The traditions recorded
sleeping-surface and sleeping-position as health-relevant variables two millennia ago; the
biomechanics field did not ask them.

### 3.7 Ontogeny: the developmental default

**The observation.** The spontaneous load-seeking behaviour — draping the body over furniture as a
frame, sleeping with an arm in the air, legs tangled — is far more prevalent in children and young
people than in adults, thinning out around 23. And floor work in an adult reproduces the same class
of postures.

**The inference.** If the mechanism is (i) a rigid datum exposes structural deviation, (ii) low-load
creep remodels the reference configuration, and (iii) growth requires continuous remodelling of that
configuration, then the **demand** for remodelling is highest during growth, and the behaviours that
produce it should be most prevalent in the growing. That is what ordinary observation reports. The
prediction is confirmed by common observation rather than by measurement — but it is a prediction the
framework makes, not a description fitted to it.

**This inverts the usual reading — permitted versus driven.** The conventional account is the
*elasticity* account: children are rubbery, so they can get into odd positions; adults stiffen, so
they cannot. On that account the postures are a *by-product* of flexibility — undirected, incidental.
The remodelling account makes them a **behaviour with a function** — directed and sustained. The
discriminating features: on the remodelling account the behaviour should be aimed at **rigid edges**
(sofa arms, doorframes, railings — frames, not cushions) and **held**, since creep needs duration. On
the elasticity account it should be undirected and brief. The reported observation is the directed,
sustained version.

**Why it thins out — two candidate drivers.** The boundary near 23 does not map onto a single
milestone: epiphyseal fusion is typically 14–18 (female) / 16–20 (male); peak bone mass ~25–30; CNS
maturation ~25. But GH and IGF-1 secretion peaks in adolescence and falls substantially thereafter —
and GH/IGF-1 is the *anabolic* driver of matrix synthesis, i.e. the substrate-gated consolidation
step in §4. **So the behaviour may thin because the consolidation capacity falls, not because the
growth demand stops.** The two are correlated but not identical, and they make different predictions
(§10, rung 13).

**Suppression versus absence.** Adults do not stop doing this; they do it in private and when
licensed. The adult forms are "stretching," "mobility work," "yoga," the post-work sprawl — the same
behaviour under a practice frame. So the thinning is better read as **suppression** than as absence,
which is the book's recurring move (the capacity is native; the environment suppresses it). It
parallels the pandiculation→Tai Chi codification in §12: **spontaneous somatic behaviour is either
suppressed by the environment or codified as a practice.** Two instances of that pattern now exist,
which is the threshold at which this project formalises.

**The ontogeny thread this resolves.** Earlier sessions flagged the ontogeny line as open: infant
apes and infant humans both sleep curled; supine sprawl is learned/cultural; McKenna's
breastsleeping. This material generalises it — the developmental default is not only the *curled sleep
posture* but **active load-seeking during the waking hours**, and both are attenuated by age and
socialisation. So "supine sprawl is learned" extends to **"postural passivity is learned."** On this
reading the traditions are less ancient wisdom than **conservation mechanisms for the developmental
default** — which is why they are transmitted (they conserve what socialisation would otherwise
remove), and it connects directly to the teacher-as-external-datum point in §9.3.

**Honest caveats.** (i) The age gradient is partly an *observation* gradient — children are observed
more, have more unstructured time and fewer social constraints; the same sampling-frame problem as
§3.1. (ii) "We see this more in children" is informal observation, not data — though it is *tractable*
data (there is a real literature on children's movement variety and postural behaviour). (iii) Do not
overclaim that children *need* it: the honest form is that the behaviour is **consistent with** a
remodelling demand that is highest during growth, not that it is proven to serve one.

**A new readout this produces.** **Postural variety during floor time is a cheap proxy for map
quality.** A clamped system settles into one or two positions and holds them; a well-mapped system
ranges widely with brief holds and exploration. Prediction: variety **increases** over months of
practice — the opposite of the naive expectation that a settled system moves less. This is consistent
with §7's gate formulation rather than in tension with it: the *settling* is **a**↓, and the *variety*
is the search that low **a** enables (pandiculation only happens when **a** is already low).

**And it answers the objection in §13.** The do-nothing instruction forbids *cortical* initiation,
not subcortical movement. **The weird postures are movement the practitioner did not initiate — which
is evidence that the gate is working, not that it has failed.** It is the same signature as "observed
but not initiated" in §9.2, and it is the second independent instance of that signature in the record.

## 4. The mechanism chain

```
Floor time  →  a falls  →  creep  →  matrix consolidation  →  prestress restored
              (control)    (minutes–hours)   (days–weeks, substrate-gated)
```

- **Floor time.** The rigid substrate is a Signorini contact; it removes postural demand and sensory
  novelty. The compliant substrate it replaces is a Winkler foundation (reaction ∝ local indentation)
  — it *accommodates* structural deviation rather than *exposing* it.
- **a falls.** The active-stress decomposition **σ** = **σ**_passive(**C**) + **σ**_active(**a**, **C**).
  **a** is not a mechanical variable; it is a neuro-control input set by the CNS. The floor drives
  d**a**/dt < 0. At each instant the body solves ∇·[**σ**_passive + **σ**_active(**a**(t))] + ρ**g** = 0
  against the rigid contact BC.
- **Creep.** Low-load, long-duration, monotonic, gravity-driven. The load-regime discrimination is
  Thornton's: low-stress creep progressively recruits fibres (remodelling, safe, asymptotic);
  high-stress creep damages fibres. The "feels right, not forced" signal is the afferent readout of
  which regime the load is in.
- **Consolidation.** Matrix synthesis and turnover — the slow, substrate-gated step (§6).

**Two timescales, and they must not be conflated.** Creep is minutes-to-hours and needs **no new
material** — it is rearrangement of existing matrix (water, proteoglycan, progressive fibre
recruitment). Consolidation is days-to-weeks and **is** substrate-gated.

**Day floor work is the control condition that prunes the hypothesis space.** If the benefits appear
in *daytime* floor work, then sleep architecture, MMC timing, sleep-stage sequencing and the
second-sleep structure are **not required** for them — the mechanical chain stands alone. If they
appear only at night, the sleep-specific mechanisms earn their place. This is the cleanest available
separator for the crowded hypothesis space in §10, and it is cheap. It also demotes the night from
"the thing" to **one instance of floor time**, which is the right structural level.

## 5. The ache-request loop

The proposed channel: the sub-cortical movement systems communicate with the cortical systems **via
aches**. The formulation forces an obvious question — *why report to a party that cannot act on it?*
The cortex cannot do the tensor analysis. The answer that makes the loop coherent: **the cortex is
the only subsystem that can change the boundary condition.** It can shift position; in the failure
case it can get vertical.

**Therefore the ache is a request, addressed to the only agent able to execute a repositioning — not
a symptom and not a warning.**

```
Ache  →  cortical reposition  →  does the search converge?
                                   ├─ yes → the comfy point (the body specifies the position)
                                   └─ no  → escalate: get vertical for a while
```

**Why this is an improvement on the original "completion signal."** A completion signal requires a
completion *sensor*, which does not exist. Search-escalation requires only a **failure to converge**
— a property of any search process. No new sensor. **The vertical-datum cycle becomes the escalation
when the horizontal local search is exhausted.**

**The tension it creates.** Failure-escalation predicts a *restless* pre-waking state (the
bed-insomnia feeling). The reported floor waking is *calm* — "time has passed but I was not
unconscious," no dozy edge, and getting vertical "not to do anything." Either the calm is the
*post*-escalation state, or the account is wrong. See §10, rung 5.

**The synthesis this produces — the unification.** Boyce's "troublesome" bedded waking and the calm
floor waking are **the two predicted outcomes of the same loop, split by surface compliance**. A
bedded population has a flattened gradient → diffuse ache field → unsatisfiable request → restless,
troublesome waking — which is precisely what the early modern prayers describe. A floor sleeper has
an intact gradient → localised ache → satisfiable request → converged comfy point → calm. This makes
the documentary corpus a **test of the mechanism** rather than a problem for it.

**The frontier qualifier.** "Lie on the ache" is the green-yellow frontier rule in the horizontal
frame. In the red zone (sustained signal) the medical expectation is correct and the point must
**not** be loaded. The same discrimination as the "feels right, not forced" criterion.

**The ache can also be a load-budget instruction** ("don't walk too far right now"), extending the
request formulation: the request can be *change position* **or** *reduce load*. Read as the governor
protecting the remodelling window. **Honest rival reading:** this is also what deconditioning in a
long-unused limb feels like. Discriminator in §10, rung 4.

## 6. Nutrition as a second sub-cortical channel

**The claim:** a requirement for **predictable** nutrition; food as fuel for fascial regeneration.

**The precision it needs.** The word doing the work is *predictable*, and it cashes as
**predictable fasting intervals** — not calorie timing. The migrating motor complex runs in the
**fasted** state, cycling at ~90–120 min, motilin-driven (Vantrappen et al. 1979; Takahashi 2012,
already cited in the tensor asset §7). Gastric phase III is **vagal-dependent**. So the requirement
is on the *gaps*: predictability is a **phase-locking requirement** for a biological oscillator, not
a preference. This is the traditional-practice register's content too (the Ayurvedic regular-meal
regimen; the monastic meal schedule), and the night is the longest fasted interval of the day.

**Where nutrition enters the chain — and where it does not.** Per §4: **the night's work is not
nutrition-gated; the retention of it is.** A floor night on poor substrate yields the mechanical
length change and then loses it. Same shape as the acute-versus-chronic stretch distinction in the
tissue literature. Collagen synthesis requires amino acids, vitamin C, iron, copper.

**Consequence for the enabling conditions.** §7's list (safe, warm, comfy) gates the **nightly**
process; nutrition gates the **consolidation** of it. The specification therefore gains a
slow-timescale member alongside the fast ones.

**A novel test this produces.** The MMC phase is predictable from the last meal; the night is the
fasted window; vagal afferents can trigger arousal. **Is the mid-night waking coupled to the MMC
phase?** If it tracks, that is a third mechanism for the waking and the one that makes "predictable
nutrition" load-bearing for the waking itself. Cheap-ish and genuinely new.

## 7. Side-lying as the creep-induction posture

Mechanically well-founded, and stronger than the form in which it was proposed:

- Side-lying presents the **largest contact area** of the three main postures, so load is distributed
  and **long-duration low-load** is achievable without pressure damage — which is the creep regime.
- Supine concentrates load on bony prominences (sacrum, occiput, heels) and is time-limited by tissue
  tolerance.
- Prone is universally discouraged — the traditions and the ape record agree.
- **The block converts isotropic creep into directed creep** along a specific line: a localised
  constraint at the knee fixing the top leg's geometry while gravity acting on the leg's mass
  produces a sustained, directed torque at the hip.

**Two things the claim must carry:**

1. **Alternation is entailed, not optional.** Creep is directional; side-lying is asymmetric; a
   single chirality induces one-sided creep. The §6 Poynting-by-analogy argument and the traditions'
   rotation (Buddhism right, Ayurveda left, the Yogaratnakara post-meal sequence supine→right→left→any)
   are the correction. **The claim requires the rotation.**
2. **It takes a side against received opinion.** The Alexander tradition explicitly cautions that
   side-lying "creates asymmetrical tension patterns" and is emphatically supine. The claim is
   against that tradition and should be stated as taking a side.

## 8. The prestress formulation

**This is the strongest and most novel claim in the material.** "Prestress" is the tensegrity term
for the self-equilibrated internal stress state that gives the structure its shape and stiffness —
i.e. it **is** the reference configuration **C**_0.

**It generalises the existing §6 result from the asymmetry to the whole configuration.** §6 currently
tracks ‖Δ**C**_0‖ (the left-minus-right reference-strain difference). The proposal is that what is
being restored is **C**_0 as a whole — its prestress *level*, not merely its symmetry.

**Why it is the right level for the floor-datum argument.** A **global** prestress can only be
restored against a **global** datum. A compliant substrate supplies a local,
deformation-history-dependent reference (the ditch), so it cannot restore a global prestress — it can
only accommodate local deviations. **The rigid floor is the only substrate that offers a global
datum.** That is the cleanest statement of why the floor and the mattress are not two points on a
softness scale but two different *kinds* of boundary condition.

**It also reframes the ache** (consistent with §5): not "tension," but a **local deviation from
global self-equilibrium** — a prestress error signal.

### 8.1 The measurement problem this creates

The existing §10 measures (standing-posture photographs, single-leg stand, ‖Δ**C**_0‖) are
**asymmetry** measures. A prestress claim needs a **global** measure.

**Warn against the obvious candidate.** Diurnal standing height is **confounded**: the overnight
recovery is mostly *disc hydration* from spinal unloading, which the bed and the floor both provide,
so it will not discriminate.

**Better cheap candidates:** **morning pandiculation** — the traditions asset §7 states that
pandiculation only happens when **a** is already low, so its ease and frequency is a readout of how
settled the system became; the feel of the first movement on rising; resting postural sway.

### 8.2 Comfy versus comfortable — the gradient argument

The existing distinction (traditions asset §7): *comfortable* is a broad high plateau, a global or
broad-basin maximum of the comfort function; *comfy* is a **local maximum amid a landscape of
less-comfortable maxima**. The extension proposed: comfortable **permits** a sprawl; comfy
**specifies** a position.

**Mechanical form.** A compliant substrate **flattens the objective function** — the pressure field
smears, the **gradient** is destroyed, so there is nothing for the sub-cortical system to descend. A
plateau carries no positional information. A rigid substrate **preserves the gradient** — pressure
localises at contact points and the local minimum is **findable** because the derivative exists. So
the floor's comfy point is a **descendable local minimum**, and the search that finds it is what
builds the map. The mattress removes the search, and therefore removes the map.

**Two repairs needed:**

1. **Sign convention.** §7 states comfy as a *local maximum of comfort*; the gradient argument
   descends a *minimum of strain*. Same point, but the asset must fix the sign or the formalism will
   contradict itself the moment someone works the derivative.
2. **"Comfortable allows a sprawl" is true only at onset.** At the creep timescale the mattress
   *becomes* prescriptive — the ditch, which holds you. So the mattress runs permissive →
   prescriptive and the floor runs prescriptive → permissive-after-convergence: **the timescales
   invert**, which is exactly the Phase 1/2/3 table in `losing-the-floor-01.md`. The sharper
   distinction is therefore not sprawl-versus-maintain but **who authors the position: the substrate
   (the ditch) or the body (against a rigid datum)**. The compliant substrate is an *author*, not a
   neutral venue — consistent with the existing "mattress as structural cast" line.

### 8.3 The mattress's harms, from the existing formalism

1. **Signal suppression** — accommodates asymmetry instead of exposing it → no reference-config
   remodelling.
2. **Supine sprawl** — the configuration no ape adopts (~65% side-lying), which the traditions
   discourage, and the one absolute exception in the pregnancy material. A mattress makes supine
   sprawl *comfortable*; a floor does not.
3. **Thermal trapping** — a genuine bed *benefit*, and probably why the bed won, but it decouples
   sleep from the thermal cycle the floor couples it to.
4. **Industrialisation of a proprioceptive judgement** — firmness rating, thread count,
   "orthopaedic" claims convert "does this surface let my governor re-chart" into a purchase
   decision. Same species as the maintenance fallacy in
   `briefs/2026-07-10-brief-sleep-tension-curation.md`.

**On the historical claim about beds.** "Beds are recent" is wrong; **compliant mattresses are
recent**. Beds are ancient (Egyptian, Roman, Chinese). What is recent: the compliant mattress
(innerspring coil patented 1865, mass production by the early 20th century; foam and memory foam
mid-to-late 20th); the bed as a *universal* household item (~200 years, industrialisation); the
private per-person bedroom. The variable that matters is the **compliance axis**, not the bed/no-bed
axis — see §3.1 on the straw tick.

## 9. The n=1 observation record

**The case.** A left lateral fascial chain in the lower leg "switched off" as a result of a leg break
in 2001. Several months of day-and-night floor work and pandiculation-based reactivation.

**Why the case is stronger than it looks — the contralateral limb is the control.** Same body, same
diet, same sleep, same practice, same months. So the measurement is not "did the left improve"
(confounded by everything) but **did the left/right difference shrink** — the §6 ‖Δ**C**_0‖↓
trajectory measured *within* the subject, with a built-in control. Most n=1 claims have no internal
reference; this one does. **Chronicity helps:** 25 years makes spontaneous change implausible, so the
attribution is *strengthened* rather than weakened by the delay.

**Why it is weaker than it looks — no baseline.** The deficit dates to 2001, the intervention to a
few months ago, so the improvement is remembered, not measured (the same weakness as the §6
hip-angle measure, which was at least an angle).

**Cheap measures.** Single-leg stand left versus right; lateral hop; eversion against resistance; and
the binary one — **does the left lateral chain now participate in a yawn-stretch?** That last is the
most direct readout of whether the governor has re-mapped the segment.

**Timescale confirmation.** Months, not nights — exactly the creep/consolidation split of §4. The
case is evidence for the two-timescale structure.

### 9.1 The medial→lateral transition: the two observations are one

Reported: as things balance, the user notices things they "should have noticed" in a quarter-century;
they were **walking and standing on the inner leg fascial chains** and are now **transitioning to the
outer feet and outer fascial chain**; the change is **observed but not initiated**; they feel **safe
and secure** as it happens; aches make walking awkward with a feeling of **"don't walk too far right
now"**; **small muscle spasms, mostly left, also right**; "it feels like things are sorting themselves
out."

**The synthesis: the medial-chain dominance was the compensation for the lateral-chain deficit.**
The lateral chain went offline in 2001 and the system substituted the medial chain. The two
observations are therefore the same observation, and the recovery *is* the lateral return.

**Mechanically consistent.** The **lateral column** of the foot (calcaneus → cuboid → 4th/5th
metatarsals) is the **stable** column; the **medial column** (talus → navicular → cuneiforms) is the
**mobile** one — the medial longitudinal arch is the shock absorber, its mobility "contributing to
foot function by accommodating to varying loads." Weight-bearing should run primarily through the
lateral column with the medial arch maintaining its curve; medial weight-bearing loads the mobile
column as a support — the pronation/collapse pattern. The transition is therefore from loading the
mobile column to loading the stable one, i.e. toward the mechanically correct configuration. Because
the compensation had re-organised the *whole* standing pattern, the recovery propagates upward and
reads as **global** — which is what the prestress claim predicts.

### 9.2 "Observed but not initiated"

The strongest epistemic feature of the record. The confabulation objection (§13) applies to the
**causal story** ("pandiculation did it"), **not** to the **phenomenological report** ("the weight
moved outward and I did not do it"). The two are separable: the phenomenology is trustworthy
precisely *because* it is a report of not having chosen — a chosen change would be suspect; an
observed one is not. The causal attribution is the part that still needs the left/right control.

### 9.3 The late noticing — you cannot notice a deficit you were mapped with

"I should have noticed this in a quarter-century" is itself a **prediction** of the framework, and
not a failure of attention. The governor's map **is** the reference; a deviation from the norm is
invisible, only a deviation from the *map* is visible — and the map was built with the deficit in it.
**Amnesia is not inattention; it is the absence of a reference.** The improvement is what *creates*
the reference against which the old state becomes visible; the body reports the old state at the
moment it is leaving it.

**Corollary with real practical weight:** the practice is **least legible when motivation is most
needed.** Early on the signal is poor; it sharpens as the change progresses — backwards from what a
practice requires. This is a new form of the Phase 3 attrition problem in `losing-the-floor-01.md`.

**And it explains why these practices are transmitted rather than self-taught: the teacher is the
external datum.** You cannot notice the deficit without a reference, and the teacher supplies it. The
floor is the geometric datum; the teacher is the somatic datum. That makes lineage transmission
structural rather than incidental.

### 9.4 Safeness as both enabling condition and product

The traditions asset §7 frames safe/warm/comfy as *preconditions* for **a** falling. The record
reports safeness as an *outcome* of the change. Same variable, both roles — a **positive feedback
loop**: safeness → **a**↓ → change → more safeness. That explains why the process accelerates once it
starts, why it cannot start from a deficit of safeness, and why fear-based approaches fail: they need
the enabling condition they are trying to produce. It is the virtuous twin of the vicious loop in
`losing-the-floor-01.md` (comfort → signal suppression → atrophy → more comfort-seeking).

### 9.5 The spasms

Fasciculations = the same readout as the tensor asset §8 tremors: evidence that **a**(t) is genuinely
*changing*, not merely low. §8's two mechanisms (feedback-loop re-tuning; stiffness–amplitude
coupling) both predict transients *during* the transition and absence at endpoints. **Left-
predominance is predicted** (the left is the segment being re-mapped, so that is where the controller
re-tunes). And the **bilateral involvement is evidence for the global/prestress reading** over a
purely local left-leg reading.

### 9.6 The calf-girth asymmetry: the first objective measure in the record

**The datum.** The left calf has a noticeably smaller diameter than the right.

**Why this matters more than anything else in §9.** Every other item in this record is
phenomenological — the weight moving outward, the spasms, the safeness, the odd postures. This one is
a **tape measure**. It converts the n=1 from a report into something with a number, and it is the
first measurement in the material that is not mediated by the practitioner's attention.

**The asymmetry is a better measurement than either girth.** Same tape, same operator, same day:
systematic errors cancel, so the **difference** is robust in a way that neither absolute value is.
This is the contralateral-control argument of §9 made concrete.

**Protocol, or the number is meaningless.** Girth varies with time of day (fluid shift), posture
(standing loads, supine unloads — so measuring after a night of floor lying and after a day of
standing will differ), landmark, tape tension, contraction state, and temperature. Fix all of them:
mark the landmark on the skin (a fixed distance below the tibial tuberosity, or the point of maximal
girth), same time of day, same position, same tension, relaxed, same operator. **Measure monthly, not
daily** — the timescale is hypertrophy, which is weeks-to-months (§4), and daily checking would be a
*doing* (see §13). A photograph with the tape in frame, taken identically each time, is more objective
still and permits retrospective re-measurement.

**The asymmetry is larger than the chain being attended to.** Calf girth is dominated by
gastrocnemius and soleus. The **peroneals** — the lateral chain in the lower leg — are small muscles
and are not significant contributors to girth. So a *noticeable* girth asymmetry indicates a deficit
that is **limb-wide**, not confined to the lateral fascial chain. That is a prediction of the
**governor-clamp** reading, which clamps a *region* rather than a fascicle: the lateral chain may be
the part that was *noticed*, but the deficit is regional. The measurement therefore supports the
regional-clamp reading over a chain-specific one.

**Convergence is the right prediction but the direction is under-specified — and the direction is
informative.** Three possibilities, all consistent with the framework, meaning different things:

- **The left grows** → recruitment restored to the segment. The direct prediction.
- **The right shrinks** → load redistribution. The right calf has been carrying a compensatory load
  for 25 years and may be *hypertrophied* rather than the left merely atrophied. Convergence by the
  right normalising down is equally consistent.
- **Both**, which is the most likely.

Record which.

**The practitioner's own reading, recorded.** The left calf reduced in size **to accommodate the
reduced load requirement** — the change is a **load adaptation**, not a deficit. This is consistent
with the book's operational heuristic (*restriction is a calculation, not a failure*): the governor
reduced recruitment, demand fell, and the tissue matched the demand. No waste, no damage, an economy.
It is the leading hypothesis — and it is an inference, not a measurement, since the load history is
not observable from the present state.

**The causal position of this measure matters.** The chain runs **governor → recruitment → load →
tissue**. The calf girth is therefore **downstream of everything**: the most integrated and most
end-to-end measure available, and correspondingly the **last** thing to move. It should not be
expected early, and its timescale is the consolidation timescale of §4, not the creep timescale.

**It is confounded by deliberate exercise.** Targeted calf work would close the difference *without*
restoring automatic recruitment, so the girth alone cannot distinguish "recruitment restored" from
"isolated training done." The measure is only clean if the calf is not directly targeted — which is
what the do-nothing / do-what-feels-tight practice implies anyway (§13).

**The asymmetry may be correct, and convergence may not be desirable.** The calf reports that the
load fell; it does not report **why**. If the load fell for a **control** reason (the governor clamped
a region it could not map), restoring the map restores the load and convergence follows. If it fell
for a **mechanical** reason (malunion, limb-length discrepancy, altered joint mechanics from the 2001
fracture), the adaptation is **appropriate** and forcing symmetry would be the error. Convergence is
therefore a **conditional** prediction, and the condition is the absence of a residual mechanical
reason. This is the operational heuristic applied to the practitioner's own body: the difference may
be a calculation, not a failure.

**And the loop is self-locking.** Reduced load → smaller calf → less capacity → further load
avoidance. The calf cannot bootstrap its own load, and the load will not return until the map permits
it. So the causal entry point is the **governor**, not the tissue — which is why isolated calf
strengthening treats the readout rather than the cause.

**And symmetry is not required.** 25 years of disuse can leave permanent
changes (fibre-type shifts, reduced motor-unit numbers, connective-tissue change), so the left may
improve substantially and still not match. **The prediction is that the difference shrinks, not that
it vanishes.** The *rate* matters more than the endpoint: a shrinking difference over months is the
signal; a plateau means the remodelling window has closed.

**A structural measure should be paired with a functional one.** Girth is structure; the complement is
**single-leg heel raise to failure, left versus right** — the classic soleus/gastrocnemius test, and
it is a within-subject comparison. Structure and function converging is a far stronger claim than
either alone.

**The differential that must be named: peroneal nerve injury.** Common peroneal (fibular) nerve palsy
is a well-known complication of proximal fibular and proximal tibial fractures. If the 2001 break
involved the fibular neck, the lateral chain could be offline for a **neurological** reason rather
than a governor-map reason — and the two have different prognoses, because re-innervation is
regeneration-limited (~1 mm/day, frequently incomplete) rather than re-mapping-limited. This is a
clinical question and not one to settle from the inside.

**The discriminator, and it is cheap.** Does the lateral chain engage when **isolated and attended**
(resisted eversion, deliberate foot placement) but fail to appear in **automatic** gait? If yes, the
machinery is intact and the map excludes it — the governor reading. If it fails in both, a
neural/structural deficit is more likely. **The user's own report argues against a complete lesion**:
they describe the transition to the outer foot as *happening* — observed, not initiated (§9.2). A
paralysed peroneal group could not produce that transition.

**This is also the measurement that most needs the §13 discipline.** It is the one place where
recording is essential, and the one where the practitioner has a stated expectation ("I would expect
the two to converge"). The expectation is fine as a prediction with a horizon; it becomes corrosive
as a setpoint checked daily. Measure monthly, as a separate act from the practice.

## 10. The falsifier ladder

Ranked by cost and discriminating power. Rungs 1–4 come from the practice record (§9); rungs 13–16
from the ontogeny thread (§3.7); rungs 17–18 from the calf-girth measure (§9.6).

1. **Day versus night floor work.** If the benefits appear in daytime floor work, sleep architecture
   and the sleep-specific mechanisms are not required (§4). The separator.
2. **Left/right difference in the lateral chain.** Single-leg stand, lateral hop, eversion, and
   yawn-stretch participation. The user's own internal control.
3. **Do the spasms fade as the change consolidates?** §8 predicts transients during, absent at
   endpoints.
4. **Does the "don't walk too far" constraint recede over weeks?** Remodelling window versus
   deconditioning.
5. **Pre-waking state: restless or calm?** Failure-escalation (§5) predicts restless-then-relief. If
   the floor waking is calm throughout, the account is wrong and a completion sensor is needed again.
   First-person, no kit.
6. **Localised versus diffuse ache field.** The mechanism *requires* the mattress to produce
   *diffuse* aches, not fewer aches. Real risk: signal suppression may produce *no* ache rather than
   noisy ache, which would break the loop. Test, do not assume.
7. **Morning pandiculation ease and frequency** — the global-prestress readout (§8.1).
8. **Nutrition-gated retention.** Does a floor-lying block hold its length change under controlled
   protein intake versus depletion? Slow, but decisive for the fuel claim.
9. **MMC-phase coupling** with the waking time, predicted from the last meal (§6).
10. **Lie on the ache**, green-yellow zone only — resolve or aggravate?
11. **The surface-compliance re-read of Ekirch's corpus.** Use sleeping-surface compliance as the
    independent variable. Prediction: second-sleep reports cluster on the **firm** end (straw tick,
    boards) and thin out on feather ticks; and the *bedded* record should report the waking as
    **troublesome** while the firm-surface record reports it as **calm**. A reading task; the
    cheapest test available, and the one that would let the book make the historical claim honestly.
12. **Load-dependence log** with scotoperiod control: given a long scotoperiod, do the waking's
    timing and character track the previous day's mechanical load, independently of SWS rebound?
13. **The anabolic axis versus the growth axis** (§3.7). Does the spontaneous load-seeking behaviour
    thin with growth completion (~18–20, epiphyseal fusion) or with the GH/IGF-1 decline (which
    continues past 23)? If the latter, the driver is consolidation *capacity*, not growth *demand*.
14. **Suppression versus absence** (§3.7). Does the behaviour survive in adults under social licence?
    Compare age-matched populations with different licensing — dancers, athletes, people from
    floor-sitting cultures — against the general adult population. If licensed groups retain it, the
    thinning is suppression rather than loss.
15. **Directedness** (§3.7). Is the behaviour aimed at *rigid* frames and *held*, or is it undirected
    and brief? The elasticity account predicts the latter; the remodelling account predicts the
    former. Observation only, no kit.
16. **Postural variety during floor time as a map-quality proxy** (§3.7). Prediction: variety
    **increases** over months of practice — the opposite of the naive expectation.
17. **Calf-girth convergence** (§9.6). Mark the landmark, measure both calves monthly, record the
    **difference** and its direction (left growing, right shrinking, or both). Prediction:
    the difference shrinks; symmetry is not required. **Conditional** — convergence if the load fell
    for a control reason, persistence if a residual mechanical reason remains. Pair with single-leg
    heel raise to failure, left versus right. Only clean if the calf is not directly trained.
18. **Isolated versus automatic recruitment** (§9.6). Does the lateral chain engage when attended but
    fail to appear in automatic gait? Governor reading: yes. Neural/structural deficit: no. This is
    also the cheapest available screen for a peroneal-nerve differential.

## 11. Honest gaps and the prediction problem

**The framework predicts everything reported.** That is the danger, not the strength: a framework
that predicts everything explains nothing (tensor asset §9). The question is what the user would
expect to feel if the framework were **wrong**.

**Correction to the rival-account framing.** "Deconditioning" is **not a rival hypothesis** — it is a
*description* of the state, not an explanation of it. The atrophy's proposed mechanism (the governor
clamps the region → motor units are not recruited → disuse atrophy) *produces* the deconditioning; the
two are the same phenomenon at different levels of description, not competing accounts. Treating them
as rivals was a category error. **The genuine rival is different:** *spontaneous recovery under any
use* versus *this specific intervention* (floor work plus pandiculation). That rival remains live, and
it is what the absent baseline and the absent intervention control fail to exclude.

Rival accounts, correctly stated, and their signatures:

- **Spontaneous recovery under ordinary use** (the limb would have recovered anyway, or with any
  use): improvement proportional to *total* use, not to the specific practice; no change in the
  automatic-versus-attended pattern (§9.6, rung 18); **no** bilateral involvement, **no** global
  posture change, **no** safeness change, and no change in the left/right calf difference beyond what
  general use would give.
- **Attention / placebo:** the report tracks *practice* rather than *state* — better after practice,
  reverting between sessions, no cumulative trend.

So the framework's **specific** predictions are the four structural features: **bilaterality,
globality, cumulativeness, and the late-arriving noticing.** Those are what distinguish it from the
rivals, and they are what should be carried into any brief.

**Methodological rule that follows.** Trust the **present-tense** reports (the weight moving outward,
the spasms, the safeness). Treat **past-tense** claims ("I was always on the medial chain") as
reconstructions — memory reconstructs, and the retrospective "I should have noticed" is partly
constructed.

**The waking now has five candidate causes** — cycle boundary, dual oscillator, thermal arousal,
completion signal, and diffuse-ache escalation (plus MMC/gut arousal as a sixth from §6). **This
field needs pruning, not more candidates.** The perturbation protocol's gate between "note" and
"touch" is the relevant discipline: note a new mechanism, do not add it to the model until one of the
others has been killed.

**Unresolved.** (i) The five/six-candidate problem for the waking. (ii) Whether the calm floor waking
is pre- or post-escalation. (iii) Whether the diffuse-ache prediction survives contact with
measurement. (iv) The prestress measurement problem (§8.1) — no global measure yet identified that
is not confounded. (v) The ontogeny thread is now **partially resolved** — see §3.7, which supplies
the developmental observation and the suppression reading; what remains open is which driver
attenuates the behaviour (§10, rungs 13–14), and the thread still needs a dedicated literature search
before it could carry any manuscript weight.

## 13. The practice claim, treated separately

The operational claim as given: *the key thing is the floor and getting there and staying there warm,
safe, and comfy; provide that and the rest happens without much conscious involvement; the real
challenge is to accept that lying on the floor doing nothing is the job — the whole job — and that is
your job; so do not have expectations, do not record anything, do not do anything; just do nothing.*

**This is a different kind of claim from §2–§8 and should not be assessed by the same standard.**

**What is sound.** If **a**↓ is driven by the autonomic system and the cortex cannot lower **a** —
it can only refrain from re-recruiting — then *any* cortical activity is at best neutral. So "do
nothing" is not a mystical instruction; it is the only instruction consistent with the mechanism. It
is also the *practice* of the anti-arrogance principle: if the cortex tries to do the job, it commits
the arrogance. Instruction and principle are the same thing. And the first sentence is the more
defensible formulation: the enabling conditions are the intervention, which matches §7's
boundary-condition specification exactly.

**Three problems, and they are real.**

1. **"Do nothing" has no execution path, and §7 already contains one.** "Do nothing" is itself a
   doing — the classic paradox of relaxation instructions, where *trying* is arousal. The traditions
   solve it by indirect means (Alexander's "inhibition"; attention on the breath; the arm-to-nose
   probe as §7's own "if you must do something" escape hatch). §7's phenomenology section is the
   correct account: the gate shifts from **held to structural, from effortful to environmental** —
   the floor removes the affordances, so the doing-thoughts have no target. **The floor is the
   instruction's execution path.** Dropping the probes while keeping "do nothing" removes the
   mechanism and keeps the slogan.
   **Partially answered, and in the practitioner's favour — see §3.7.** The observation that floor
   work produces spontaneous odd postures supplies the execution path directly: the do-nothing
   instruction forbids *cortical* initiation, not subcortical movement, and the postures are
   movement the practitioner did not initiate. So the instruction does have an execution path, and
   it is the subcortical search. What remains true in the objection is narrower: the instruction is
   not itself the execution path, and stated alone it gives the cortex nothing to *do* while waiting.
2. **"Do not record anything" contradicts the falsifier ladder, and it is the mechanism of the
   confabulation it seeks to avoid.** The present-tense reports *are* the data (§9.2). Not recording
   means the data is gone and the confabulation wins by default — the user's own point in §13's
   preceding item. **Resolution: separate the practice from the measurement in time.** Do not record
   *during* floor time (recording is a doing, and it recruits the cortex). Record briefly *after*, as
   a separate act. Both are honoured; the confabulation problem is still addressed.
3. **"No expectations" is correct, but as a *consequence* rather than a virtue.** Expectation is a
   setpoint, and a setpoint is what a feedback controller tracks — set one and the loop reports the
   error. The practical reason it must be dropped is §9.3: the signal is poor early, so early
   expectations cannot be supported by the evidence available. It is derived from the signal-quality
   curve, not merely good advice.

**A note on emphasis.** The instruction is itself cortex-authored content: a rule written by the
cortex about how to stop the cortex interfering. The emphasis in "just do NOTHING" is the tell —
emphasis is a doing, and it suggests the cortex is still trying to control the outcome through the
instruction. The traditions that hold this instruction best hold it lightly. This does not make the
instruction wrong; it makes it a **working instruction** rather than a law. The test: if the
instruction were the mechanism, everyone who did nothing on the floor would get the same result.
They do not. **The conditions are the cause; "do nothing" is the practice name for providing them.**
