# The Unattended Protocol

*Persisted 19 September 2026. A first-person protocol description — floor sessions of open-ended duration, dietary directives, and the claim that none of it is consciously directed — assessed against the literature and against the model's own logic. Third in the 2026-09-19 thread, after the literature digest and the two-timescale argument. Research material (the yap), not voiced text. No voice pass has run.*

---

## The protocol as described

- **Duration is open-ended** — "as long as one wants."
- **Typical session:** a couple of hours on the floor, then up, then movement, then sitting on a low wooden stool.
- **The stool phase is experienced as "checking out the effects so far."**
- **Dietary directives:** predictable across sessions — carbs, some protein, vitamin C, slightly salty water.
- **Nothing is consciously directed.** Aches-to-lie-on, foods, and times are generated by the sub-cortical movement processes.
- **The operator follows the feeling** and does whatever is indicated.

---

## 1. The protocol matches a prediction derived independently from tissue physics

This is the most interesting thing about it, and it should be recorded as such rather than passed over.

The digest derived, from muscle thixotropy plus tendon preconditioning, that the sequence must be **passive first, active second** — because stillness *increases* cross-linked stiffness and only movement breaks it. The observed protocol is exactly that: lie (passive), then get up and move (active). The derivation came from the tissue literature; the protocol came from behaviour. Those are independent routes to the same ordering.

The stool adds a third element the derivation did not specify but the model needs — the **readout step**. So the whole protocol reads:

> load the datum → unload → shear by moving → sample from a third rig

Every step has a mechanism in the model, and the ordering was not designed. That is weak evidence, and it is the right *kind* of weak evidence: a coincidence between a derived prediction and an independently produced behaviour. Worth stating plainly and worth not overstating.

## 2. The stool is a third datum, and the right kind of one

A low wooden stool is well-chosen on the model's own terms:

- **Rigid** — which preserves the pressure readout. This is the same argument that puts a hard floor above a mattress, applied one level up.
- **Takes load off the feet without loading the pelvis** against a backrest, and leaves the spine free.
- **Intermediate between the floor** (maximal contact, posterior chain) **and standing** (point contact at the feet).

And it is *not the floor*, which is what makes the check meaningful. A change read from the same rig could be a change in the rig. Reading from a different rig is a genuine measurement design, arrived at without being designed. The "checking out the effects" instinct is doing real epistemic work.

## 3. Open-ended duration is defensible — and for a specific reason

The safety of unbounded duration rests on the same mechanism the protocol depends on, which is why the two questions have one answer.

Sustained pressure is what produces pressure injury; the protective response is the ache → shift loop. **The ache is not an inconvenience to be endured — it is the pressure-relief reflex.** That is why an unbounded session is tolerable for someone who obeys it. The duration is not safe because floors are harmless; it is safe because the sub-cortical loop is a functioning regulator.

Which makes the failure modes precise, and they are all failures of the *loop* rather than of the duration:

- **Impaired sensation** — diabetic or other neuropathy, where the ache signal is attenuated. Unbounded duration is not safe here, and this is the one absolute contraindication.
- **Sedation, alcohol, or sleep.** Falling asleep on a hard floor loses the shifting behaviour and the ability to get up. The loop requires a conscious operator.
- **Cold.** Conductive heat loss to a hard floor over hours is significant, and the felt signal for cold is **less salient** than the felt signal for ache. This is the one failure mode the protocol does not self-correct, and it is the one that warrants an explicit rule rather than trust in the feeling.
- **Any framing that treats discomfort as weakness.** That is the loop being deliberately disabled, and it is how ascetic practice turns a protective reflex into a hazard.

## 4. The nutritional directives: sound biochemistry, weaker appetite science

The list has a genuine biochemical basis, item by item.

- **Vitamin C.** Prolyl and lysyl hydroxylase are ascorbate-dependent — ascorbate is the reducing agent for the active-site iron. Without it, collagen is under-hydroxylated and mechanically unstable; scurvy is the demonstration. If the session is doing matrix work, vitamin C is a real cofactor requirement.
- **Protein — glycine, proline, lysine.** Collagen is roughly one-third glycine, and glycine is the rate-limiting substrate in some conditions. A protein appetite is supported in humans by the **protein leverage** framework (Raubenheimer & Simpson): intake is regulated toward a protein target, and diluting dietary protein drives compensatory overeating.
- **Copper — missing from the list, and it belongs on it.** Lysyl oxidase, which performs the enzymatic cross-linking named in the two-timescale argument, is **copper-dependent**. If cross-link state is a lever, copper is its cofactor. The list names the substrate and the vitamin and omits the trace metal that does the cross-linking.
- **Carbs.** Matrix synthesis is metabolically expensive — hydroxylation, glycosylation, the secretory pathway, and the ATP for all of it. The repo already carries this claim at `maintenance-burden-equation.md:72`. Plausible as an energy demand; not a specific appetite.
- **Salt.** The most interesting item and the most apt. Hyaluronan is a **polyanion**: its very large water-binding capacity is substantially a Donnan/osmotic effect mediated by bound Na⁺ counterions. Sodium is not incidental to matrix hydration — it is the mechanism of it. And **sodium appetite is one of the few nutrient-specific appetites well established in humans**, driven by aldosterone and angiotensin II. Strongest item on the list, at both ends.

**But the inferential step is weaker than the biochemical step.** The question is not whether the nutrients are *needed*; it is whether a *felt desire* is a readout of that need. On that question:

- **Sodium appetite: established.** A real regulatory appetite with identified drivers.
- **Protein appetite: supported** in humans via protein leverage.
- **Specific hungers for vitamins and minerals: mostly not established in humans.** The classic cases are pica (ice, dirt, clay) with iron deficiency, and calcium appetite in some animals. "Cravings signal nutrient deficiency" as a general principle is *not* supported — food cravings in humans are better predicted by restriction, learned association, mood, and culture than by nutrient status. Chocolate craving is not magnesium deficiency.

So the list is a mix. **Salt is well-supported as an appetite, protein moderately, and vitamin C is biochemically necessary without being established as something humans develop a specific hunger for.** The claim narrows rather than failing: the biochemical list is sound as a description of what matrix work requires, and only some of those requirements have a demonstrated appetite channel — and those happen to be the two the list emphasises as felt.

## 5. "Nothing is directed by the conscious mind" — mostly right, and the exception matters

What is genuinely sub-cortical:

- **Homeostatic regulation** — breathing, blood pressure, temperature, blood glucose — runs below awareness entirely.
- **Rest–activity timing** — the suprachiasmatic nucleus, the orexin and MCH systems, and the basal ganglia generate the urge to move and the urge to rest.
- **Postural adjustment and habitual movement** — basal ganglia and cerebellum.
- **The ache → shift loop** — a nociceptive/pressure-relief reflex.
- **Pandiculation is involuntary.** Bertolucci's abstract: "the involuntary stretching of the soft tissues, which occurs in most animal species and is associated with transitions between cyclic biological behaviors." Direct precedent for the claim, in the same literature the model already draws on.

The strong version — *nothing* is consciously directed — is too strong, because you are choosing to lie down, choosing to get up, choosing what to eat. The accurate and more interesting version:

> **The timing and the targets are generated sub-cortically. The conscious mind supplies permission and veto.**

Which is what chapter 15 already says in its own register ("get your stupid intent out of the way," chapter-15 line 11). The protocol is that idea executed over hours instead of minutes.

## 6. The circularity — the real objection, and it needs a specific answer

Not "you might be wrong." The specific objection is:

> The model's premise is that the system runs on **stale priors** — that is what structural debt *is*. If the directives are generated by the same sub-cortical system that generated the distortion, then the directives are the thing needing calibration, not the thing doing the calibrating. **The correction is being authored by the process under correction.**

This is a better objection than the generic one, and it is answerable by splitting the directives by *kind*:

- **Reflexive directives** — the ache → shift loop, the urge to pandiculate, the urge to get up and move. These are **closed loops with immediate feedback**: the behaviour changes the signal within seconds, and the loop terminates when the signal resolves. A closed loop with a fast feedback term is self-correcting by construction. **Follow these.**
- **Interpretive directives** — "this ache means I need to work on the psoas"; "this craving means I need protein." These are **open-loop narratives** attached to a signal. Nothing in them closes; nothing can disconfirm them. **Record these; do not follow them on their own authority.**

The practical rule: **follow what is closed, record what is open.** The ache → shift loop is a control system. The story about what the ache means is a hypothesis, and it should be written down as one so that it can turn out to be false.

## 7. The interoception problem with "I follow the feeling"

There is a specific literature here, and it is unhelpful to the protocol's default posture. The three-dimensional model of interoception separates:

- **interoceptive accuracy** — how well internal signals are actually detected, against an objective criterion;
- **interoceptive sensibility** — the person's *belief* about their interoceptive ability;
- **interoceptive awareness** — the correspondence between the two.

The finding that matters: **sensibility and accuracy dissociate.** Confidence in one's bodily read is not a reliable proxy for the read's quality. "I follow the feeling" carries an implicit assumption — that the feeling is accurate — and the literature does not grant it. This does not mean the feeling is wrong. It means **the feeling cannot be its own audit.**

Which is why the stool check is more important than it looks. It is the only step in the protocol that compares two states rather than reporting one. It is the protocol's only measurement, and it should be made explicit, consistent, and repeated — same rig, same time, same question.

## 8. What the protocol needs in order to become auditable

Concrete and cheap:

- **Log the directives, not the experience.** Ache location, position adopted, food wanted, time. Not "it felt good."
- **Keep report and interpretation in separate columns.** "Ache in left hip crease" (report) versus "my psoas is releasing" (interpretation). Keeping them apart is the whole discipline — it is the difference between a datum and a story.
- **Take the predictability seriously.** Cross-session stability of the craving cluster is the single most informative observation available here, because **a homeostatic driver is state-locked and a habit is context-locked.** That is a real discriminator, and the user has already noticed the stability.
- **The discriminating prediction:** if the dietary directives are homeostatic, they should *precede* and *predict* something measurable, and should disappear once the state is corrected. If they persist unchanged regardless of state, they are habit or expectation.
- **Time the stool check.** If it reports change at 1 h and not at 3 days, the two-timescale prediction is confirmed by the protocol's own readout — which would be the first independent test the model has had.

## 9. The session sequence, as described

A four-stage sequence was reported within a session:

1. Aches reduce, both after lying on them and after moving through them exploratorily.
2. Then, with movement, cracking and clicking — "like snapping elastic bands."
3. Then it feels as though new ranges of movement have come online, and they are still a bit sensitive.
4. Additional range of initially careful motion is realised.

Each stage has a different mechanism, and — this is the point — they are not all on the same clock.

### 9a. The ache reduction is neural and fast; the range gain is mechanical and slower

**Ache reduction** has at least three established mechanisms, all fast:

- **Gate control** (Melzack & Wall): non-noxious mechanoreceptive input — the pressure of lying on the region, and the movement through it — inhibits nociceptive transmission at the dorsal horn. This is why rubbing a hurt place helps, and it operates in seconds.
- **Exercise-induced hypoalgesia**: movement engages descending modulatory pathways and produces a general reduction in pain sensitivity. Well documented.
- **Extinction of the threat response.** The CNS learns the position is not dangerous. This is the model's own approach-and-retreat protocol, and chapter 15's cramp scam.

**Range gain** draws on a different set: thixotropic breakdown (the SREC, from movement), viscoelastic creep, and reduced reflex guarding. Slower, and mechanical.

**The consequence for the model:** the ache reduction and the range gain co-occur but are *not the same process*, and they are not on the same clock. The model has been treating "the map re-zeroing" as one thing. It is at least two — a **neural** layer that changes in seconds (gating, descending inhibition, threat extinction) and a **mechanical** layer that changes over hours to days (thixotropy, matrix state). That is the same two-timescale structure as the remodelling argument, one level up: fast neural, slow mechanical. Worth naming, because it explains why the ache can vanish while the range has not yet changed, and the reverse.

### 9b. The sounds: cavitation is a marker, not a mechanism

The physics of the crack is settled. Kawchuk et al. (2015) imaged it in real time: **joint cracking is cavity formation** — tribonucleation, a gas bubble forming as the joint is distracted and the synovial fluid's pressure drops. The older "bubble collapse" theory is wrong. The gas takes roughly twenty minutes to re-dissolve, which is the refractory period — the reason the same joint cannot be cracked twice immediately.

Three distinct sounds are in play, and the distinction matters:

- **Cavitation** — a single distinct crack, once, with a refractory period. Intra-articular.
- **Snapping** — a tendon or fascia sliding over a bony prominence. This is the classic presentation of snapping hip (*coxa saltans*), snapping scapula, and snapping triceps, and it *repeats with the same movement* rather than happening once. The "elastic band" quality of the description points here rather than to cavitation: a taut structure releasing under tension is a tendon-snapping signature.
- **Crepitus** — grinding, grating, crunching. Different again, and the one worth knowing about, because it can indicate chondral change.

**The therapeutic point is the important one: the audible crack is not required for the effect.** The manipulation literature is clear that an audible pop is not needed to produce the CNS changes that follow manipulation. The crack is a *side effect* of the mechanical event, not its mechanism.

So the honest reading of the sequence: the crack and the range change are both **consequences of the same mechanical event**, not cause and effect. The crack is a *marker* that something moved. It is not what moved it.

Which gives the actionable rule: **do not use the sound as the evidence. Measure the range.** The range is what matters and what can be checked — goniometer, or a landmark test (fingertip-to-floor, hand-behind-back, a fixed reference point). The sound is a datum about an event, not about an outcome.

**Superseded for this case — see §9f.** The operator corrected the record: this is not cavitation. Cavitation remains the correct frame for a *joint* crack with a refractory period; it is not the frame for what is being described here. §9f replaces this section's reading, and also revises its epistemic conclusion — the interfacial sound turns out to be a *readout* of the friction regime rather than a mere event marker.

### 9c. "Sensitive" is the signature of newly-mapped territory

The third and fourth stages are the most interesting, because they describe a state the model has not yet accounted for.

Newly-available range is **proprioceptively unfamiliar** — the map has not been extended into it. That is the chapter 18 situation, the map undefined in unmapped regions. But the phenomenology is the *opposite* of the arm routine's:

- In the arm routine, the uncalibrated hand reads as **subdued** — the region has *lost* its data (pendular, effort-masked, unanchored).
- Here, the newly-available range reads as **sensitive** — the region has just *gained* data, and novel afferent input is flagged as news.

So **subdued and sensitive are two signs of the same condition** — an uncalibrated region — differing by the direction of the signal change. Lost data reads as absence; new data reads as sensitivity. That is a refinement of the model's map account and belongs in the felt vocabulary.

And the "careful motion" is not timidity. It is the **writing phase** — the same role pandiculation plays in the brief's anchoring routine (step 4: "active slow sweep through loaded↔unloaded — to write the new set-point"). The carefulness *is* the calibration procedure. It should be done, not avoided — and slowly, which is what is already happening.

### 9d. The caution that matters most

Two, and the second is the serious one.

**The range gain is probably transient.** Consistent with thixotropy and viscoelastic effects. If it is gone in a day it was the matrix, and the two-timescale prediction holds. If it persists unchanged for a week, something else is doing the work. Same test as before.

**The serious one: the "release" narrative is the exact story the manual-therapy literature over-reads, and chasing it is a known pattern of harm.** The sequence — ache, crack, new range, a feeling of something letting go — is compelling, coherent, and felt. It is also precisely the phenomenology that gets inflated into structural claims the evidence does not support. And there is a specific hazard: **if the range gain comes with reduced protective tone, then pursuing more range is the wrong direction.** Acquired hypermobility and instability from repeated manipulation aimed at "releasing" restrictions is a real clinical pattern, not a hypothetical.

The rule that follows: **the range that has opened is to be *mapped*, not extended.** The careful motion does the mapping. Chasing further range — trying to reproduce the crack, seeking the next release — converts a calibration procedure into a stretch habit, and the thing being calibrated is protective tone, which is the thing you least want to lose.

### 9e. What to record

Extending §8:

- **Characterise the sound.** Single crack with a refractory period (cavitation), repeatable snap on the same movement (tendon), or grinding (crepitus). Three different things, free to record, diagnostic later.
- **Measure the range; do not infer it from the sound.** A landmark test before and after, and at 1 h, 1 day, 3 days. This is the only objectively checkable part of the sequence, and it tests the two-timescale prediction directly.
- **Keep report and interpretation apart** — "crack on the left hip at the top of the movement" versus "the band released." Same discipline as §6.
- **Note whether the sensitivity resolves.** If the newly-opened range stops feeling sensitive within a day or two, the mapping completed. If it stays sensitive, either it is not mapped or something else is going on, and that is worth knowing.

### 9f. Correction: the interface reading, and the slip-pulse physics

The operator corrected the record: this is **not** cavitation. It is "tension release in a snapping motion with boundary effect noise." The correction is right, and it lands on better physics than either cavitation or stick-slip.

**The relevant result is recent and directly on point.** Djellouli et al., "Squeaking at soft–rigid frictional interfaces," *Nature* (2026) — Harvard SEAS with Nottingham, CNRS and the Hebrew University. They imaged a soft solid sliding on a rigid substrate at up to one million frames per second and found that the sound is **not** produced by conventional stick-slip. It is produced by **opening slip pulses**: rapid, wrinkle-like *detachment* fronts that propagate along the contact interface at near-supersonic speeds, detaching and reattaching it. The audible pitch is set by the *repetition rate* of these pulses. The authors are explicit that the stick-slip framework "does not fully capture the physics of soft-on-rigid interfaces."

Three of their findings bear directly on the observation:

1. **The sound is generated at the interface.** It is a boundary phenomenon in the literal sense — the noise is the acoustic signature of the contact boundary opening and closing. "Boundary effect noise" is the correct description, not a metaphor.
2. **Geometry decides the character of the sound.** Flat or irregular interfaces produce complex, irregular pulses and **broadband** noise — "rushing or swooshing." Confined, ridged geometries trap the pulses into a periodic cycle and produce a **focused tonal pitch**. So *a snap plus broadband noise* is the signature of an irregular or unconfined interface, and a clean *tone* is the signature of a structured, confined one.
3. **The pulses significantly affect the frictional resistance.** The sound is not incidental to the mechanics — it is coupled to them.

**Why this is a good analogy rather than a loose one.** Their system is a soft solid on a rigid substrate. The body contains exactly this class of interface: a tendon running through a retinaculum or pulley, fascia sliding over bone, a tendon crossing a bony prominence. The tendon–pulley interface has a measured friction coefficient and a measured gliding resistance, and trigger finger is described in the literature precisely as a disruption of the gliding system. So "soft tissue sliding over a rigid boundary" is not an analogy stretched from rubber and glass — it is the same class of system.

Marked as analogy, not demonstration: the *Nature* result is on rubber and glass, not on tissue. It establishes that the mechanism class exists and behaves this way. It does not establish that a given bodily snap is this mechanism.

**Two things this changes.**

**First, the epistemic status of the sound.** §9b concluded that the crack is "a marker, not a mechanism" — right for cavitation, because the pop carries no information about the therapeutic mechanism. The interfacial sound is different: it is a **readout of the friction regime**, because the pulses are coupled to frictional resistance. A change in the sound's character is therefore evidence of a change in the interface state. That is information, not merely an event marker. The two sounds have different epistemic statuses and should not be lumped.

**Second, a diagnostic that was not available before.** §9b concluded that tendon snapping and interfacial slip could not be told apart from the inside. The geometry result gives a physical basis for telling them apart from the *sound*: a confined, structured pathway — a tendon in a pulley or retinaculum — should produce a **tonal** sound, while a flat or irregular interface — a fascial sheet over a plane of bone — should produce **broadband** noise.

> **Tonal → a structured, confined pathway. Broadband → a flat or irregular interface.**

Which makes the sound worth recording in more detail than "a crack": tone versus broadband, single event versus repeating, pitch stable versus wandering. Free data with a mechanism behind it.

**On "tension release."** What the slip-pulse frame has releasing is *adhesion at the interface* — the contact opening — not stored elastic energy in a band. That distinction matters. "Tension release" is correct if it means the interface letting go; it is not correct if it means a fascial band discharging elastic energy like an over-stretched rubber band, which would be a material failure and is not supported. Given the "boundary effect" framing, the interface reading is the one intended — and it is the one the physics supports.

**And it coheres with the thread.** The whole argument has been that gliding is the goal and liquidity is not: the HA-rich layer between deep fascia and muscle, densification as the stuck state, movement as the shear that restores gliding. The slip-pulse result says a stuck soft–rigid interface releases in **discrete events** rather than smoothly. So the snapping is the acoustic signature of a densified or high-friction interface returning to a gliding state — precisely the transition the model is trying to produce. The paper's own framing — a material that can "transition from low-friction to high-grip states on demand" — is the same transition read the other way.

### 9g. Cascades: the crackling-noise frame, and what the termination tells you

The further observation: the sounds come in **cascades**, and a cascade ends either abruptly — "that was the last one" — or by decaying in amplitude until it is gone.

**This is a recognised signature, and the frame for it is crackling noise.** Sethna, Dahmen & Myers (*Nature* 410:242, 2001): "crackling noise arises when a system responds to changing external conditions through discrete, impulsive events." The paradigm is a system with many metastable states and a threshold, **driven slowly**, releasing energy in **avalanches of many sizes** whose distribution is typically a power law. Magnets, martensites, paper tearing, earthquakes. Friction interfaces are squarely in the class: slip avalanches and their acoustic emission are a studied problem, with the extreme-value statistics of stick-slip motion an active area.

**Why the body qualifies.** A disordered sliding interface under slow drive is the setup exactly. The drive is the exploratory movement; the disorder is local variation in adhesion and lubrication; the threshold is the local static friction. When one site slips, elastic coupling to its neighbours redistributes stress, which can push a neighbour past its own threshold, which slips, and so on. **That is the mechanism for a cascade specifically — and it is why a cascade is evidence of coupling.**

**That point matters more than it looks.** Independent sites would produce isolated events. Coupled sites produce cascades. So a cascade is weak evidence that the sites are **mechanically connected** — which is a fragment of support for the "long fascial chain" idea (§6a of the digest), the part flagged earlier as a map rather than a measured structure. The cascade does not show that the named chains exist. It does say the tissue is not behaving as a set of independent points.

**The two termination modes, and what they mean.** Both are documented ways for an avalanche to stop, and both mean the same thing:

- **Abrupt termination** — a large final event relaxed the remaining stored stress in one go; the avalanche consumed all the locally unstable sites.
- **Decaying amplitude** — progressive exhaustion, the system asymptotically approaching the relaxed state.

Either way the cascade ends when **local stress is below threshold everywhere**. Which makes the end of a cascade a **state marker**: it marks the region reaching a locally stable configuration. That is genuinely useful, because it gives the practice a termination criterion that is *observable* rather than felt — and by the closed-loop / open-story split of §6, an observable termination is the kind of directive that is safe to follow.

> **Follow the cascade to its end; the end is the region reporting that it is locally relaxed.**

**Four cautions, and the first is the important one.**

**1. Crackling noise is a universality class, so the observation is weak evidence for any specific mechanism.** Power-law avalanche statistics appear in magnets, earthquakes, torn paper and martensitic transformations alike. Finding cascades establishes "this is a slowly-driven threshold system with disorder," which is true of nearly everything. It is *consistent with* the model and it is *not diagnostic* of it. Using "it's avalanche dynamics" as though it confirmed a particular tissue mechanism over-reads a universality result.

**2. The felt "last one" is not reliable evidence of a physical last one.** The perception that a cascade has finished is subject to expectation and auditory adaptation, and it is partly constructed after the fact — you notice it stopped, then infer it was final. The test is free and immediate: **move again.** If the region is genuinely relaxed, the next movement is silent. If more come, the "last one" was premature. That converts a felt claim into an observable one at no cost.

**3. Decay and growth mean opposite things.** A decaying cascade is convergence. If the cascades *grow* across sessions, or the amplitude rises, the interface is becoming more stuck rather than less — a signal worth attending to rather than a sign of progress.

**4. A large cascade is a large mechanical event.** It is a big stress drop, not a small one. Not obviously harmful, but "a snap is something small letting go" is wrong on the size axis: a loud cascade is a large local shear event, and the ordinary caution about loading a freshly-changed interface applies.

**The experiment the observation makes possible.** If this is avalanche dynamics, the size distribution of the events should be **heavy-tailed** — many small, a few large — rather than clustered around a typical size. That is testable by ear and by tally: log each event as barely audible / clear / loud, within a session and across sessions, and look at the shape of the distribution. A heavy tail would be a real, if modest, result — and it is the kind of observation a self-report practice is unusually well placed to make, because the practitioner is the instrument and is already listening.

**One methodological sharpness.** The *decay* alone does not discriminate between the two candidate readings. Avalanche dynamics predicts a heavy-tailed distribution with a decay envelope; progressive interface lubrication predicts monotonic decay without a scale-free distribution. Both predict that the sound fades. **Only the shape of the distribution separates them** — which is why the tally is worth keeping and the decay is not, by itself, informative.

Marked: this is a *prediction*, not a finding. No crack-size distribution for a floor practice has been published.

### 9h. "Muscle guards being fully released" — the right intuition, the wrong causal slot

The operator's further reading: the cracking feels like **muscle guards being fully released, so that a recovered range of motion is available again.**

Two halves, with different epistemic status.

**The second half is well supported.** Guarding — protective co-contraction held by nociceptive drive — is a direct limiter of available range. Reduce the drive and the range returns, on the fast neural clock of §9a. "Guard released → range available" is not controversial; it is the standard account of why pain limits movement and why removing the drive restores it.

**The first half is where the physics breaks.** A guard is a **policy, not a material**. It is a maintained pattern of motor-neuron output — an electrochemical set point in the nervous system. Materials emit acoustic emissions when they slip or fail. Policies do not. There is no route by which the cessation of alpha-motor-neuron firing produces a broadband impulsive crack.

What muscle *does* sound like is documented, and it is the opposite of a crack. Acoustic myography records a **low-frequency rumble** in the sound/infrasound band, arising from the lateral dimension change of fibres during contraction (Oster & Jaffe, *Biophys J*, 1980). Continuous, low-frequency, and present *during* contraction — not impulsive, not broadband, not coincident with release. The sound of muscle working is a hum; the sound in this practice is a snap. Those are different signal classes and they should not be merged because both are audible.

**The steelman, which is worth keeping.** There *is* a coherent version in which the guard matters: **the guard changes the load on the interface.** A held contraction alters the stress across the soft–rigid contact; its release changes the boundary condition; the interface then slips when the load crosses a threshold. In that version the guard release is the *trigger* and the crack is the interface's response — not the guard releasing. That preserves the intuition while moving the arrow, and it is consistent with §9f's slip-pulse physics and §9g's avalanche dynamics.

**What the cascade structure argues against.** If each crack were a motor-unit de-recruitment, events would track motor events one-to-one, with sizes set by motor-unit size. Cascades are avalanche dynamics — a slip redistributing stress into its neighbours, with power-law sizes. That is a material process, not a recruitment process. §9g's observation is therefore evidence *for* the interface reading and against the direct guard-release reading. The two readings are not merely different in emphasis; the cascade structure is a datum that separates them.

**The better framing: co-effects of a common cause.** The nociceptive drive that holds the guard is generated by the tissue state. Change the tissue state and *both* fall together — the guard releases and the interface frees. The felt simultaneity is real; the causal chain is not. This is the same error as the cavitation reading of §9b, a temporal correlation read as a mechanism, but in the opposite direction: §9b mis-assigned the *sound*, this mis-assigns the *cause of the sound*.

**Supporting evidence that an audible release is a by-product.** In the manipulation literature, joint space increases **whether or not** an audible pop occurs, and no consistent correlation has been established between the sound and outcomes such as pain, mobility or disability. The conclusion stated in that literature is explicit — clinicians "should refrain from using the presence of an audible sound as an indicator of successful joint manipulation," the pop being "a biomechanical byproduct rather than a marker of clinical efficacy." That is for synovial joints, not fascial interfaces, so it establishes the *pattern* rather than the specific case. But it is the pattern this claim is up against.

**A terminological caution.** "Muscle guard" is doing work here that the literature will not fully underwrite. Guarding is a real clinical construct but a loosely operationalised one, and the adjacent spasm / taut-band account has been critically challenged — Quintner, Bove & Cohen (*Rheumatology* 54(3):392, 2015) argue the palpable taut band has not been shown to be a contractile entity. So "the guard released" is a *story* in the sense of §6 — unfalsifiable as stated — while "the range holds when I move again" is a *loop*. The practice should keep the loop and record the story.

**The test that separates the two readings, and they predict oppositely.**

- **If the limiter is the guard:** the new range should be **reversible within minutes** by re-imposing guarding. Voluntarily co-contract the region, or re-drive it nociceptively, and the range should retreat; release, and it should return.
- **If the limiter is the interface:** the range should be **persistent across the session**, should not reverse with voluntary guarding, and should track the cascade events rather than the relaxation.

Opposite predictions from the same observation is what makes this a genuine discriminator — the same structure as the heavy-tail test in §9g. And it is directly instrumentable: **surface EMG**. If the crack is a guard release, there should be an EMG drop coincident with the cascade. If it is an interface slip, EMG should be unchanged. The multimodal rig used to quantify hypertonia — sEMG plus force plus kinematics, synchronised — is exactly the instrument class for this, and it is portable.

**One more thing to check, in the practice itself.** "Recovered" presupposes the range was there before and was being guarded *from*. If it was never available in the adult lifetime, no guard was holding it, and §9c's reading — sensitivity as the signature of newly-mapped territory — applies instead. The two histories predict different things about whether the range was previously reachable, and the practitioner is the only one who can say which one he is in.

**Opinion.** The intuition points at something real — guarding is a genuine limiter and its release is a genuine gain — but it puts the sound in the causal slot, and the sound cannot be there. The crack is an interface event; the guard release is a policy change; they co-occur because the same tissue state drives both. Keep the observation, move the arrow.

## 11. Where this leaves the model

The protocol is the most interesting artefact in the thread, because it was generated behaviourally and it matches a prediction derived independently from tissue physics. That is the right kind of coincidence and it should be recorded as a coincidence, not as proof.

The nutrition list is sound biochemistry attached to appetite science that only partly supports it — salt strongest, protein moderate, the rest unestablished. Copper should be added. The duration claim is defensible for a reason that is the same reason the protocol works: the sub-cortical loop is a functioning regulator. And "follow the feeling" is right where the feeling is a closed loop and unsafe where it is an open story — with the additional hazard that the system generating the directives is, on the model's own account, the system running the stale prior.

The one-line version: **follow the ache, record the story, and let the stool be the measurement.**

**A pattern worth naming, because it has now recurred three times.** The session sequence has three clocks in it — the ache on the neural clock, the range on the mechanical clock, the crack on the interface clock — and the readings offered have repeatedly collapsed them into a single chain. Three candidate mechanisms for the crack have now been tried and set aside: cavitation (§9b, wrong sound class), stick-slip (§9f, superseded by the slip-pulse result), and guard release (§9h, right intuition, wrong causal slot). Each failed the same way: **a real temporal correlation read as a causal arrow.** The sequence is genuinely a sequence; it is not a chain. That distinction is the one to carry into the draft.
