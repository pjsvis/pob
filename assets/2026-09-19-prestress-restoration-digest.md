# Prestress Restoration in the Wild — A Digest

*Persisted 19 September 2026. A literature sweep answering three questions posed against `briefs/2026-07-13-brief-the-floor-as-calibration-datum.md`: (1) does anything in the wild describe a biological system reducing excess stress and returning to an optimum pre-stress level; (2) does the floor-as-global-calibration-rig claim survive contact with the literature; (3) does the "floor reduces noise so the pre-conscious systems can do maintenance" claim survive. This is research material (the yap), not voiced text. No voice pass has run.*

---

## The short version

**On (1): yes, but the field splits cleanly into two registers that are not equally load-bearing.**

- There is a *rigorous* mechanobiology literature on prestress homeostasis — cells regulate an endogenous tensile prestress to a set point, within a range, via a feedback loop, and the loop's failure is a hallmark of senescence. That literature is quantitative and falsifiable. It stops at the cell.
- There is a *speculative* whole-body literature that uses the same word "prestress" for the myofascial system. Its single load-bearing paper is Bertolucci 2011 — and Bertolucci's abstract uses the exact phrase the claim needs: *"modulating the pre-stress state of the myofascial system."* That paper is an opinion piece. Its most thorough critic calls it "obscurant, bloviating bafflegab."
- The gap between the two registers is where the claim lives. Nothing in the wild has measured a whole-body fascial prestress set point, because no instrument does that.

**On (2): the metaphor is good and it is half-true. The half that is false is the important half.** The floor is a *position* datum, not a *tension* datum. It can re-zero where the segments are. It cannot measure or set the prestress. There is a partial rescue (contact pressure is a weak readout of the internal tension field), but it does not carry the weight the word "calibration rig" puts on it.

**On (3): the noise claim is right in spirit and wrong in mechanism as usually stated.** Lying still does not reduce tissue tension — muscle is thixotropic and gets *stiffer* with stillness. What drops is *motor* noise (postural control off, fusimotor drive down, reafference removed). That is a real and large effect. But it means the passive idle state alone cannot restore a clean position sense — it has to be followed by movement, because movement is what breaks the thixotropic bonds. **This resolves the open active-vs-passive tension in the brief** (brief lines 63, 110): it is not a choice between two methods, it is a sequence, and the sequence order is forced by the tissue physics.

## Part 1 — What is actually in the wild

### 1a. The rigorous register: prestress is a real regulated quantity, at the cell

**Rashid & Wang (2026), "Cytoskeletal prestress homeostasis is a biological principle," *Mechanobiology in Medicine*.** The strongest thing in the sweep. Proposes that regulating endogenous cytoskeletal tensile stress *within a physiological range* is a foundational biological principle, alongside the central dogma and metabolism. The quantified evidence:

- Human airway smooth muscle cells hold a **baseline prestress of ~1000 Pa**, regulable down to ~300 Pa (relaxing agonists) and up to ~2000 Pa (contractile agonists) **without changing cell spreading area**. A set point with a working range, measured.
- Higher prestress → higher stiffness; prestress sets the *speed* of mechanotransduction (Src/Rac activation at 100–300 ms, ~100× faster than growth-factor signalling) and the *distance* over which stress propagates (tens of µm, decaying ~1/r rather than ~1/r²).
- Restoration is described as a homeostatic feedback loop: myosin II contractility, actin dynamics, crosslinkers (plectin, ACF7, α-actinin), the LINC complex, integrins, and nuclear sensors (LAP2β, nuclear pore complexes as the mechanomemory element).
- **The failure states are the evidence.** Senescent cells break the mechano-adaptive loop and show either insufficient prestress or dysregulated excess prestress; the authors call this "a hallmark of the senescent state." Tumour-repopulating cells are *soft with low prestress*. Pre-apoptotic cells drop 3D prestress to zero before apoptosis is measurable.
- Explicit falsifier, stated by the authors: sustained deviation without restoration would refute the principle.

This is the real thing. It is also, note carefully, **at the cell scale**. It is not a claim about the whole body.

Adjacent rigorous material at the tissue scale: **tensional homeostasis** (Bissell and the ECM-remodelling tradition; Humphrey's mechanotransduction-and-ECM-homeostasis review, *Nat Rev Mol Cell Biol* 2014), and **Frost's mechanostat** — bone modelled as a feedback controller holding strain inside a window, with a set point, a dead zone, and a disuse-driven lower threshold. Frost is the cleanest existing precedent for "a tissue that regulates its own loading state toward a target."

### 1b. The speculative register: the whole-body claim, and its one paper

**Scarr, Blyum, Levin & de Solórzano (2025), "Biotensegrity is the super-stability hypothesis for biology," *BioSystems* 256:105569.** The current state of the whole-body biotensegrity argument. Its framing: anatomy as "the physical representation of a hugely complex pattern of interacting force vectors," stability and motion "controlled from within the structure itself," governed by "the homeostatic algorithm of super-stability." The abstract asserts intrinsic stability and energy-efficiency across "every heterarchical level from viruses to vertebrates."

Read it as a *framework paper*, not a measurement. It names homeostasis and super-stability as the organising principles; it does not report a whole-body prestress set point, because none has been measured. This is the ceiling of the biotensegrity literature's evidential strength on this specific question.

**Bertolucci (2011), "Pandiculation: nature's way of maintaining the functional integrity of the myofascial system?," *J Bodyw Mov Ther* 15(3):268–80.** The direct hit. Abstract, verbatim:

> "This paper explores the hypothesis that the SYS [stretch-yawning syndrome] might also have an auto-regulatory role regarding the locomotor system: to maintain the animal's ability to express coordinated and integrated movement by **regularly restoring and resetting the structural and functional equilibrium of the myofascial system**… It is argued here that pandiculation might preserve the integrative role of the myofascial system by (a) developing and maintaining appropriate physiological fascial interconnections and (b) **modulating the pre-stress state of the myofascial system** by regularly activating the tonic musculature."

This is, so far as the sweep can tell, **the only paper in the wild that puts "pre-stress state of the myofascial system" and a restorative mechanism in the same sentence.** If the claim needs a citation for "someone has proposed this," this is it. Pandiculation is described as occurring across most animal species, tied to transitions between cyclic behaviours — especially the sleep–wake rhythm — and as largely involuntary. That is a useful fact in itself: the maintenance behaviour is *built in* and *not consciously driven*, which is exactly what claim (3) asserts about the pre-conscious systems.

**The counterweight — Ingraham, "Does Pandiculation 'Reset' Muscle Tone?" (PainScience).** Essential, and it is the honest reading:

- Bertolucci is "a lot of overheated speculation… thick with advanced-sounded scientific language… often quite simplistic and silly," and "a terrific demonstration of obscurant, bloviating bafflegab."
- The citation chain is degenerate: searches for "pandiculation activates muscle spindles" return "citations… missing entirely… or just pointed to another similar opinion… or to *this one weird paper*: Bertolucci."
- The verdict: "It might, but it's probably no more profound than resetting your thirst with a glass of water." And: "the reset metaphor" is something "alternative medicine and quackery have always had a love affair with."
- The framing that matters most for this project: the reset claim is a **deepity** — "the more prosaic of which is true but trivial, while the most profound and interesting interpretation is false."

Ingraham's critique is not that the physiology is wrong. It is that **the specific, testable claims have not been tested**, and that a plausible-but-trivial regulatory story is being inflated into a profound one. That is the correct epistemic posture to adopt toward this literature, and it should be adopted toward our own version of the claim too.

### 1c. The tissue layer: why "lying still" is not obviously relaxing

Three findings that complicate the claim, all well-established:

- **Muscle thixotropy.** (Lakie & Campbell, "Muscle thixotropy — where are we now?," *J Appl Physiol* 2018.) Resting muscle stiffness is *history-dependent*: stable, slowly-cycling cross-bridge attachments persist at rest and produce the short-range elastic component (SREC) — high stiffness to small slow stretches, breaking down past a "give" threshold. **Rest increases stiffness; movement reduces it.** The transition from posture to movement is mechanically different depending on which state you are in. Implication: a long passive lie-down does not leave the tissue looser. It leaves it *stiffer*, and the first movement has to break through the catch.
- **Preconditioning.** (Schatzmann et al., *Arch Orthop Trauma Surg* 1998, and the standard testing literature.) A tendon or ligament's first stress–strain cycles differ from its steady-state cycles; you must cyclically precondition the specimen before the measurement is repeatable. The tissue has a *loading history* and relaxes into a repeatable equilibrium state only after being cycled. This is a literal, measured "return to a stable pre-stress state," and it is *driven by movement*, not by rest.
- **Residual stress.** (Fung and successors; the arterial opening-angle experiment.) Biological tissue carries locked-in residual stress — a ring of artery springs open when cut, which proves the intact wall was not stress-free at its unloaded state. Tissue has a built-in prestress that is structural, not neural, and is not something a rest position can zero out.

### 1d. The neural layer: the two sub-claims of "calibration" are separately real

**Proske & Gandevia (2012), "The Proprioceptive Senses," *Physiol Rev* 92(4):1651–97.** The authoritative decomposition. Position sense, movement sense, sense of effort, sense of force, and sense of heaviness are *distinct* sub-modalities with distinct mediation — muscle spindles for position/movement, efference copy and corollary discharge for effort. The dissociation literature (deafferented subjects) shows effort and position can come apart. **This directly supports the brief's "lump of clay" reading** (brief lines 51–53): if the sense of effort is a separate channel, then stripping the gravity load removes that channel's contribution while leaving position intact — which is exactly "angle and presence, without the effort pattern." The brief's research item 2 is answered: the decomposition is real and citable.

**Proprioceptive recalibration is a measured, real phenomenon.** Visuo-proprioceptive recalibration is well documented (cursor-rotation and channel paradigms; recalibration is *enduring*, observable in each modality alone, and reaches asymptote within a few reaches). The recalibration is *somatotopically focal* — shifting a fingertip visual cue shifts felt fingertip position, shifts the knuckle less, and does not shift the wrist or elbow at all. **Two things follow.** First, the map genuinely drifts and genuinely re-zeroes — the claim's premise is sound. Second, recalibration is *local to the segment that received the conflicting signal*, which cuts against "global." The floor's effect should be expected to be focal too.

### 1e. The reference-frame layer: contact really does anchor

**Jeka & Lackner's light-touch work.** Fingertip contact with a stationary surface — at forces far too small to provide mechanical support, on the order of a few grams — reduces postural sway substantially, and does so *even in subjects with bilateral vestibular loss*. The contact force is not doing the stabilising; it is supplying a **reference frame**. This is the single strongest empirical support for the floor-as-datum idea in the whole sweep, and it is a well-replicated finding. It is also, note, a *contact* result, not a *lying-down* result, and the effect is largest when the surface is stationary and rigid.

**Graviceptive reference frames are recalibrated by experience.** The subjective visual vertical (SVV) is the standard readout of the internal estimate of gravity; it is tilted by head/body roll and shifts in vestibular disorder. Post-spaceflight, astronauts show measurable postural and sensory-reweighting deficits that resolve over days to weeks as the sensorimotor system re-adapts to 1g — a body recalibrating its spatial map against a gravitational reference, documented. There is even a countermeasure literature proposing proprioceptive/tactile interventions specifically to keep the system "tuned."

**Sensory reweighting.** Postural control continuously reweights vision, vestibular, and somatosensory channels by their reliability. Removing the postural task (lying down) removes the reweighting problem. And postural control demonstrably consumes attentional resources — dual-task paradigms show cognitive load and postural control competing. This is the cleanest support for the *cost* half of the brief's cost-calibration-tracking claim (brief line 35).

### 1f. The maintenance layer: the pre-conscious systems do have maintenance

This half of claim (3) is the best-supported part of the whole model, though the literature describes it as sleep- and activity-driven, not floor-driven:

- **Offline replay and consolidation.** Neuronal activity patterns from awake experience are replayed during sleep; motor memory consolidates offline; targeted memory reactivation during sleep can enhance it. This is literally a pre-conscious system doing maintenance on movement representations while the body is still.
- **Glymphatic clearance.** Sleep drives clearance of metabolic waste from the CNS via the glymphatic system; the current review literature treats this as a core restorative function, distinct from energy conservation.
- **Pandiculation as built-in maintenance.** Bertolucci's own framing — an auto-regulatory behaviour, conserved across most animal species, tied to sleep–wake transitions and largely involuntary. Ingraham's fair concession: it is "plausible and intriguing at a high level, a fun idea, and probably worthy of research attention."
- **Bone's mechanostat and targeted remodelling** — maintenance of a loaded structure, running continuously, unconsciously.

So: the body does run unconscious maintenance on movement and structure. **The claim needs the modifier that the wild evidence ties it to sleep and to activity, not to floor contact.** Floor contact is an *additional* hypothesis on top of an established one.

### 1g. The engineering analogies — where the metaphor came from and where it breaks

The floor-as-datum idea is native to engineering, and the analogies are genuinely clarifying. In descending order of usefulness:

1. **Inertial navigation stationary alignment.** A strapdown INS cannot navigate until it has *aligned* — and alignment requires the system be **stationary on the ground**. Fine alignment uses zero-velocity updates (ZUPT): the system is *known* to be still, that known-zero is the observation, and the accumulated drift in velocity and attitude is estimated and compensated. **This is the exact structure of the claim**: known-stillness + known-ground = a correction of accumulated error. If the claim wants an engineering precedent, this is it, and it is a strong one.
2. **IMU accelerometer calibration.** Static six-position calibration against the gravity vector: place the sensor in known static orientations on a level surface, and the gravity vector is the standard. The floor literally is a calibration rig for accelerometers — but note it calibrates *attitude and scale factor*, not force. The analogy carries the position half of the claim and not the tension half.
3. **SLAM loop closure.** Revisiting a known location collapses accumulated drift. Same structure, and it makes the point that a *revisit* is what corrects drift — you have to go back to the datum.
4. **Metrology surface plate / datum plane.** The granite surface plate is the reference from which all other measurements are taken. But a surface plate is *certified* — flat and level to a stated tolerance. A domestic floor is flat to a few mm over a few metres at best, and is frequently out of level by a degree or more (17 mm/m). The floor is an *uncertified* datum.
5. **The break in the analogy.** A calibration rig works by comparison against a **standard of the quantity being calibrated**. The floor supplies a standard for *position* (z ≈ 0). It supplies no standard for *tension*. You cannot calibrate a spring constant against a flat surface.

## Part 2 — The floor-as-global-calibration-rig claim, tested

### What the metaphor gets right

- **A plane is a field constraint, not a point constraint.** Every other intervention available — massage, stretch, manipulation, dry needling — is local: it perturbs one region. The floor imposes a boundary condition on **many segments simultaneously**. That is the sense in which "global" is the right instinct: it is the only commonly available surface that constrains a large fraction of the body at once, in a single operation, with no operator and no skill.
- **The contact set is the antigravity chain.** Supine puts the posterior surface — the structures that carry the postural load all day — against the datum. It is not global, but it is *targeted at the loaded chain*, which is arguably better than global would be.
- **It converts absolute localisation into relative localisation.** The brief's argument (brief lines 29–31) is sound and is the strongest part of the model. A pendular segment needs a world-frame solution; a segment at z ≈ 0 needs only a body-relative solution. The floor does supply the frame.
- **The light-touch result is real evidence for the anchoring mechanism.** Not for the whole claim, but for the specific mechanism that contact supplies a reference frame. This is the claim's best empirical footing.

### Where it stops — the load-bearing objection

**The floor is a position datum, not a tension datum.** The claim as stated does two jobs with one word. "Calibration" implies measuring against a standard and correcting. The floor can do that for *where the segments are*. It cannot do it for *how much prestress the system carries*, because:

- No in-vivo instrument measures whole-body fascial prestress. Shear-wave elastography and myotonometry measure *local* stiffness in a *specific* muscle under *specific* conditions, with real inter-operator variability. There is no whole-system readout to calibrate *against*.
- A rig calibrates a quantity against a standard of that quantity. A flat surface is a standard for position. There is no flat surface equivalent for tension.
- So the mechanism the first question asks about — returning to an *optimum pre-stress level* — is exactly the part the floor metaphor does not deliver.

**The partial rescue, which is worth keeping.** Contact pressure *is* a readout of the internal tension field. A tensegrity resting on a plane distributes its weight over the contact patch according to its internal force distribution; change the prestress and the pressure map changes. The body has mechanoreceptors (Ruffini, Merkel, Pacinian, and the fascial receptors) that sample that map. So the floor does supply a *weak, low-resolution, indirect* tension readout — not a calibration standard, but a signal.

And this supplies a **mechanism for a claim the repo already makes without one**. `maintenance-burden-equation.md:37` asserts that "mattress sleeping bypasses the nightly re-computation" — "the compliant surface absorbs the perturbation before the eigenstructure can be read." The digest can now say *why*: **a compliant surface smears the pressure distribution, and the pressure distribution is the only readout of the internal tension field.** The mattress does not fail to support; it destroys the signal. That is a testable mechanistic claim, and it is a genuine addition to the repo.

### The correction to make

Replace "global calibration rig" with something the evidence carries:

> The floor is a **large-area position datum with a weak pressure readout** — the only commonly available constraint that touches the antigravity chain at once. It re-zeros location and supplies a low-resolution map of the tension field. It does not measure or set prestress.

"Global" should be retired for the same reason the brief retired the height-monotonic SNR version (brief line 23): it is the version that over-claims and will be the one a critic attacks. The defensible version is narrower and still interesting.

## Part 3 — The noise claim, tested

### What it gets right

Four distinct noise sources genuinely drop when you lie on the floor, and they are separable:

1. **Postural motor noise.** The antigravity muscles stop firing to hold the body up. Since muscle spindle discharge is dominated by fusimotor (γ) drive, removing the postural task removes a large, continuous, self-generated afferent signal. This is probably the largest single term.
2. **Reafference.** Self-generated movement produces sensory input that must be predicted and cancelled (efference copy / sensory attenuation — a well-established phenomenon, necessary for attenuating self-generated touch). With the body still, there is no reafference to cancel, so no cancellation error to inject.
3. **Vestibular noise.** The otoliths still report 1g (the reference is unchanged), but the semicircular canals go quiet — no head movement, no angular signal.
4. **Attentional cost.** Postural control competes for attentional resources; removing the task frees them. This is the brief's $E_{\text{computational}}$ term with an actual literature behind it.

### Where the usual statement is wrong

**"Lying on the floor reduces the noise level" conflates motor noise with tissue tension, and the tissue does the opposite of what the claim implies.** Muscle is thixotropic: stillness *increases* resting stiffness as stable cross-bridges accumulate (Lakie & Campbell 2018). The SREC means the rested muscle is *stiffer and catchier*, not looser. So:

- The claim is **right** that a noise floor drops — the motor/attentional/reafferent noise floor.
- The claim is **wrong** if it implies the tissue goes slack. It does not. It gets stiffer, and the stiffness is load-bearing: a stiffer, catchier muscle has a *different* spindle response to a small perturbation, which degrades small-signal position acuity. The resting state is not a clean slate; it is a biased one.

### The resolution — and the answer to the brief's open question

This is the most useful thing in the sweep, because it converts the brief's live tension into a forced ordering.

The brief's open question (brief line 63) is whether the *passive* idle-state (ch18's method: "do not try to move it") and the *active* pandiculation (the arm routine) are complements or competitors. The thixotropy result answers it:

> **Passive rest alone cannot produce a clean position sense, because stillness increases thixotropic stiffness and biases the small-signal response. Movement is required to break the bonds. Therefore the two operations are not alternatives to be chosen between — they are a forced sequence, and the order is: passive first, active second.**

- **Passive phase** removes the load and the motor noise. It restores *presence/ownership* (the region comes back online) and drops the threat level — exactly as ch18 says. It cannot restore *precise position*, because the tissue is now stiffer.
- **Active phase (pandiculation)** breaks the thixotropic bonds by sweeping the joint through loaded↔unloaded. It restores *position* — and the preconditioning literature says it needs to be *cyclic* and repeated to reach the repeatable equilibrium, which matches the "slow sweep" description in the arm routine.

This makes the brief's proposed reconciliation (line 63) the right one, and it upgrades it from "evidence suggests complement" to "tissue physics forces the sequence." It also predicts the brief's test (line 110) in a specific direction: **passive-only should improve presence and *not* improve joint position sense — and may transiently worsen it**, because the thixotropic state has had time to set. That is a falsifiable prediction with a stated mechanism, which is a stronger position than the brief currently holds.

It also supplies a mechanism for why pandiculation and not passive stretching is the right operation: passive stretch does not break cross-bridges the way active contraction-relaxation cycling does (the cross-bridge detachment is load- and history-dependent). Bertolucci's paper says "regularly activating the tonic musculature" — and on this reading, the *activation* is the operative word, not the stretch.

## Part 4 — Falsifiers and the experiments that would actually settle it

The brief asks for research; the more valuable output is the test. Ranked by cost-to-decisiveness ratio:

### The decisive, cheap experiment: position sense after floor vs mattress

**Design.** Within-subject. Measure elbow (or shoulder) joint position sense error by active matching or passive-to-active matching, before and after 15 minutes in each of three conditions, counterbalanced across days:

1. Supine on a **hard floor** (rigid contact, low compliance).
2. Supine on a **mattress** (same posture, same duration, compliant contact).
3. Supine on a **rigid board** raised off the floor (contact present, floor absent).

**Prediction of the model:** matching error drops most after (1), least after (2), intermediate after (3). If (2) and (3) match (1), the effect is *rest*, not *datum* — and the floor claim dies. If (1) and (3) both beat (2), the effect is *contact rigidity*, and the "floor" is doing the work through compliance, not through being a global reference. Only if (1) > (3) is there something floor-specific.

This is non-invasive, cheap, uses standard proprioception methodology, and the mattress condition is a natural control that the repo's own model already predicts the direction of. **The repo already asserts the mattress is worse (`maintenance-burden-equation.md:37`); this tests it.**

### The tension-datum test: pressure mapping

Lie supine on a pressure mat. The model predicts that the pressure distribution is a readout of the internal tension field, and that a compliant surface smears it. **Measure:** spatial entropy / peak pressure / contact area of the pressure map on hard floor vs mattress, before and after a floor session. If the map is not more structured on the hard surface, the readout mechanism does not exist and the "partial rescue" above is void.

### The tone test: is anything actually "releasing"?

**Shear-wave elastography or myotonometry** of a chosen muscle (masseter, upper trapezius, erector spinae) at rest, before and after. Thixotropy predicts a *specific* and counterintuitive result: passive rest **increases** measured stiffness. If that is observed, the "lying on the floor reduces tension" framing is dead in its naive form and must be restated as the sequence above. If resting stiffness *drops*, the thixotropy reading is wrong for this context and needs revisiting.

Cheaper proxy: **EMG at rest** and **passive joint torque during a slow controlled ramp** (the SREC shows up as a characteristic catch-and-give in the torque trace, and its presence or absence is directly observable).

### The maintenance test

The maintenance claim is the least testable and should be held most loosely. The closest measurable proxies: resting metabolic rate by indirect calorimetry (the repo's own falsifier at `maintenance-burden-equation.md:88`), and cognitive battery performance before/after (line 92). Both are already stated. The sweep adds nothing better, which is itself information: **the maintenance claim is currently a model with a plausible literature behind it and no direct test.** It should be marked as such.

### What would falsify the whole model

- If joint position sense after floor rest is no better than after mattress rest → the datum claim fails.
- If the pressure map is no more structured on a hard surface → the readout mechanism fails.
- If resting stiffness decreases with stillness → the thixotropy-based sequencing fails.
- If contact-based recalibration is not somatotopically focal → the "local chart" analogy is wrong and the effect is something else.

**The one-line read.** The model is a good model with a bad name for its central mechanism. The literature supports: prestress as a regulated quantity (at the cell), contact as a reference frame (measured), rest as a reducer of motor and attentional noise (measured), and unconscious maintenance (measured). The literature does not support: a whole-body prestress set point, or the floor as a rig that can calibrate one. The strongest move available is to stop claiming the floor calibrates the *prestress* and start claiming it calibrates the *map* — which is defensible, testable, and consistent with everything above.

## Part 6 — The phase-change and remodelling claims (added the same day)

Three further claims were put against the model: that lying on the floor incentivises exploration of positions (back, side, a long fascial chain laid along the datum); that the pressure and warmth drive the thixotropic elements into a liquid phase; and that this provides a remodelling opportunity. Taken in order.

### 6a. The incentive to explore — plausible, and structurally motivated

This one has a mechanism available that does not require the floor to be doing anything mysterious.

The brief's own queue logic (brief lines 55, 74: "the unanchored adjacent segment is the next DOF… work outward from the anchor") predicts a queue *within* a position. The same logic predicts a queue *across* positions, because a plane constrains only one side. Supine calibrates the posterior chain and leaves the anterior chain off the datum; the anterior chain is now the salient, uncalibrated thing, and the natural move is to roll. Side-lying puts the lateral line on the datum and leaves both front and back off it. **The constraint is always incomplete, and the incompleteness is what generates the next position.** The floor does not need to invite exploration; it cannot avoid generating a queue of unresolved surfaces.

The "long fascial chain laid against the floor" is the strongest form of this and is worth separating from the weaker versions. A point intervention (massage, a needle, a roller) constrains a point. A plane constrains a *set of points*. A fascial chain laid along a plane is constrained along a *line* — its whole length at once. That is a qualitatively different constraint, and it is the only way to put a one-dimensional structure onto a one-dimensional datum.

**But the chain is a map, not a measured structure.** Huijing's work establishes myofascial force transmission beyond the tendon (epimuscular and extramuscular pathways) and is cited for exactly that in Bertolucci's abstract. The *named* chains — Superficial Front Line, Lateral Line, Spiral Line — are Myers' anatomical heuristics. They are useful organising vocabulary and they are not measured anatomical units. "Laying a long fascial chain against the floor" therefore operates on a map whose units have not all been demonstrated. That does not make it useless; it makes it a hypothesis about which lines exist to be laid.

**Two competing explanations should be named, because they predict different things.** (i) Plain discomfort: a hard floor hurts after a few minutes and you shift. (ii) Novelty: a new contact set is new afferent data, and the system attends to it. The queue account predicts shifts that are *directed* — you move toward the surface that has just become salient. Discomfort predicts undirected shifting, or quitting. Novelty predicts shifting toward wherever the signal is strongest, which need not be the salient segment. **These are separable by self-report alone** (log the sequence of positions and what prompted each move), which makes this the cheapest of the model's claims to test.

### 6b. The phase change — the target is real, the mechanism is wrong in three ways

There is a real, shear-sensitive, history-dependent matrix in the right anatomical place, and the claim is pointing at it. Three corrections are needed before it can be stated.

**1. Thixotropy is not a phase change.** Thixotropy is a reversible, *isothermal* change in microstructure — entanglement density, aggregate size, network connectivity — under shear, recovering at rest. The material stays in one phase; its internal arrangement reorganises. A phase transition is a different thing, with a latent heat and a coexistence line. And on the specific "gel to sol" story: native hyaluronan does **not** undergo a sol/gel transition at body temperature. The sol/gel HA literature achieves it by *chemically grafting* a thermoresponsive polymer (NIPAM) onto the HA backbone and varying the graft ratio — a synthetic construct, not native physiology. "The ground substance goes liquid at body temperature" is therefore not a supported claim about human tissue.

**2. The trigger is shear, not pressure.** This is the load-bearing error, and it is the same error as the previous section's noise claim: stillness being credited with an effect only movement produces. Normal pressure — which is all a body lying on a plane applies — compresses a layer; it does not shear it. Shear requires *relative sliding* between adjacent layers. Normal or hydrostatic stress alone does not reduce a thixotropic fluid's viscosity. **Lying still cannot do this. Only movement can.**

Which produces a cleaner formulation of the floor's role than the original claim: **the floor does not shear anything. It supplies the anchorage that makes shear possible.** Fix one side of a layer against a plane, move the free side against it, and the interface shears. The floor is a *reaction surface*. This is structurally the same contribution the floor makes in the calibration argument — it supplies a frame — so the two arguments now share one mechanism instead of needing two.

The anatomy supports the target: HA-rich layers between deep fascia and muscle facilitate gliding between them (Stecco et al.'s *fasciacytes*, 2018 — a cell class devoted to producing that HA layer), and reduced gliding with HA accumulation is the clinical picture called densification. The right tissue and the right molecule are in play. The trigger is movement, not contact.

**3. The warmth runs the wrong way.** The felt temperature of a surface is governed by its **thermal effusivity** — how fast it draws heat — not by its actual temperature. That is the standard explanation for why a metal surface at room temperature feels colder than a wooden one at the same temperature. A hard floor feels *cold* precisely because it conducts heat away from the contact region efficiently; a mattress or rug feels warm because it insulates and lets the body's own heat accumulate. **So the warmth argument favours the mattress over the floor, not the floor over the mattress.**

The effect size is small even where it exists: the tissue is already at body temperature, and a local shift of a degree or two changes viscosity modestly rather than switching a state.

### 6c. The remodelling opportunity — narrowed, and the narrowing is decisive

Here the claim meets its hardest constraint, and it is quantitative.

**Structural collagen barely turns over at all.** Racemization measurements put the half-life of collagen at **117 years in cartilage** (Verzijl et al., *JBC* 2000) and **197.5 ± 18.2 years in equine tendon** (Sivan et al.), with 95–215 years reported for intervertebral disc. The collagen scaffold in tendon and fascia is, for practical purposes, permanent. **No floor session remodels collagen. Nothing does, on any human timescale, short of injury and repair.**

**The matrix does turn over, and fast.** The half-life of hyaluronan in tissue is **less than one day to several days** (Laurent & Fraser, *J Intern Med* 1992). That is roughly six orders of magnitude faster than collagen, and it is the correct target for anything a session might do.

So the claim survives only in a narrowed form, and the narrowing is not a quibble — it decides what the practice is *for*:

> A floor session offers a **matrix** remodelling opportunity — the HA-rich gliding layer, water, and the cross-linking state — measured in hours to days. It offers **no** collagen remodelling opportunity. The scaffold is not on the table.

Two further constraints, both of which the original claim misses:

**The mechanism would defeat its own conclusion.** Remodelling is driven by *load*: the cell reads mechanical strain and responds. A matrix that had genuinely liquefied could not transmit load, and a liquid matrix transmits nothing. So the "liquid state" as stated would abolish the very signal that drives the remodelling it is supposed to enable. The useful state is not liquid; it is a matrix that *glides* — low shear resistance between layers, so that load is transmitted *along* the fibres rather than dissipated at the interfaces. Gliding is the goal; liquidity is not.

**Remodelling follows habitual load, not transient load.** Tissue adapts to what it experiences most of the time. Fifteen minutes on a floor followed by eight hours at a desk is swamped by the desk, and this is the strongest objection to the whole model. Which points at the version that survives: **the session does not remodel directly — it changes the configuration from which the rest of the day's loading proceeds.** That is precisely the repo's own diurnal-budget frame (`maintenance-burden-equation.md`): pay a small cost at night to avoid paying a large one during the day. On this reading the floor session is the *set-point adjustment* and the remodelling is what the following sixteen hours do with it. It also explains why the practice must be daily rather than occasional — something the model already asserts and now has a reason for.

### 6d. A practice-vocabulary problem: the rug

Chapter 15 calls the practice "the Rug Sandbox" (chapter-15 line 18). A rug is **compliant** and **insulating**, and those two properties point in opposite directions from the model's own mechanisms:

- Compliant contact **smears the pressure distribution**, which is the only readout of the internal tension field (Part 2). The rug is bad for the mechanism that works.
- Insulating contact **retains heat**, which is the mechanism just proposed. The rug is good for the mechanism that does not work.

The rug is therefore optimised for the wrong half. If the pressure readout is doing the work, the practice wants a **hard, rigid, uncushioned surface** — which is uncomfortable, which is presumably why the rug is there. That tension is real and the vocabulary should not paper over it. Either the rug stays and the pressure-readout mechanism is demoted, or the rug goes and the practice gets less comfortable. This is a decision, not a detail.
