Here is a proposed operational framework for the **CCV (Clarity, Consistency, Veracity) Index**, designed for a "fail-fast" vector search.


### I. The "Fail Fast" Filters (The Sieve)
Before applying a granular scale, we apply binary "tripwires" to immediately discard vector configurations that are "fucked-adjacent". If a model output triggers these, it scores a **0** and we move to the next vector.


1. **Verbosity Tripwire:** Does the token count exceed $x$ for a simple query? $\rightarrow$ **FAIL** (Too wordy).
2. **Structure Tripwire:** Does it fail to use requested formatting (e.g., Markdown)? $\rightarrow$ **FAIL** (Low control).
3. **Refusal Tripwire:** Does it incorrectly trigger a refusal/safety lecture? $\rightarrow$ **FAIL** (Broken alignment).

### II. The CCV Measurement Scale (0-5)
For survivors of the Sieve, we apply this 3-dimensional scoring.


#### **C1: Clarity (Signal-to-Noise)**

- **5 (Crystal):** High information density. Zero fluff. Perfect syntax.
- **3 (Murky):** Understandable but verbose. Contains "filler" phrases (e.g., "It is important to note...").
- **1 (Opaque):** Rambling, circular logic, or jargon-heavy without definition.

#### **C2: Consistency (Reproducibility)**
*Requires generating the same output $n$ times (e.g., 3 runs).*


- **5 (Deterministic):** Identical logic and structure across runs. Minor phrasing variations only.
- **3 (Drifting):** Core logic holds, but structure or tone varies significantly.
- **1 (Volatile):** Contradictory answers or wildly different formatting across runs.

#### **C3: Veracity (Grounding)**

- **5 (Empirical):** Fully aligned with provided facts/context. No hallucinations.
- **3 (Approximate):** Mostly correct but misses nuance or invents minor filler details.
- **1 (Hallucinatory):** Confidently asserts falsehoods or misinterprets the source text.

### III. Search Strategy: "Limit-to-Locus"
You mentioned exploring the "limits of the numbers." This suggests a **Bracketing Strategy**:


1. **Range Finding (The Extremes):** Push the steering vectors to their max/min until the model breaks (gibberish or silence). This defines the "Safe Envelope."
2. **Bisection Search:** Test the midpoint of the Safe Envelope.

- *If High CCV:* We have found a candidate **Max CCV Area**.
- *If Low CCV:* We discard that half.
3. **Gradient Ascent:** Once a "High CCV" point is found, we micro-step around it to determine the **Granularity**.

### IV. Determining Topology (The "Sweet Spot" Analysis)
This answers your question about whether the area is "large or small" (robust vs. brittle).


- **The Plateau (Ideal):** A broad range of vector settings where CCV remains at 5/5/5. This indicates a **Robust Configuration**. We want to find the center of this plateau.
- **The Peak (Acceptable):** A single point of 5/5/5 that degrades to 3/3/3 with slight changes. This is **Brittle** but usable if locked down.
- **The Archipelago (Complex):** Multiple disconnected islands of high performance. This implies the model has distinct "modes" of clarity.
