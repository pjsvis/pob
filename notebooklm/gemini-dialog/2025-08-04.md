## Cello-Adjacent Performances and Activities in Ottawa
### The Crown Jewel: Puccini's *Tosca* in Concert
The undisputed highlight in Ottawa during this period is the National Arts Centre Orchestra's season-opening production of Puccini's *Tosca*. Presented as a semi-staged opera in concert on September 10 and 12 in Southam Hall, this event features the NACO under the baton of its music director, Alexander Shelley.[7, 19, 20]


This is a premier event for any lover of the cello. In Puccini's masterful score, the cello section is far more than mere accompaniment; it is a primary dramatic voice, articulating the opera's core themes of passion, political tyranny, and tragedy. The work's opening, with the powerful, menacing chords associated with the villain Scarpia, relies on the weight and darkness of the low strings. The cello section carries some of the most beautiful and heartbreaking melodic lines in the repertoire, providing the lush, romantic underpinning for Cavaradossi's famous arias, most notably the Act III lament *E lucevan le stelle*. Throughout the dramatic confrontations of Act II, the cellos are instrumental in building and sustaining an almost unbearable level of tension. This performance offers a chance to hear one of the great orchestral cello parts in the operatic canon performed by a top-tier orchestra.


### A Worthy Excursion: Bach's *Magnificat* in Arnprior
On September 7, the organization CAMMAC (Canadian Amateur Musicians Musiciens Amateurs du Canada) presents a concert in nearby Arnprior featuring Johann Sebastian Bach's celebrated *Magnificat*, alongside other classical works.[19] For the dedicated enthusiast willing to make the short journey from Ottawa, this event offers a chance to appreciate the cello in a foundational role. In Baroque music, the cello is an indispensable part of the *basso continuo*. Paired with a keyboard instrument like the harpsichord or organ, the cello provides the continuous bass line that is the harmonic and rhythmic anchor for the entire ensemble. In a large-scale choral work like the *Magnificat*, the cello's role is vital, driving the rhythm in the exuberant choruses and providing a sensitive, supportive voice in the solo arias.
