### Identifying Edible Mulberries
To determine if mulberries are ripe and good to eat, observe their colour and how easily they come off the tree. Ripe mulberries are typically a deep, dark purple or black, although some varieties can be white or red when mature. They should be plump and juicy. A key indicator of ripeness is that the berries will detach from the stem with only a gentle touch. If you have to pull firmly, they are likely not ready.


Unripe mulberries, which are often green or red, can have an unpleasant, sour taste and may cause stomach upset. Therefore, it is always best to select the darkest and most easily picked berries.


### Mulberry Trees in the UK
While not as common as some other fruit trees, mulberry trees (genus *Morus*) do grow in the UK. They have a long history in the country, with some ancient specimens found in London parks and gardens, originally planted in an attempt to start a silk industry. The two main types you might encounter are the Black Mulberry (*Morus nigra*) and the White Mulberry (*Morus alba*). The Black Mulberry is more commonly grown for its fruit in the UK.


Identifying a mulberry tree can be done by observing its leaves, which are often heart-shaped with serrated edges, and can sometimes be lobed. The bark of older trees is often gnarled and rugged.


### Mulberries and Dogs

However, it is crucial to only give dogs ripe mulberries. Unripe mulberries can cause stomach problems and, in some cases, may even have mild hallucinogenic effects in dogs. As with any new food, it's always best for pet owners to introduce it in moderation.
