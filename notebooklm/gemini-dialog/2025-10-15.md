### User

How would we fine tune an llm on a set of core directives? would we need to generate questions and answers? couldn’t we just embed the directives in the LLM like a system prompt

### Gemini

That's an excellent question, and it gets to the heart of how fine-tuning fundamentally works. While it seems intuitive to simply provide the LLM with a list of directives, similar to a system prompt, the process of permanently embedding these rules into the model's behavior requires a different approach. You are correct that you would need to generate a dataset of examples, often in a question-and-answer or instruction-response format.


Here’s a breakdown of why this is the case and how the process works.


### System Prompts vs. Fine-Tuning: Temporary Instruction vs. Permanent Learning
The method of "embedding" directives like a system prompt is a form of **In-Context Learning (ICL)**. When you provide instructions in a prompt, the model uses its existing knowledge to follow those directions for that single interaction. The guidance is temporary and must be provided with every new query.


**Fine-tuning**, on the other hand, is a supervised learning process that permanently alters the model's internal parameters, or "weights". The goal is not just to show the model the rules, but to fundamentally change its default behavior so it follows those rules automatically, without needing to be reminded each time.


### Why LLMs Learn from Examples, Not Just Rules
An LLM doesn't "read" and "understand" a list of abstract directives in the way a human does. The fine-tuning process works by showing the model thousands of concrete examples of the desired behavior and training it to replicate them.


Think of it this way:


- **The Directive:** "Provide clear, actionable, and precise responses."
- **The Learning Process:** The model is given a prompt, like, "I lost my credit card. How can I block it?" It is then shown a "correct" output that exemplifies the directive: "To block your lost credit card, you can log in to your bank's mobile app... or call our 24/7 helpline at 1-800-555-1234...".

During training, the model attempts to generate the response. The training algorithm calculates the difference (the "loss") between the model's attempt and the correct example. It then adjusts the model's internal weights to reduce this difference. By repeating this process with thousands of diverse examples, the model doesn't just memorize answers; it learns the underlying *pattern* of what a "clear, actionable, and precise response" looks like across many different scenarios.


Simply providing the text of the directive itself doesn't create this training signal. The model needs to learn by doing and being corrected, which is only possible with a dataset of high-quality examples.


### How to Translate Core Directives into a Fine-Tuning Dataset
The critical task is to convert your abstract directives into a structured dataset of instruction-output pairs. This is the most important step, as the quality of your dataset directly determines the quality of the fine-tuned model.


Here is a practical workflow:


1. **Deconstruct Your Directives:** Start with a high-level directive, such as "Adopt a professional and helpful tone." Break this down into specific, observable behaviors.


- **Do:** Use formal language, provide step-by-step instructions, explain reasoning.
- **Don't:** Use slang, give vague answers, express personal opinions.
2. **Generate Diverse Scenarios (Instructions/Questions):** Create a wide range of prompts that the model is likely to encounter where these directives would apply. The dataset must cover the full scope of your use case to avoid creating a biased or limited model.


3. **Author High-Quality Outputs (Answers):** For each prompt, write an ideal response that perfectly follows your deconstructed rules. This creates the labeled `(instruction, output)` pairs that form your dataset.


- **Instruction:** "Extract the product details from the description."
- **Input:** "The latest iPhone 15 Pro features a 6.1-inch OLED display, powered by the A17 Pro chip, with 256GB of storage..."
- **Output (demonstrating the directive "be structured and precise"):** `{"Product_Name": "iPhone 15 Pro", "Display": "6.1-inch OLED", "Processor": "A17 Pro chip",...}`.
4. **Structure the Data:** Format these pairs into a consistent structure, such as JSON Lines (JSONL), which is commonly used for fine-tuning. A popular format follows the Alpaca dataset structure, which includes fields for "instruction," "input" (optional context), and "output".


While creating this dataset manually is time-consuming, it is the necessary process for instruction fine-tuning. For larger-scale needs, you can create a smaller "seed set" of a few hundred perfect examples and then use a powerful LLM to synthetically generate thousands more examples that follow the same pattern.


In summary, while a system prompt offers temporary control, fine-tuning achieves permanent behavioral change. This change is accomplished not by giving the model a list of rules to read, but by training it on a comprehensive dataset of question-and-answer examples that demonstrate those rules in action.

### User

The bank of scotland have restricted my access to the bank account. does this mean that the company passes the companies house test for dormancy

### Gemini

That's an important question, but unfortunately, the bank restricting access to the account does not mean the company passes the Companies House test for dormancy.


Here’s a breakdown of why:


1. **The Test is Transaction-Based, Not Access-Based:** Companies House defines a company as dormant if it has had no "significant accounting transactions" in a financial year. This test is based on the financial events that legally must be recorded in your company's books, not on whether you can physically access the bank account to make payments.


2. **The Loan Liability Persists:** The Bounce Back Loan is a legal debt owed by your company. The bank restricting access to the account does not erase this debt. The loan continues to exist as a liability on your company's balance sheet, and interest will likely continue to accrue.


3. **Loan Repayments are the Key Transaction:** As established previously, any repayment of a loan is a "significant accounting transaction". Even if you are currently unable to make these repayments due to the restriction, the underlying obligation and the accrual of interest are still recordable financial events.


In short, the critical factor that prevents the company from being dormant for Companies House purposes is the existence of the Bounce Back Loan itself. The bank's action is a consequence of the company's financial situation, but it doesn't change the fundamental accounting reality that disqualifies it from filing dormant accounts. You must still follow the "Holding Pattern Strategy" outlined in the report, which involves filing full (though simple) statutory accounts.
