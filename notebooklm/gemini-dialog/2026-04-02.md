## The Sensory Channels: Proprioception vs. Interoception
To navigate and tune the biotensegrity structure, the nervous system utilizes two primary sensory channels: proprioception and interoception. While these terms are sometimes used interchangeably in casual movement pedagogy, they represent distinct neurological pathways with different functional goals.[15, 16] Proprioception is the awareness of the body's position and movement in external space, relying on mechanical receptors in the muscles, tendons, and joints to provide feedback on limb location and velocity.[15, 17] It is the "outer" sense that allows one to perform the choreographed steps of a Tai Chi form or walk without looking at one's feet.[4, 15]


Interoception, by contrast, is the "hidden sense" that communicates the internal physiological state of the organism.[16, 18, 19] It encompasses the sensing of heartbeat, respiration, hunger, and, critically for movement therapy, the subtle gradations of internal muscle tension and emotional-physiological states.[15, 16] While Tai Chi teachers frequently emphasize proprioceptive alignment—the "where" of the body—they often fall short in teaching the systematic development of interoception, which is the "how" of the body's internal feeling.[4, 16, 18]

Pandiculation is a three-step neuromuscular re-education process that bypasses the guarding reflex:


1. **Voluntary Contraction**: The practitioner deliberately shortens the already tight muscle, bringing it under conscious control.

3. **Complete Rest/Pause**: A period of stillness allows the nervous system to register the new, lower state of resting tension.[29]

1. **Voluntary Contraction**: The practitioner deliberately shortens the already tight muscle, bringing it under conscious control.[11, 17]

3. **Complete Rest/Pause**: A period of stillness allows the nervous system to register the new, lower state of resting tension.[11, 17]

The "slow return" is the most critical phase because it is during this eccentric lengthening that the "gamma loop"—the feedback circuit that regulates the resting level of muscle tension—is naturally reset.[5, 28] By going as slowly as possible, the practitioner allows the insular cortex to accurately map the change in tension, signaling to the brain's "guard" that it is safe to let go.[16, 26]

## Physiological Anomalies in the Relaxation Phase: Woltman's Sign
The importance of the relaxation phase in Tai Chi is highlighted by its pathological inverse, known as "Woltman's Sign".[41, 42] While Tai Chi practitioners *voluntarily* slow the return to rest to gain interoceptive clarity, certain conditions cause an *involuntary* delay in muscle relaxation.[41, 43] Woltman's sign is classically observed in patients with hypothyroidism (myxoedema), where a deep tendon reflex (such as the Achilles jerk) produces a normal contraction followed by a remarkably slow, "hung-up" return to the neutral position.[42, 43]

Scraping modern dashboards is "grumpy" due to MFA and dynamic rendering.


- **The Best Way:** Use **Playwright** with **Persistent Contexts**. This allows the agent to use a browser profile where you have already manually logged in and solved the MFA once. The agent then simply "re-opens" that session to grab the numbers.
- **Anti-Bot:** We should use `playwright-stealth` to avoid being flagged as a bot by Cloudflare/AWS WAF.

## 3. Security: Charm's Skate
**Yes, we can and should use [Skate](https://github.com/charmbracelet/skate).**
Since you are familiar with the Charm ecosystem, Skate provides a multi-machine, encrypted KV store.


- **Implementation:** The agent calls `skate get providers.anthropic.password` to retrieve the secret only at the moment of execution.
- **Benefit:** This keeps your credentials out of the agent's code, `.env` files, and session logs. It adheres to **OH-077 (Destructive Operation Safeguard)** by keeping the "keys to the kingdom" behind your local SSH/Age key.
