To set up the **Picture-in-Picture (PiP)** view effectively with your external BenQ monitor, you need to treat the OBS "Canvas" like a stage where you are layering your camera on top of your screen.


Since you're using an external monitor, the key is to ensure your "Stage" matches the BenQ's resolution so your code looks sharp.


### 1. Match the "Canvas" to your BenQ
First, let's make sure OBS is recording at the BenQ's native resolution ($1920 \times 1080$):


- Go to **Settings > Video**.
- Set **Base (Canvas) Resolution** to **1920x1080**.
- Set **Output (Scaled) Resolution** to **1920x1080**.
- This ensures your PiP layout fits perfectly without black bars.


---

### 2. Layer the Sources (The Stack)
In the **Sources** dock (bottom of the screen), the order of the list determines what is on "top."


1. **Bottom Layer (The IDE):** Add a **macOS Screen Capture**. In its properties, select your **BenQ Monitor**. If it doesn't fill the screen, right-click it in the preview and select **Transform > Fit to Screen**.
2. **Top Layer (Your Face):** Add your **Video Capture Device** (Facecam). It will likely appear huge and cover your code.


---

### 3. Create the PiP Effect
Once your camera is visible on top of your IDE:


- **Scale:** Click the camera feed in the preview window. Grab one of the **red corner handles** and drag it inward to shrink your face down to a "thumbnail" size.
- **Position:** Click and hold the middle of the camera feed to drag it to a corner (usually bottom-right or top-right).
- **The "Pro" Crop:** If there’s too much dead space around you, hold the **Option (⌥) key** while dragging the red handles. This **crops** the feed (the lines turn green) instead of resizing it, allowing you to create a tight square or vertical rectangle around your head.


---

### 4. Lock the Layout
Once you have the camera exactly where you want it:


- In the **Sources** dock, click the **Lock icon** next to both your Screen Capture and your Camera.
- This prevents you from accidentally clicking and moving your face mid-recording while you're trying to highlight code in your IDE.

**Operational Tip:** If you want your PiP to look "grounded," try to position your IDE's font size or terminal layout so that the PiP doesn't cover the active line of code you're typing. Most developers prefer the **Top Right** corner, as the bottom of the screen often houses terminal outputs or status bars.

how would I setup OBS on my MacBook Air to record a video with a Picture in Picture of me and the rest of the screen being the IDE/terminal

### Gemini

Setting up OBS for a "Picture-in-Picture" (PiP) layout on a MacBook Air is straightforward, but since you're likely dealing with limited screen real estate, the setup requires a bit of intentional layering.


Here is the operational workflow to get your IDE and camera synced up.


### 1. Initial Permissions
macOS is strict about privacy. Before opening OBS, ensure you’ve granted the necessary permissions:


- Go to **System Settings > Privacy & Security**.
- Ensure OBS is toggled **ON** under **Screen Recording** and **Camera**.


---

### 2. Configure Your Sources
In OBS, look at the **Sources** dock at the bottom. You need to stack two specific layers. Think of these like physical sheets of paper; the top item in the list covers the bottom item.


**A. The Base Layer (Your IDE/Terminal)**


1. Click the **+** icon in the Sources dock.
2. Select **macOS Screen Capture**.
3. **Method:** Choose "Window Capture" if you only want to show the IDE, or "Display Capture" if you plan on switching between the terminal and a browser.
4. Select your specific IDE window from the list.

**B. The PiP Layer (Your Camera)**


1. Click the **+** icon again.
2. Select **Video Capture Device**.
3. Name it "Facecam" and select your MacBook’s built-in camera (or external webcam).
4. Once it appears, it will likely cover the whole screen. Grab the **red corners** of the camera feed in the preview window to scale it down and drag it to your preferred corner.


---

### 3. The "Grounded" Setup: Optimization for MacBook Air
Since the MacBook Air uses Apple Silicon (M1/M2/M3), you want to ensure OBS isn't eating up cycles that your IDE needs for compiling or running scripts.


- **Encoder:** Go to **Settings > Output**. Set "Output Mode" to **Advanced**, click the **Recording** tab, and ensure the **Video Encoder** is set to **Apple VT H.264 Hardware Encoder**. This uses the chip's dedicated hardware rather than the CPU.
- **Resolution:** If you have a Retina display, your "Base Canvas" might be huge. Set your **Canvas Resolution** and **Output (Scaled) Resolution** to **1920x1080** in the **Video** tab for a standard, high-quality file that isn't unnecessarily massive.


---

### 4. Audio Check
Don't forget the "Justify Engine" for your audio—make sure your levels justify the output quality:


- **Mic/Aux:** Ensure your "Mic/Aux" bar in the **Audio Mixer** is bouncing when you speak.
- **Desktop Audio:** On macOS, OBS usually needs the "macOS Screen Capture" source to have "Capture Audio" checked within its properties to grab sounds from your terminal or system.

### Pro-Tip: The "Alt-Crop"
If your camera feed shows too much of your room, hold the **Option (⌥) key** while dragging the red bounding box edges in the OBS preview. This will crop the edges of your camera feed instead of scaling the whole image, allowing for a tighter PiP square.
