## The Historical Foundations and the 1988 Statutory Regime
To comprehend the implications of the transition proposed in the correspondence between Kathryn and Peter, it is essential to first analyze the mechanics of the Short Assured Tenancy (SAT) as established under the Housing (Scotland) Act 1988. For nearly three decades, the SAT was the primary vehicle for private renting in Scotland, designed during an era of market liberalization to encourage private investment in housing by providing landlords with a guaranteed exit strategy.[7, 8]


The defining characteristic of the SAT was the inclusion of a fixed term—historically a minimum of six months—and the landlord’s "absolute right" to recover possession at the "ish" (end date) of the tenancy without establishing a specific ground for eviction, provided that a Section 33 notice was served alongside a Notice to Quit.[7, 9] A critical procedural requirement for the creation of an SAT was the service of an AT5 form prior to the signing of the lease; failure to serve this form resulted in the creation of an "Assured Tenancy," which offered significantly higher security for the tenant.[8, 10]
