### 1. The Primacy of Sympathy (Moral Sentiments)
The most effective refutation begins by highlighting that Smith was a Professor of Moral Philosophy, not just an economist.


- **The Argument:** Smith’s economic theories are built upon a foundation of "sympathy" (what we now call empathy).
- **Left-Leaning Appeal:** In *TMS*, Smith argues that human beings have an innate interest in the fortune of others, even if they derive nothing from it but the pleasure of seeing it. This contradicts the "greed is good" caricature often attributed to him by both the right and the left.

### 2. Critique of "Vile" Landlords and Monopolies
Smith was deeply suspicious of the merchant class and concentrated capital.


- **The Argument:** He famously wrote that "People of the same trade seldom meet together... but the conversation ends in a conspiracy against the public."
- **Left-Leaning Appeal:** Smith viewed monopolies and the "vile maxim" of the masters (all for ourselves and nothing for other people) as a distortion of the natural order. He advocated for markets specifically because they could break the power of the landed gentry and the merchant-state collusion (Mercantilism).

### 3. Support for Progressive Taxation and High Wages
Smith’s stance on labor and tax is often more "radical" than his modern champions admit.


- **The Argument:** Smith supported high wages, stating that "No society can surely be flourishing and happy, of which the far greater part of the members are poor and miserable."
- **Left-Leaning Appeal:** He explicitly advocated for a form of progressive taxation, suggesting that "It is not very unreasonable that the rich should contribute to the public expense, not only in proportion to their revenue, but something more than in that proportion."

### 4. Education as a Public Good
Smith recognized the "mental mutilation" caused by the division of labor.


- **The Argument:** He feared that repetitive work would make laborers "as stupid and ignorant as it is possible for a human creature to become."
- **Left-Leaning Appeal:** To counter this, he argued for state-funded education for the common people, a position that aligns more with social democratic values than strictly *laissez-faire* libertarianism.
