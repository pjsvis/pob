Okay, let's break down those terms:


**Polydactyl**


- **Meaning:** "Polydactyl" literally means "many fingers" or "many toes," derived from Greek. In cats, it refers to a congenital physical anomaly where a cat is born with more than the usual number of toes on one or more of its paws.
- **Normal Toe Count:** Typically, cats have 18 toes in total: five on each front paw (including the dewclaw, which is like a thumb) and four on each back paw.
- **Extra Toes:** A polydactyl cat will have at least 19 toes. Some can have many more; the record is 28! The extra toes can appear on any paw, but it's common for them to be on the front paws, sometimes resembling "mittens" or "thumbs."
- **Cause:** This trait is due to a genetic mutation that is passed down through a dominant gene from one of the parents. If a polydactyl cat mates with a non-polydactyl cat, each kitten has about a 50% chance of being polydactyl.
- **Other Names:** Polydactyl cats are sometimes called "Hemingway cats" (after author Ernest Hemingway, who was fond of them), "mitten cats," or "cats with thumbs."
- **Impact:** The extra toes don't usually cause health problems and may even give some cats better balance or dexterity for climbing and jumping.

**Splooting**


- **Meaning:** "Sploot" is a slang term used to describe a specific posture pets, especially cats and dogs, adopt. It's when they lie flat on their stomach with their hind legs stretched out straight behind them, rather than tucked under their body.
- **Appearance:** It's a position that showcases their flexibility and often indicates a state of relaxation.
- **Variations:**

**The classic sploot:** Both hind legs are stretched out directly behind the body.
- There can be other variations, like a "half-sploot" where only one leg is extended.


**Why they do it:**

- **Relaxation and Comfort:** It's often simply a comfortable and relaxed position for them.
- **Stretching:** It allows for a good stretch of the hips and legs.
- **Cooling down:** Spreading out on a cool surface can help them regulate their body temperature, especially in warmer weather.


**Commonality:** While perhaps more famously associated with certain dog breeds (like Corgis), cats are also known to sploot, often in a very graceful manner.
