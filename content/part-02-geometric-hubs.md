# Part II: The Geometric Hubs

If Part I is the substrate, Part II is the architecture. Six chapters, each mapping a specific region of the body as a geometric interface — a place where the continuous fascial envelope changes shape, direction, and function.

The oxter wing (armpit), the sub-occipital kernel (base of the skull), the zero-gravity manifold (the floating sensation of a released spine), the laminated core (abdominal pressure system), the Kua vault (pelvic basin), and the plantar interface (sole of the foot). Each is a hub in the sense that traffic passes through it — forces, signals, constraints — and each has a characteristic failure mode when the traffic jams.

These chapters can be read independently of each other, but they all assume the vocabulary of Part I. The reader who starts here without reading Part I will understand the sensations but not the equations.
