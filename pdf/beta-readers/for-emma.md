# For Emma — the accessibility test

You're reading this book as the person it's written for: an intelligent, curious reader who is not a mathematician or an engineer. You study biology; you think about extremophiles and how living things survive impossible conditions. That's exactly the lens this book needs to pass.

## What to read

1. **The onboarding chapter** (`chapter-00-the-vocabulary.pdf`) — start here. It teaches the book's mathematical language using a wet towel and a t-shirt. If it works for you, the rest of the book is legible. If it doesn't, that's the most important thing you can tell me.
2. **The staved finger essay** (`2026-06-26-staved-finger-tensor-algebra.pdf`) — the piece the whole book is modelled on. A finger injury, treated with tensor algebra.
3. **Chapter 03: The Crystalline Grid** (`chapter-03-the-crystalline-grid.pdf`) — impact dynamics, the one-inch punch. Relevant to biology: how a continuum can instantaneously freeze into a rigid structure.
4. **Chapter 12: The Plantar Interface** (`chapter-12-the-plantar-interface.pdf`) — the foot as a sensory surface, the ground as a data source.

## What to look for

- **The familiar hooks.** Each chapter opens with a sensation you're supposed to recognise from your own body. Do they land? Does the opening make you want to read on, or does it feel like a textbook?
- **The deks.** The one or two sentences that promise what the chapter is about. Do they make sense before you've read the chapter?
- **The math explanations.** The onboarding chapter teaches grad, div, and curl. After reading it, do you feel you understand what those operations *do*, even if you couldn't compute them? Where did the explanation lose you?
- **The smoke tests.** Each chapter ends with a "try this on your own body" exercise. Are they actually doable? Do they invite, or do they instruct?

## What not to worry about

- Whether the tensor notation is rigorous. That's for the engineers.
- Whether you're "qualified" to comment. You are the qualification — the book is written for you. If it loses you, it has failed, and I need to know where.

## The tardigrade thing

If the book's bio-tensegrity framework makes you think of tardigrades — the tun state, survival through a phase change in the tissue matrix — I want to hear that. Cross-species comparisons are not in the book yet, and they might belong there.

## How to respond

Tell me where you put the PDF down. That's the most useful data point — the sentence where you stopped reading. A few paragraphs per chapter is plenty. Don't worry about being nice; worry about being specific.
