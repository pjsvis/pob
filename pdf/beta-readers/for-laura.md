# For Laura — the humanities test

You're reading this book as a writer and an argument-maker, not as a scientist. That's the point. The book has to hold up for someone who reads for the story, the structure, and the quality of the prose — someone who will notice if the argument is circular, if a transition is weak, or if the voice drifts.

## What to read

1. **The staved finger essay** (`2026-06-26-staved-finger-tensor-algebra.pdf`) — the gold standard. This is the voice the whole book is reaching for. Read it first; it calibrates your ear.
2. **The onboarding chapter** (`chapter-00-the-vocabulary.pdf`) — the book's introduction to its own mathematical language, written around a wet towel and a t-shirt.
3. **Chapter 16: The Amnesia Paradox** (`chapter-16-the-amnesia-paradox.pdf`) — the fallacy of deconstruction. This is an argument about historiography as much as biomechanics: why you can't reconstruct a path from its destination.
4. **Chapter 18: The Clavicular Fuse** (`chapter-18-the-clavicular-fuse-and-the-tensor-of-impact.pdf`) — a detective story. It should read as a narrative, not a lecture.

## What to look for

- **The voice.** Does it stay consistent across the four pieces, or does it shift? Where does it shift, and how?
- **The argument.** Does each chapter assert something and then demonstrate it, or does it assert and move on? Where does the evidence not quite carry the claim?
- **The transitions.** Does each chapter earn its move from the familiar hook to the math to the smoke test? Where does the seam show?
- **The prose.** Sentences that are doing work, and sentences that are filling space. The book is trying to be dry and precise, not enthusiastic — flag anything that reads as manic or self-congratulatory.

## What not to worry about

- Whether the math is correct. That's Barbara and Craig's job.
- Whether the equations render. They do.
- Whether you "get" the tensor notation. The onboarding chapter is there to give you enough to read the rest; if it fails at that, tell me — that's the most useful feedback you can give.

## How to respond

A few paragraphs per chapter is plenty. Tell me where you stopped believing, where you noticed the voice change, and where the argument asked you to take a step it hadn't earned. Don't copy-edit — I have a pass for that. I want the reader's report, not the proofreader's.
